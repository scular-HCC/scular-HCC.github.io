ConceptPointwise Product · DFT 8
DFT 8

Pointwise in time ⇔
$\tfrac{1}{N}$ circular convolution in frequency.

$$x \odot y \,\leftrightarrow\, \tfrac{1}{N}\, X \circledast Y$$

The pointwise product is $(x \odot y)[n] = x[n]\, y[n]$.

So multiplying two signals in time mixes their spectra — circularly.

ConceptENES-222 · L16
ConceptCircular Convolution
Definition

Reverse, shift, dot.

$$(a \circledast b)[n] \,=\, a^{\top}\,P^{n}\,R\,b$$
$$=\, \sum_{m=0}^{N-1} a[m]\, b[(n-m) \bmod N]$$

Commutative: $a \circledast b = b \circledast a$. Inherited from pointwise multiplication in the other domain.

Compute all $N$ outputs in one matrix-vector product: build the circulant matrix whose rows are $P^{n} R\, b$, multiply by $a$.

ConceptENES-222 · L16
Worked ExampleCircular Convolution · $N=4$
Worked example

One matrix–vector product
computes all four outputs.

$$a = (\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta)^{\top}$$ $$b = (1, 2, 3, -2)^{\top}$$
Circulant of $b$
$$a \circledast b \,=\, \begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & 3 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 & -2 & 3 \\ 3 & 2 & 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 3 & 2 & 1\end{bmatrix}\!a$$
Read off the entries
$$(a \circledast b)[0] = \alpha - 2\beta + 3\gamma + 2\delta$$ $$(a \circledast b)[1] = 2\alpha + \beta - 2\gamma + 3\delta$$

Each row is the previous one circularly shifted by one position.

ConceptDFT 9 — Time Convolution
DFT 9 · Dual of DFT 8

Circular convolution in time ⇔
pointwise multiplication in frequency.

$$x \circledast y \,\leftrightarrow\, X \odot Y$$

The workhorse identity of digital filtering. Convolve in time → multiply DFTs in frequency → inverse-DFT to recover the result.

Derived from DFT 8 + duality DFT 7.

X = fft(x); Y = fft(y); s = ifft(X.*Y);

ConceptENES-222 · L16
Worked ExamplePolynomial Multiplication
Why convolution multiplies polynomials

$a[n]\, t^{n}$ encodes a polynomial.
$\circledast$ then multiplies them.

If $a = (1, -2, 3, 0, 0, 0)^{\top}$ and $b = (2, 1, -1, 2, 0, 0)^{\top}$, zero-padded to length 6, then $a \circledast b$ holds the coefficients of

$$(1 - 2t + 3t^{2})(2 + t - t^{2} + 2t^{3}).$$
Result
$$a \circledast b = (2,\, -3,\, 3,\, 7,\, -7,\, 6)^{\top}$$

i.e.

$$(1-2t+3t^{2})(2+t-t^{2}+2t^{3}) = 2 - 3t + 3t^{2} + 7t^{3} - 7t^{4} + 6t^{5}.$$

Zero-padding makes the circular convolution behave like ordinary (non-circular) convolution — preview of L23.

ConceptPeriodic Extension by $M$ Copies
Periodic extension

$M$ copies of $s$ in time ⇒
$S$ with $M-1$ zeros inserted between bins.

$$x = \underbrace{[s; s; \ldots; s]}_{M \text{ copies}}$$
$$X = M\,[S[0], 0_{M-1}, S[1], 0_{M-1}, \ldots, S[L-1], 0_{M-1}]^{\top}$$

When $N = ML$, the $kM$-th Fourier sinusoid of length $N$ is just the $k$-th Fourier sinusoid of length $L$, periodically repeated. So only those bins fire.

Scaling factor $M$ because there are $M$ overlapping contributions.

PracticeENES-222 · L16
SolutionSparse Length-12 Spectrum
Solution

Four-times higher peaks,
three of them, evenly spaced.

$x$
$$x = (a,b,c,a,b,c,a,b,c,a,b,c)^{\top}$$
$X$
$$X = (4A,\, 0,\, 0,\, 0,\, 4B,\, 0,\, 0,\, 0,\, 4C,\, 0,\, 0,\, 0)^{\top}$$
Reading the spectrum

Only the bins $0, M, 2M = 0, 4, 8$ are populated — exactly the bins whose length-12 frequency $\omega_k = k\,2\pi/12$ coincides with a length-3 Fourier frequency.

Each peak is scaled by $M = 4$.
