ConceptCircular Convolution
Definition · vectors of length $N$

Reverse, shift, dot.

$$(a \circledast b)[n] \,=\, a^{\top}\,P^{n}\,R\,b$$
$$=\, \sum_{m=0}^{N-1} a[m]\, b[(n-m) \bmod N]$$

All $N$ outputs come from one matrix-vector product: build the circulant matrix whose row $n$ is $(P^{n}Rb)^{\top}$ and multiply by $a$.

Why the two forms agree
$(Rb)[m]=b[(-m)\bmod N]$, and $P^{n}$ shifts down by $n$, so $(P^{n}Rb)[m]=(Rb)[m-n]=b[(n-m)\bmod N]$. Taking the dot product with $a$ gives the sum.
Recall (L14): R and P
$R$ reverses a vector, $(Rb)[m]=b[(-m)\bmod N]$. $P$ shifts a vector down one place and moves the last entry to the top.
Commutative
Substitute $m'=n-m$ in the sum: $\sum_m a[m]b[n-m]=\sum_{m'}a[n-m']\,b[m']=(b\circledast a)[n]$.

Worked example · build the circulant

Each row is the previous one, shifted.

$$a = (\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta)^{\top},\qquad b = (1, 2, 3, -2)^{\top}$$

Row $n$ of the matrix is $(P^{n}Rb)^{\top}$, so start from $Rb$.

Step 1 · reverse b
$$Rb=(b[0],b[3],b[2],b[1])^{\top}=(1,\,-2,\,3,\,2)^{\top}$$
Step 2 · shift down, row by row
$$\begin{aligned}Rb&=(1,-2,3,2)\\PRb&=(2,1,-2,3)\\P^{2}Rb&=(3,2,1,-2)\\P^{3}Rb&=(-2,3,2,1)\end{aligned}$$ Each shift moves the last entry to the top.
Step 3 · stack the rows
$$a \circledast b \,=\, \begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & 3 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 & -2 & 3 \\ 3 & 2 & 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 3 & 2 & 1\end{bmatrix}\!a$$

ConceptPointwise Product · DFT 8
DFT 8 · uses the $\circledast$ just defined

Product in time ⇔ convolution in frequency.

$$x \odot y \,\leftrightarrow\, \tfrac{1}{N}\, X \circledast Y$$

$(x \odot y)[n] = x[n]\, y[n]$. Multiplying two signals in time mixes their spectra, circularly.

Start
$$Z[k]=\sum_{n}x[n]\,y[n]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$$
Insert the synthesis equation for x
$x[n]=\tfrac1N\sum_{m}X[m]e^{j2\pi mn/N}$, so $$Z[k]=\tfrac1N\sum_{m}X[m]\sum_{n}y[n]\,e^{-j2\pi(k-m)n/N}$$
Recognise the inner sum
The inner sum is $Y[(k-m)\bmod N]$, hence $$Z[k]=\tfrac1N\sum_m X[m]\,Y[(k-m)\bmod N]$$ $$=\tfrac1N(X\circledast Y)[k]$$

Worked ExampleDFT 8 · $N=4$ by Hand
Worked example · DFT 8 with numbers

DFT 8 with numbers.

Given
$$x=(1,2,3,4)^{\top},\quad y=(1,0,-1,0)^{\top}$$
Step 1 · pointwise product
$z[n]=x[n]\,y[n]$: $$\begin{aligned}z&=(1{\cdot}1,\,2{\cdot}0,\,3{\cdot}(-1),\,4{\cdot}0)\\&=(1,\,0,\,-3,\,0)^{\top}\end{aligned}$$
Step 2 · its DFT, straight from the definition
Only $n=0,2$ contribute, and $(-j)^{2k}=(-1)^k$: $$Z[k]=1-3(-1)^{k}\;\Rightarrow\;Z=(-2,\,4,\,-2,\,4)^{\top}$$
Step 3 · the two DFTs
$$\begin{aligned}X&=(10,\;-2+2j,\;-2,\;-2-2j)\\Y&=(0,\;2,\;0,\;2)\end{aligned}$$ $Y[k]=1-(-1)^{k}$; $X$ is worked out again in the DFT 9 example.
Step 4 · convolve X with Y and divide by N
For each $k$ add the four products $X[m]\,Y[(k-m)\bmod 4]$: $$\begin{aligned}k=0&:\;\tfrac14\big[0+(-4{+}4j)+0+(-4{-}4j)\big]=-2\\k=1&:\;\tfrac14\big[20+0+(-4)+0\big]=4\\k=2&:\;\tfrac14\big[0+(-4{+}4j)+0+(-4{-}4j)\big]=-2\\k=3&:\;\tfrac14\big[20+0+(-4)+0\big]=4\end{aligned}$$ Same as $Z$ ✓. Without the $\tfrac1N$ we would get $(-8,16,-8,16)$.

ConceptDFT 9 — Time Convolution
DFT 9 · Dual of DFT 8

Circular convolution in time ⇔ product in frequency.

$$x \circledast y \,\leftrightarrow\, X \odot Y$$

The workhorse of digital filtering: convolve in time, multiply DFTs, inverse-DFT.

X = fft(x); Y = fft(y); s = ifft(X.*Y);

Transform the convolution
$$Z[k]=\sum_n\sum_m x[m]\,y[(n-m)\bmod N]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$$
Substitute r = n − m, factor
$$Z[k]=\sum_m x[m]e^{-j2\pi km/N}\sum_r y[r]e^{-j2\pi kr/N}$$ $$=X[k]\,Y[k]$$

$x=(1,2,3,4)$ and $h=(1,0,-1,0)$. Plan: $y=x\circledast h$ via $Y=X\odot H$ (DFT 9), then invert.

Step 3 · multiply bin by bin
$$Y=X\odot H=(0,\;2(-2+2j),\;0,\;2(-2-2j))^{\top}$$ $$=(0,\,-4+4j,\,0,\,-4-4j)^{\top}$$

Worked ExampleApplication · a Two-Point Average
Application · filtering by multiplying spectra

A two-point average.

Given
$$x=(1,2,3,4)^{\top},\quad h=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,0,0)^{\top}$$

$y=x\circledast h$ averages each sample with the one before it, wrapping around.

Step 1 · in time
$y[n]=\tfrac12\big(x[n]+x[(n-1)\bmod 4]\big)$: $$\begin{aligned}y[0]&=\tfrac{1+4}{2}=2.5,\quad y[1]=\tfrac{2+1}{2}=1.5\\y[2]&=\tfrac{3+2}{2}=2.5,\quad y[3]=\tfrac{4+3}{2}=3.5\end{aligned}$$

$|H|=(1,\,0.707,\,0,\,0.707)$: bin 2 is removed, so this is a low-pass filter.

Step 2 · frequency response of h
$H[k]=\tfrac12\big(1+(-j)^{k}\big)$: $$H=\big(1,\;\tfrac{1-j}{2},\;0,\;\tfrac{1+j}{2}\big)$$
Step 3 · multiply the spectra
With $X=(10,\,-2{+}2j,\,-2,\,-2{-}2j)$: $$\begin{aligned}Y[1]&=\tfrac{(-2+2j)(1-j)}{2}=\tfrac{4j}{2}=2j\\Y[3]&=\tfrac{(-2-2j)(1+j)}{2}=\tfrac{-4j}{2}=-2j\end{aligned}$$ $Y=(10,\;2j,\;0,\;-2j)$
Step 4 · back to time
$y[0]=\tfrac14(10+2j-2j)=2.5$ and $y[1]=\tfrac14\big(10+2j\cdot j+(-2j)(-j)\big)=\tfrac14(10-2-2)=1.5$ ✓ (and $y[2]=2.5$, $y[3]=3.5$).

Worked ExamplePolynomial Multiplication
Why convolution multiplies polynomials

Coefficients convolve.

Let $p=(1,-2,3)$ and $q=(2,1,-1,2)$ be the coefficients of $1-2t+3t^{2}$ and $2+t-t^{2}+2t^{3}$. Zero-pad both to length $3+4-1=6$ (call the padded vectors $\tilde p,\tilde q$), so no wrap-around occurs.

The coefficient of $t^{n}$ in the product is $c_n=\sum_m p_m\,q_{n-m}$ (with $q_k=0$ outside $0..3$).

Check at t = 1
$(1-2+3)(2+1-1+2)=2\cdot4=8$ and $2-3+3+7-7+6=8$ ✓. The notebook agrees: np.convolve and the padded ifft(fft*fft) return this vector.
Coefficient by coefficient
$$\begin{aligned}c_0&=1\cdot2=2\\c_1&=1\cdot1+(-2)\cdot2=-3\\c_2&=1(-1)+(-2)(1)+3(2)=3\\c_3&=1\cdot2+(-2)(-1)+3(1)=7\\c_4&=(-2)(2)+3(-1)=-7\\c_5&=3\cdot2=6\end{aligned}$$
Result
$$\tilde p\circledast\tilde q=(2,-3,3,7,-7,6)^{\top}$$ $$(1-2t+3t^{2})(2+t-t^{2}+2t^{3})$$ $$=2-3t+3t^{2}+7t^{3}-7t^{4}+6t^{5}$$

Solved ExampleSolved Example B · Padding
Solved Example B · linear vs circular

Padding removes wrap-around.

$\mathbf{b}=(1,2)$, $\mathbf{s}=(3,-1)$.

Step 1 · linear convolution
$$\begin{aligned}c[0]&=1\cdot3=3\\c[1]&=1(-1)+2\cdot3=5\\c[2]&=2(-1)=-2\end{aligned}$$ so $\mathbf{b}*\mathbf{s}=(3,5,-2)^\top$.
Padding rule

Linear convolution of lengths $K$ and $L$ equals circular convolution once both are zero-padded to $N\geq K+L-1$.

Step 2 · pad to N = 3, convolve circularly
$b=(1,2,0)$, $s=(3,-1,0)$, $(b\circledast s)[n]=\sum_m b[m]\,s[(n-m)\bmod3]$: $$\begin{aligned}n=0&:\;1\cdot3+2\cdot0+0\cdot(-1)=3\\n=1&:\;1(-1)+2\cdot3+0\cdot0=5\\n=2&:\;1\cdot0+2(-1)+0\cdot3=-2\end{aligned}$$ so $(3,5,-2)^\top$ ✓
Why N = 3 is needed
With only $N=2$, the entry $-2$ wraps onto index 0: $(3+(-2),\,5)=(1,5)$.

ConceptPeriodic Extension by $M$ Copies
Periodic extension

$M$ copies of $s$ in time ⇒
$S$ with $M-1$ zeros inserted between bins.

$$x = \underbrace{[s; s; \ldots; s]}_{M \text{ copies}}$$
$$X = M\,[S[0], 0_{M-1}, S[1], 0_{M-1}, \ldots, S[L-1], 0_{M-1}]^{\top}$$

When $N = ML$, the $kM$-th Fourier sinusoid of length $N$ is just the $k$-th Fourier sinusoid of length $L$, periodically repeated. So only those bins fire.

Scaling factor $M$: each of the $M$ copies contributes the same amount, so the $M$ equal contributions add (derived on the next slide). Hands-on check two slides later.

ConceptENES-222 · L16
ConceptWhy only every M-th bin fires
Derivation

Split the sum into $M$ blocks.

Let $N=ML$ and $x[n+rL]=s[n]$ for $n=0,\dots,L-1$ and $r=0,\dots,M-1$.

Step 1 · block decomposition
$$X[k]=\sum_{r=0}^{M-1}\sum_{n=0}^{L-1}s[n]\,e^{-j2\pi k(n+rL)/(ML)}$$ $$=\Big[\sum_{n}s[n]e^{-j2\pi kn/N}\Big]\sum_{r=0}^{M-1}e^{-j2\pi kr/M}$$
Step 2 · geometric sum over r
Ratio $\rho=e^{-j2\pi k/M}$, $\rho^{M}=1$. If $M\nmid k$ then $\rho\neq1$ and the sum is $\tfrac{1-\rho^{M}}{1-\rho}=0$. If $k=iM$ then $\rho=1$ and the sum is $M$.
Step 3 · surviving bins
For $k=iM$: $e^{-j2\pi iMn/(ML)}=e^{-j2\pi in/L}$, so $X[iM]=M\,S[i]$.

Common MistakesFour Ways to Lose Marks
Common mistakes

Check these before you hand in.

1 · Forgetting the 1/N in DFT 8

$x\odot y\leftrightarrow\tfrac1N X\circledast Y$. Without it the example gives $(-8,16,-8,16)$ instead of $(-2,4,-2,4)$. DFT 9 has no factor.

2 · Linear instead of modular indices

$(a\circledast b)[0]$ needs $b[(0-1)\bmod 4]=b[3]$, not $b[-1]$. Every index $n-m$ is reduced mod $N$.

3 · Circular where you needed linear

$(1,2)$ with $(3,-1)$ at $N=2$ gives $(1,5)$: the $-2$ wrapped onto index 0. Pad to $N\ge K+L-1=3$ first.

4 · Reading M copies wrongly

$M$ copies of $s$ put $M\,S[k]$ at bins $0,M,2M,\dots$ and zero elsewhere. They do not put $S$ at bins $0,1,\dots,L-1$.

Self-check for any convolution answer: the outputs must add up to (sum of $a$)(sum of $b$).
