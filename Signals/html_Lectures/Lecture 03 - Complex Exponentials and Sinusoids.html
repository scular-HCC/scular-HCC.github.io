ConceptEuler's Formula
Euler's formula

$e^{j\theta}$ is just polar form
written differently.

$$e^{j\theta} \,=\, \cos\theta + j\sin\theta$$
$$z \,=\, r e^{j\theta}$$

Substitute $t = j\theta$ into the Taylor series for $e^{t}$, separate real and imaginary terms, and recognise the series for $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$.

This is the single most useful identity in the course.

ConceptENES-222 · L03
ConceptWhy Euler Holds
Derivation

Euler's formula from the Taylor series.

Start: the exponential series
$$e^{t} = 1 + t + \frac{t^{2}}{2!} + \frac{t^{3}}{3!} + \frac{t^{4}}{4!} + \frac{t^{5}}{5!} + \cdots$$
Powers of the imaginary unit
$$j^{2}=-1,\;\; j^{3}=j^{2}\cdot j=-j,\;\; j^{4}=(j^{2})^{2}=1,\;\; j^{5}=j$$

The pattern $j,-1,-j,1$ then repeats.

Notation: factorial
$n!$ ("$n$ factorial") is the product $n(n-1)\cdots2\cdot1$. Example: $3!=3\cdot2\cdot1=6$, $5!=120$.
Substitute and replace each power
$$e^{j\theta} = 1 + j\theta - \frac{\theta^{2}}{2!} - j\frac{\theta^{3}}{3!} + \frac{\theta^{4}}{4!} + j\frac{\theta^{5}}{5!} - \cdots$$
Group real and imaginary terms
$$e^{j\theta} = \underbrace{\Big(1 - \tfrac{\theta^{2}}{2!} + \tfrac{\theta^{4}}{4!} - \cdots\Big)}_{\cos\theta} + j\,\underbrace{\Big(\theta - \tfrac{\theta^{3}}{3!} + \tfrac{\theta^{5}}{5!} - \cdots\Big)}_{\sin\theta}$$
ConceptENES-222 · L03
ConceptWhy Euler Wins
Multiplication, division, conjugate

Polar rules become plain exponent arithmetic.

Product
$$\begin{aligned} r_1 e^{j\theta_1} \cdot r_2 e^{j\theta_2} &= r_1 r_2\, e^{j\theta_1} e^{j\theta_2} \\ &= r_1 r_2\, e^{j(\theta_1+\theta_2)} \end{aligned}$$

Regroup factors, then use $e^{a}e^{b}=e^{a+b}$ with $a=j\theta_1$, $b=j\theta_2$.

Quotient
$$\frac{r_1 e^{j\theta_1}}{r_2 e^{j\theta_2}} = \frac{r_1}{r_2}\, e^{j\theta_1} e^{-j\theta_2} = \frac{r_1}{r_2}\, e^{j(\theta_1-\theta_2)}$$

$e^{-b}=1/e^{b}$ because $e^{b}e^{-b}=e^{0}=1$.

Quick question: the conjugate

For $z=re^{j\theta}$, conjugating flips the sign of every $j$: $\;z^{*}=r\,(e^{j\theta})^{*}=r(\cos\theta - j\sin\theta)=r\,e^{-j\theta}$, since $e^{-j\theta}=\cos(-\theta)+j\sin(-\theta)=\cos\theta-j\sin\theta$. Same modulus, opposite angle.

ConceptInverse Euler
Cosine and sine, rebuilt

Add and subtract $e^{j\theta}$ with $e^{-j\theta}$.

Two Euler equations
$$e^{j\theta} = \cos\theta + j\sin\theta$$$$e^{-j\theta} = \cos\theta - j\sin\theta$$

The second is Euler with $\theta\to-\theta$, using $\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta$ and $\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta$.

Add: the sine terms cancel
$$e^{j\theta}+e^{-j\theta}=2\cos\theta\;\Rightarrow\;\cos\theta=\frac{e^{j\theta}+e^{-j\theta}}{2}$$
Subtract: the cosine terms cancel
$$e^{j\theta}-e^{-j\theta}=2j\sin\theta\;\Rightarrow\;\sin\theta=\frac{e^{j\theta}-e^{-j\theta}}{2j}$$
$$\cos\theta \,=\, \frac{e^{j\theta} + e^{-j\theta}}{2}$$
$$\sin\theta \,=\, \frac{e^{j\theta} - e^{-j\theta}}{2j}$$

These reappear constantly in phasor and Fourier analysis.

Conceptn-th Roots
Roots of a complex number

$n$ equally spaced points on a circle of radius $|v|^{1/n}$.

Write both sides in polar form
$$z=\rho\, e^{j\alpha},\qquad v=|v|\,e^{j\angle v}$$

$\rho=|z|\ge 0$ and $\alpha=\angle z$ are the unknowns.

Raise to the n-th power
$$z^{n}=\rho^{n}\,e^{jn\alpha}=|v|\,e^{j\angle v}$$

Multiplying $n$ copies adds the angle $n$ times.

Match moduli, then angles
$$\rho^{n}=|v|\;\Rightarrow\;\rho=|v|^{1/n}$$$$n\alpha=\angle v+2k\pi\;\Rightarrow\;\alpha=\frac{\angle v+2k\pi}{n}$$

Angles that differ by $2k\pi$ give the same number because $e^{j2k\pi}=1$.

$$z_k \,=\, |v|^{1/n}\, e^{j\,\theta_k}$$
$$\theta_k \,=\, \frac{\angle v + 2k\pi}{n}$$

$k = 0,1,\ldots, n-1$

All $n$ roots share the modulus $|v|^{1/n}$. Consecutive angles differ by $2\pi/n$.

Why only $k=0,\ldots,n-1$? Taking $k+n$ adds $2\pi$ to $\theta_k$, which returns to the same point.

Worked ExampleFour Roots of j
Worked example

Find the four roots of $z^{4}=j$.

Set up
$$|j|=\sqrt{0^{2}+1^{2}}=1,\qquad \angle j=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$$$$\rho=1^{1/4}=1\quad(\text{unit circle})$$
Angles for n = 4
$$\theta_k=\frac{\pi/2+2k\pi}{4}=\frac{\pi}{8}+k\frac{\pi}{2}$$$$\begin{aligned}\theta_0&=\tfrac{\pi}{8}, & \theta_1&=\tfrac{\pi}{8}+\tfrac{4\pi}{8}=\tfrac{5\pi}{8},\\ \theta_2&=\tfrac{\pi}{8}+\tfrac{8\pi}{8}=\tfrac{9\pi}{8}, & \theta_3&=\tfrac{\pi}{8}+\tfrac{12\pi}{8}=\tfrac{13\pi}{8}\end{aligned}$$
Check one root
$$\left(e^{j\pi/8}\right)^{4}=e^{j4\pi/8}=e^{j\pi/2}=j\;\checkmark$$
Four dots: the roots of $z^{4}=j$ on the unit circle. The first is at $\pi/8$; each next is $\pi/2$ further. Square: $j$.

ConceptPeriodicity & Symmetry
Cosine and sine

Periodic with $2\pi$. Even and odd. Quarter-shifted.

Periodicity
$$\cos(\theta + 2\pi) = \cos\theta,\;\; \sin(\theta + 2\pi) = \sin\theta$$

Adding $2\pi$ is one full turn: $e^{j(\theta+2\pi)}=e^{j\theta}e^{j2\pi}=e^{j\theta}$.

Symmetry
$$\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta\;\; (\text{even})$$$$\sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta\;\; (\text{odd})$$

Reflecting $\theta\to-\theta$ flips the point to its conjugate: $\cos\theta - j\sin\theta$.

Quarter-shift
$$\sin\theta = \cos(\theta - \tfrac{\pi}{2})$$

$\cos(a-b)=\cos a\cos b+\sin a\sin b$ with $a=\theta$, $b=\tfrac\pi2$: $\;\cos\theta\cdot0+\sin\theta\cdot1$.

Half-shift
$$\cos(\theta + \pi) = -\cos\theta$$

$\cos(a+b)=\cos a\cos b-\sin a\sin b$ with $b=\pi$: $\;\cos\theta(-1)-\sin\theta\cdot 0$. Likewise $\sin(\theta+\pi)=-\sin\theta$.

ConceptShifted Copies
Look at the graphs

$\sin\theta$ is $\cos\theta$ delayed by $\pi/2$.

Both curves repeat every $2\pi$ (two full cycles over $0$ to $4\pi$). $\cos\theta$ starts at its peak, $\cos 0=1$; $\sin\theta$ is the same curve shifted right by $\pi/2$, so $\sin 0=0$. Cosine is even about $\theta=0$, sine is odd.

ConceptContinuous-Time Sinusoid
The generic CT sinusoid

Three numbers describe it.

$$x(t) \,=\, A\cos(\omega t + \phi)$$
Amplitude
$A > 0$
Angular frequency
$\omega$ in rad/sec
Initial phase
$\phi$ in rad, the phase at $t=0$
Time
$t$ in sec
Period
$$T \,=\, \frac{2\pi}{\omega} \text{ seconds}$$

$x(t+T)=A\cos(\omega t+\phi+\omega T)$ repeats when the argument grows by one cycle: $\omega T=2\pi$.

Cyclic frequency
$$f \,=\, \frac{1}{T} \,=\, \frac{\omega}{2\pi} \text{ Hz}$$

Cycles per second: one cycle takes $T$ seconds, so there are $1/T$ cycles each second.

Worked ExampleRecover the Amplitude
Worked example

Three clues, three unknowns: first $A$.

  • Clue 1. $x(t) \geq 1.50$ for $23\%$ of its period.
  • Clue 2. It takes $0.02$ s to drop from $1.50$ to $0$.
  • Clue 3. $x(0) = -0.3$, with $x'(0) > 0$ ($x'$ = slope).

Unknowns: amplitude $A$, angular frequency $\omega$, phase $\phi$. Let $\theta=\omega t+\phi$, so $x=A\cos\theta$.

Where the signal is high
$x\ge1.5\iff\cos\theta\ge c$ with $c=1.5/A$. Per period, cosine is even and decreasing on $[0,\pi]$: $$\cos\theta\ge c\iff |\theta|\le\cos^{-1}(c)$$

Notation. $\cos^{-1}(c)$ is the inverse cosine (arccos): the angle in $[0,\pi]$ whose cosine is $c$, not $1/\cos c$. E.g. $\cos^{-1}(0.75)=0.7227=0.23\pi$.

Fraction of the period
The interval has width $2\cos^{-1}(c)$ out of $2\pi$: $$\frac{2\cos^{-1}(c)}{2\pi}=\frac{\cos^{-1}(c)}{\pi}=0.23$$$$\cos^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{1.5}{A}\right)=0.23\pi$$
Solve for the amplitude
Take the cosine of both sides; $\cos(0.23\pi)=0.7501$: $$A=\frac{1.5}{\cos(0.23\pi)}=\frac{1.5}{0.7501}=1.9997\approx 2.0$$

Worked ExampleRecover the Phase
Worked example

Last: the phase $\phi$.

Use the starting value
$$x(0)=2\cos(\omega\cdot0+\phi)=2\cos\phi=-0.3$$$$\Rightarrow\;\cos\phi=-0.15$$$$\phi=\pm\cos^{-1}(-0.15)=\pm1.721\text{ rad}$$

Two candidates, because cosine is even.

Use the slope
Chain rule: $x'(t)=-A\omega\sin(\omega t+\phi)$, so $$x'(0)=-A\omega\sin\phi>0\;\Rightarrow\;\sin\phi<0$$

because $A\omega>0$.

Pick the branch
$\cos^{-1}$ returns $+1.721\in[0,\pi]$, where $\sin>0$. We need $\sin\phi<0$, so take the negative one: $$\phi=-\cos^{-1}(-0.15)=-1.721\text{ rad}$$
Check
$\sin\phi=-\sqrt{1-0.15^{2}}=-0.9887<0$, and $x'(0)=2(42.41)(0.9887)=83.9>0\;\checkmark$
$$x(t)=2\cos(13.5\pi\, t-1.721)$$

Convert, then multiply and divide.

$z_1=-1+j$ and $z_2=1+j\sqrt{3}$. Find the exponential forms, $z_1z_2$ and $z_1/z_2$.
