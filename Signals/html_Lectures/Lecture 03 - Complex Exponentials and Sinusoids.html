ConceptEuler's Formula
Euler's formula

$e^{j\theta}$ is just polar form
written differently.

$$e^{j\theta} \,=\, \cos\theta + j\sin\theta$$
$$z \,=\, r e^{j\theta}$$

Substitute $t = j\theta$ into the Taylor series for $e^{t}$, separate real and imaginary terms, and recognise the series for $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$.

This is the single most useful identity in the course.

ConceptENES-222 · L03
ConceptWhy $e^{j\theta}$ Wins
Multiplication and division

Polar rules become
plain exponent arithmetic.

$$r_1 e^{j\theta_1} \cdot r_2 e^{j\theta_2} \,=\, r_1 r_2\, e^{j(\theta_1+\theta_2)}$$
$$\frac{r_1 e^{j\theta_1}}{r_2 e^{j\theta_2}} \,=\, \frac{r_1}{r_2}\, e^{j(\theta_1-\theta_2)}$$

The identity $e^{a+b} = e^{a} e^{b}$ does all the work — no angle-sum formulas needed.

Quick question

If $z = r e^{j\theta}$, what is $z^{*}$ in exponential form?

$z^{*} = r\,e^{-j\theta}$ — same modulus, opposite angle.

Worked example

Write $z_1$ and $z_2$ as $re^{j\theta}$.

$z_1 = -1 + j$
$$|z_1| = \sqrt{2},\;\; \angle z_1 = \tfrac{3\pi}{4}$$ $$z_1 \,=\, \sqrt{2}\, e^{j\,3\pi/4}$$
$z_2 = 1 + j\sqrt{3}$
$$|z_2| = 2,\;\; \angle z_2 = \tfrac{\pi}{3}$$ $$z_2 \,=\, 2\, e^{j\,\pi/3}$$

ConceptInverse Euler
Cosine and sine, rebuilt

Add and subtract
$e^{j\theta}$ with $e^{-j\theta}$.

$$\cos\theta \,=\, \frac{e^{j\theta} + e^{-j\theta}}{2}$$
$$\sin\theta \,=\, \frac{e^{j\theta} - e^{-j\theta}}{2j}$$

From $e^{j\theta} = \cos\theta + j\sin\theta$ and $e^{-j\theta} = \cos\theta - j\sin\theta$.

Add to isolate $\cos\theta$. Subtract to isolate $\sin\theta$.

These will reappear constantly in phasor and Fourier analysis.

Concept$n$-th Roots
Roots of $z^{n} = v$

$n$ equally spaced points
on a circle of radius $|v|^{1/n}$.

$$z_k \,=\, |v|^{1/n}\, e^{j\,\theta_k}$$
$$\theta_k \,=\, \frac{\angle v + 2k\pi}{n},\;\; k = 0,1,\ldots, n-1$$

All $n$ roots share the same modulus $|v|^{1/n}$.

Their angles are equally spaced by $2\pi/n$ around the circle.

Example

The four roots of $z^{4} = j$:

$$z_k \,=\, e^{j\pi/8},\;\; e^{j\,5\pi/8},\;\; e^{j\,9\pi/8},\;\; e^{j\,13\pi/8}$$

ConceptPeriodicity & Symmetry
$\cos$ and $\sin$

Periodic with $2\pi$.
Even and odd. Quarter-shifted.

Periodicity
$$\cos(\theta + 2\pi) = \cos\theta,\;\; \sin(\theta + 2\pi) = \sin\theta$$
Symmetry
$$\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta\;\; (\text{even})$$ $$\sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta\;\; (\text{odd})$$
Quarter-shift
$$\sin\theta = \cos(\theta - \tfrac{\pi}{2})$$
Half-shift
$$\cos(\theta + \pi) = -\cos\theta$$

ConceptContinuous-Time Sinusoid
The generic CT sinusoid

Three numbers describe it.

$$x(t) \,=\, A\cos(\omega t + \phi)$$
$A > 0$
amplitude
$\omega$
angular frequency (rad/sec)
$\phi$
initial phase (rad)
Period
$$T \,=\, \frac{2\pi}{\omega} \text{ seconds}$$
Cyclic frequency
$$f \,=\, \frac{1}{T} \,=\, \frac{\omega}{2\pi} \text{ Hz}$$
ConceptENES-222 · L03
Worked ExampleRecover $A$, $\omega$, $\phi$
Worked example

Three clues, three unknowns.

  • $x(t) \geq 1.50$ for $23\%$ of its period.
  • It takes $0.02$ s to drop from $1.50$ to $0$.
  • $x(0) = -0.3$, with $x'(0) > 0$.
Amplitude
$$\cos^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{1.5}{A}\right) = 0.23\pi \;\Rightarrow\; A = \tfrac{1.5}{\cos(0.23\pi)} = 2.0$$
Angular frequency
$$\omega = \frac{0.5\pi - 0.23\pi}{0.02} = 13.5\pi \text{ rad/s}$$ $$f = 6.75 \text{ Hz},\;\; T = 0.148 \text{ s}$$
Phase
$$\phi = -\cos^{-1}\!\left(\tfrac{-0.3}{2.0}\right) = -1.721 \text{ rad}$$

Negative branch chosen because $x'(0) > 0$ forces $\sin\phi < 0$.

Solved Example A · §1.3.4

Write $z_1=-1+j$ and $z_2=1+j\sqrt{3}$
in exponential form.

Then compute $z_1z_2$ and $z_1/z_2$.

Step 1 · Convert $z_1$
$|z_1|=\sqrt{2}$, $\angle z_1=\arctan(-1)+\pi=3\pi/4$ $$z_1=\sqrt{2}\,e^{j3\pi/4}$$
Step 2 · Convert $z_2$
$|z_2|=2$, $\angle z_2=\arctan(\sqrt{3})=\pi/3$ $$z_2=2e^{j\pi/3}$$
Step 3 · Product and quotient
$$z_1z_2=2\sqrt{2}\,e^{j(3\pi/4+\pi/3)}=2\sqrt{2}\,e^{j13\pi/12}$$ $$z_1/z_2=\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,e^{j(3\pi/4-\pi/3)}=\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,e^{j5\pi/12}$$

Solved Example B · §1.3.4

Find all cube roots
of $v=-8$.

$v=8e^{j\pi}$. Three roots spaced $2\pi/3$ apart on circle $|z|=2$.

Step 1 · Modulus
$$|z|=8^{1/3}=2$$
Step 2 · Angles at $k=0,1,2$
$k{=}0:\;\theta=\pi/3\Rightarrow z_0=2e^{j\pi/3}=1+j\sqrt{3}$ $k{=}1:\;\theta=\pi\Rightarrow z_1=2e^{j\pi}=-2$ $k{=}2:\;\theta=5\pi/3\Rightarrow z_2=2e^{j5\pi/3}=1-j\sqrt{3}$
Sanity check

$z_0^3=8e^{j\pi}=-8\;\checkmark$. Roots equally spaced at $120°$ on $|z|=2$.

One identity, three consequences.

$e^{j\theta} = \cos\theta + j\sin\theta$

$n$ roots of $z^{n} = v$.
