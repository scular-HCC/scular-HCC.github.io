Periodic Inputs
& Cascaded Filters
Periodic in, periodic out. The DFT of the output is the DFT of the input multiplied by samples of the frequency response. Cascade ⇒ multiply system functions.
Reading · Periodic inputs and cascaded filters
Lecture 22Objectives
By the end of this lecture you will

Predict the steady-state output
of an FIR filter.

  1. Apply (amplitude $A$, frequency $\Omega_0$, phase $\phi$) $A\cos(\Omega_0 n + \phi) \to |H(e^{j\Omega_0})|\,A\cos(\Omega_0 n + \phi + \angle H(e^{j\Omega_0}))$.
  2. Extend to oscillating exponentials $r^{n}\cos(\Omega_0 n + \phi)$ via $z = r e^{j\Omega_0}$.
  3. Filter a periodic input with the DFT: $Y[k] = H(e^{j k\,2\pi/L})\, X[k]$.
  4. Cascade two FIR filters: $H(z) = H_1(z)\, H_2(z)$.

ConceptReal Sinusoidal Inputs
Real sinusoid in

Same frequency out — scaled and phase-shifted.

$$x[n] = \cos(\Omega_0 n + \phi) \;\Rightarrow\; y[n] = |H(e^{j\Omega_0})|\,\cos\!\big(\Omega_0 n + \phi + \angle H(e^{j\Omega_0})\big)$$
1 · Split into exponentials
$$x[n]=\tfrac12 e^{j\phi}e^{j\Omega_0 n}+\tfrac12 e^{-j\phi}e^{-j\Omega_0 n}$$

Euler: $\cos\theta=\tfrac12(e^{j\theta}+e^{-j\theta})$, $\theta=\Omega_0 n+\phi$.

2 · Eigen-property on each
$$y[n]=\tfrac12 H(e^{j\Omega_0})e^{j(\Omega_0 n+\phi)}+\tfrac12 H(e^{-j\Omega_0})e^{-j(\Omega_0 n+\phi)}$$

Real $b$: $H(e^{-j\Omega_0})=H^{*}(e^{j\Omega_0})$ (L21).

3 · Combine

With $w=H(e^{j\Omega_0})e^{j(\Omega_0 n+\phi)}=|H|\,e^{j(\Omega_0 n+\phi+\angle H)}$: $\;y[n]=\tfrac12(w+w^{*})=\Re\{w\}=|H|\cos(\Omega_0 n+\phi+\angle H)$. Frequency is preserved — that is why FIR filters are frequency-selective.

ConceptOscillating Exponentials
Damped sinusoid

$r^{n}\cos(\Omega_0 n + \phi)$ — same shape,
evaluated at $z = r e^{j\Omega_0}$.

$$y[n] \,=\, |H(r e^{j\Omega_0})|\, r^{n}\cos\!\big(\Omega_0 n + \phi + \angle H(r e^{j\Omega_0})\big)$$

Why: $z^{n}=r^{n}e^{j\Omega_0 n}$, so $x[n]=\Re\{e^{j\phi}z^{n}\}$. Real $b$ lets $\Re$ pass through the filter: $y[n]=\Re\{e^{j\phi}H(z)z^{n}\}$. Writing $H(z)=|H(z)|e^{j\angle H(z)}$ gives the formula.

Reduces to the previous slide when $r = 1$. For $r < 1$ the input decays; for $r > 1$ it grows.

Worked example

Damped cosine through the filter.

Input $x[n]=2^{-n}\cos(\pi n/3+\pi/4)$; filter from L21: $H(z)=1+2z^{-1}+2z^{-2}+z^{-3}$.

Step 1 · Identify r, Ω₀, φ and z

$2^{-n}=(1/2)^n$, so $r=\tfrac12$, $\Omega_0=\pi/3$, $\phi=\pi/4$, and $z=re^{j\Omega_0}=\tfrac12 e^{j\pi/3}$ (check: $z^n=2^{-n}e^{j\pi n/3}$).

Step 2 · Powers of 1/z
$$z^{-1}=2e^{-j\pi/3},\qquad z^{-2}=4e^{-j2\pi/3},\qquad z^{-3}=8e^{-j\pi}$$
Step 3 · Substitute into H(z)
$$H(z)=1+2\cdot 2e^{-j\pi/3}+2\cdot 4e^{-j2\pi/3}+8e^{-j\pi}=1+4e^{-j\pi/3}+8e^{-j2\pi/3}+8e^{-j\pi}$$

ConceptTransient Versus Steady State
Why the first samples differ

Steady state begins at $n = M$.

The filter $y[n]=x[n]+2x[n-1]+2x[n-2]+x[n-3]$ has order $M=3$. The formula assumes $x$ has existed forever; the notebook starts $x[n]$ at $n=0$, so for $n<3$ some samples the filter needs are zero.

Filter output versus formula
$$\begin{array}{c|ccc} n & x[n] & \text{filter} & \text{formula}\\\hline 0 & 0.7071 & 0.7071 & 0.9845\\ 1 & -0.1294 & 1.2848 & 6.1838\\ 2 & -0.2415 & 0.9139 & 2.8458\\ 3 & -0.0884 & -0.1231 & -0.1231\\ 4 & 0.0162 & -0.7730 & -0.7730 \end{array}$$

formula $=13.748\cdot2^{-n}\cos(\pi n/3-1.499)$

Hand check at n = 3
$$\begin{aligned}y[3]&=x[3]+2x[2]+2x[1]+x[0]\\&=-0.0884-0.4830\\&\quad-0.2588+0.7071\\&=-0.1231\end{aligned}$$

All four taps see real input ($n-3\ge0$), so $y[3]$ matches the formula.

Rule: with $M+1$ taps the first $M$ outputs are start-up transient; from $n=M$ on the prediction is exact.

ConceptPeriodic Inputs via DFT
Periodic in, periodic out

Multiply DFTs.

$$Y[k] \,=\, H\!\big(e^{j k\,2\pi/L}\big)\, X[k],\;\; k = 0, \ldots, L-1$$

$X[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{L-1}x[n]e^{-j2\pi kn/L}$ is the DFT of one period. Inverse DFT: $x[n]=\tfrac1L\sum_k X[k]e^{j\Omega_k n}$ with $\Omega_k=2\pi k/L$. Each term is a complex exponential, so the filter multiplies it by $H(e^{j\Omega_k})$: that gives $Y[k]$ (transient ignored; period $L$ in ⇒ period $L$ out).

X = fft(x); H = fft(b, L); Y = H .* X; y = ifft(Y);

ConceptENES-222 · L22
DerivationWhy Multiplying DFTs Works
Where the product rule comes from

Three lines, using the eigen-property.

1 · Inverse DFT: input as a sum of exponentials
$$x[n]=\frac1L\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}X[k]\,e^{j\Omega_k n},\qquad \Omega_k=\frac{2\pi k}{L}\ \text{rad/sample}$$
2 · Filter each exponential

$e^{j\Omega_k n}=z^{n}$ with $z=e^{j\Omega_k}$ comes out as $H(e^{j\Omega_k})\,e^{j\Omega_k n}$; linearity handles the sum:

$$y[n]=\frac1L\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\big[H(e^{j\Omega_k})\,X[k]\big]\,e^{j\Omega_k n}$$
3 · Read off Y[k]

This is an inverse DFT with coefficients $Y[k]=H(e^{j\Omega_k})X[k]$. And $H(e^{j\Omega_k})=\sum_{m=0}^{M}b_m e^{-j2\pi km/L}$ is the DFT of $b$ zero-padded to length $L$.

Needs $L\ge M+1$ and a start-up transient that has died out.

Practice

Find $L$ for the DT input.

Signal
$$x[n] = A_1\cos(2\pi f_1 n + \phi_1) + A_2\cos(2\pi f_2 n + \phi_2) + A_3\cos(2\pi f_3 n + \phi_3)$$ $$f_1 = \tfrac{1}{8},\;\; f_2 = \tfrac{3}{20},\;\; f_3 = \tfrac{5}{12}$$
  • Write each $f_i=p_i/q_i$ in lowest terms (cycles/sample); its period is $L_i=q_i$.
  • $L$ is the LCM of the three periods; then locate the DFT bins $k_i=Lf_i$.
PracticeENES-222 · L22
Solution$L = 120$
Individual periods

$\cos(2\pi\tfrac pq n)$ repeats when $\tfrac pq L$ is an integer; with $\gcd(p,q)=1$ the smallest such $L$ is $q$ (so $1/f$ works only when $p=1$).
$f_1=\tfrac18$: $L_1=8$. $f_2=\tfrac3{20}$ ($\gcd=1$): $L_2=20$. $f_3=\tfrac5{12}$ ($\gcd=1$): $L_3=12$.

LCM

$8=2^3,\ 20=2^2\cdot5,\ 12=2^2\cdot3$: take the highest power of each prime.

$$L=\mathrm{lcm}(8,20,12)=2^3\cdot3\cdot5=120$$

Check: $120/8=15$, $120/20=6$, $120/12=10$ are integers.

Spectral picture

Bins $k_i=Lf_i$: $120\cdot\tfrac18=15$, $120\cdot\tfrac3{20}=18$, $120\cdot\tfrac5{12}=50$. Each cosine puts energy at $k_i$ and $L-k_i$: $\{15,105\}$, $\{18,102\}$, $\{50,70\}$.

So six nonzero DFT bins out of $120$.

DT factoid: a finite sum of periodic DT signals is always periodic. Not true in CT.

Worked example

Length-4 periodic input.

Filter $y[n]=x[n]-4x[n-1]+x[n-2]$, so $b=(1,-4,1)^{\top}$. One period of the input, $L=4$:

$$x[0:3]=(2,\,1,\,-3,\,5)^{\top}$$
Plan

X = fft(x); H = fft(b, 4);
Y = H .* X; y = ifft(Y);

With $L=4$: $e^{-j2\pi kn/4}=(-j)^{kn}$.

Step 1 · DFT of x
$$\begin{aligned}X[0]&=2+1-3+5=5\\ X[1]&=2+1(-j)+(-3)(-1)+5(j)=5+4j\\ X[2]&=2+1(-1)+(-3)(1)+5(-1)=-7\\ X[3]&=2+1(j)+(-3)(-1)+5(-j)=5-4j\end{aligned}$$

Powers used: $(-j)^2=-1$, $(-j)^3=j$, and $X[3]=X^{*}[1]$ as $x$ is real.

ConceptCascade of Filters
Cascade

$H(z) = H_1(z)\, H_2(z)$.
Order doesn't matter.

$$H(z) \,=\, H_1(z)\, H_2(z) \,=\, H_2(z)\, H_1(z)$$

Proof: feed $x[n]=z^n$. Filter 1 gives $H_1(z)z^n$; by linearity filter 2 scales that by $H_2(z)$, so $y[n]=H_2(z)H_1(z)z^n$.

Two FIR filters in series ⇒ one FIR filter whose coefficient vector is the linear convolution of the two.

Order is irrelevant because $H_1(z)$ and $H_2(z)$ are ordinary numbers for each $z$, and numbers commute (unlike matrices, $AB\neq BA$).

Worked ExampleCascade of Two FIRs
Worked example

Multiply the system functions.

$$b^{(1)} = (1, 2, 2, 1)^{\top},\;\; b^{(2)} = (1, -4, 1)^{\top}$$

Let $u=z^{-1}$. Then $H_1=1+2u+2u^2+u^3$ and $H_2=1-4u+u^2$. Multiply each term of $H_1$ by $H_2$:

$$\begin{aligned}1\cdot H_2&=1-4u+u^2\\ 2u\cdot H_2&=\phantom{1}\;\;2u-8u^2+2u^3\\ 2u^2\cdot H_2&=\phantom{1-4u+u^2}\;2u^2-8u^3+2u^4\\ u^3\cdot H_2&=\phantom{1-4u+u^2+2u^2\ }u^3-4u^4+u^5\end{aligned}$$
Collect powers of u

$u^0$: $1$. $\;u^1$: $-4+2=-2$. $\;u^2$: $1-8+2=-5$. $\;u^3$: $2-8+1=-5$. $\;u^4$: $2-4=-2$. $\;u^5$: $1$.

$$H(z)=1-2z^{-1}-5z^{-2}-5z^{-3}-2z^{-4}+z^{-5}$$
Cascade I/O
$$\begin{aligned}y[n]&=x[n]-2x[n-1]-5x[n-2]\\&\quad-5x[n-3]-2x[n-4]+x[n-5]\end{aligned}$$

Check at $z=1$: $H_1(1)H_2(1)=6\cdot(-2)=-12$ and $1-2-5-5-2+1=-12$ ✓

Solved Example A

Steady-state output for a cosine.

Filter $b=(1,2,1)$, input $x[n]=\cos(\pi n/2)$, so $\Omega_0=\pi/2$ rad/sample and $\phi=0$.

Step 1 · Frequency response
$$\begin{aligned}H(e^{j\Omega})&=1+2e^{-j\Omega}+e^{-j2\Omega}\\&=e^{-j\Omega}\big(e^{j\Omega}+2+e^{-j\Omega}\big)\\&=e^{-j\Omega}(2+2\cos\Omega)\end{aligned}$$

Euler: $e^{j\Omega}+e^{-j\Omega}=2\cos\Omega$.

Step 2 · Evaluate at Ω₀ = π/2
$$\begin{aligned}H(e^{j\pi/2})&=e^{-j\pi/2}(2+2\cos\tfrac\pi2)\\&=(-j)(2+0)=-2j\end{aligned}$$ $|H|=2$, $\angle H=-\pi/2$.
Step 3 · Apply the sinusoid rule
$$y[n]=2\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{2}n-\tfrac{\pi}{2})=2\sin(\tfrac{\pi}{2}n)$$

Identity: $\cos(\theta-\tfrac\pi2)=\sin\theta$.

Check
$x[n]$: $\dots,0,1,0,-1,0,\dots$ at $n=-1,0,1,2,3$.
$y[1]=x[1]+2x[0]+x[-1]=0+2+0=2=2\sin\tfrac\pi2$ ✓
$y[3]=x[3]+2x[2]+x[1]=0-2+0=-2=2\sin\tfrac{3\pi}2$ ✓

Solved Example B

Cascade of two simple filters.

$H_1(z)=1-z^{-1}$ and $H_2(z)=1+z^{-1}$. Find the combined $H(z)$, its frequency response, and the I/O equation.

Step 1 · Combined system function
$$H(z)=(1-z^{-1})(1+z^{-1})=1-z^{-2}$$

Difference of squares: $(1-u)(1+u)=1-u^2$ with $u=z^{-1}$.

Step 2 · Frequency response
$$\begin{aligned}H(e^{j\Omega})&=1-e^{-j2\Omega}=e^{-j\Omega}\big(e^{j\Omega}-e^{-j\Omega}\big)\\&=2j\,e^{-j\Omega}\sin\Omega\end{aligned}$$ $$|H(e^{j\Omega})|=2|\sin\Omega|$$

Here $z=e^{j\Omega}$. Euler: $\sin\Omega=(e^{j\Omega}-e^{-j\Omega})/2j$; $|2j\,e^{-j\Omega}|=2$.

Step 3 · I/O equation

Coefficients of $z^{-k}$: $b_0=1$, $b_1=0$, $b_2=-1$.

$$y[n]=x[n]-x[n-2]$$
Nulls

$\sin\Omega=0$ at $\Omega=0,\pi$: $H(e^{j0})=1-1=0$ and $H(e^{j\pi})=1-e^{-j2\pi}=0$ — rejects DC and $\pi$.

ReviewCommon Mistakes
Check before you hand in

Six slips to avoid.

1 · Damped input, undamped z

For $r^{n}\cos(\Omega_0 n+\phi)$ evaluate $H$ at $z=re^{j\Omega_0}$, not $e^{j\Omega_0}$. Same filter, $\Omega_0=\pi/3$: $|H(e^{j\Omega_0})|=3.46$ but $|H(\tfrac12 e^{j\Omega_0})|=13.75$.

2 · Quadrant of the angle

$H=-9-j10.39$ is in the third quadrant: $\angle H=\arctan(10.39/9)-\pi=-2.285$, not $+0.857$.

3 · Mixing up scale and shift

Amplitudes multiply ($A|H|$); phases add ($\phi+\angle H$). The frequency $\Omega_0$ never changes.

4 · Wrong period

$L$ is the LCM of the denominators of all $f_i=p_i/q_i$ in lowest terms: not their product, and not $1/f$ unless $p=1$.

5 · Padding and pointwise product

Use fft(b, L) with $L\ge M+1$ and combine element-wise, $H[k]X[k]$; never a matrix or dot product.

6 · Adding instead of multiplying

Cascade: $H=H_1H_2$, so the coefficient vectors convolve. $(1,2,2,1)+(1,-4,1)$ is wrong; the answer has $4+3-1=6$ taps.
