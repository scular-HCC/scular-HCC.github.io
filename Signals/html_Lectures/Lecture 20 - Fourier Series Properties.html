Properties 1–3

Conjugation and reversal
act through index negation.

PROP 1
$y(t) = x^{*}(t)$
$Y_k = X^{*}_{-k}$.
PROP 2
$x$ real ⇒ $X_{-k} = X^{*}_{k}$.
Conjugate symmetric spectrum.
PROP 3
$y(t) = x(-t)$
$Y_k = X_{-k}$.
CT vs DFT
Reversals are not circular here.

ConceptENES-222 · L20
ConceptWhy Properties 1 to 3 hold
Derivations

Conjugate and reverse inside the integral.

Throughout, $x$ has period $T_0$, $\omega_0=2\pi/T_0$, $X_k=\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}x(t)e^{-jk\omega_0 t}dt$.

Prop 1 · conjugation

Use $(ab)^{*}=a^{*}b^{*}$ and $(e^{-j\phi})^{*}=e^{+j\phi}$. Replacing $k$ by $-k$ in $X_k$ flips the exponent's sign, so:

$$\begin{aligned}Y_k&=\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}x^{*}(t)e^{-jk\omega_0 t}dt\\&=\Big[\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}x(t)e^{+jk\omega_0 t}dt\Big]^{*}=X_{-k}^{*}\end{aligned}$$
Prop 2 · real signals

$x$ real means $x^{*}=x$, so $Y_k=X_k$; Prop 1 gives $X_k=X_{-k}^{*}$.

Prop 3 · reversal

With $y(t)=x(-t)$, put $u=-t$, $dt=-du$ (the flipped limits absorb the minus sign; periodicity allows any length-$T_0$ interval):

$$\begin{aligned}Y_k&=\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}x(-t)e^{-jk\omega_0 t}dt\\&=\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}x(u)e^{+jk\omega_0 u}du=X_{-k}\end{aligned}$$

Worked ExampleEven & Odd Decomposition
Worked example

Split $s$ into even + odd parts.

Let $s$ be real with coefficients $S_k$. Define $$x(t) = \tfrac{s(t) + s(-t)}{2},\;\; y(t) = \tfrac{s(t) - s(-t)}{2}$$ so $x(-t)=x(t)$ (even), $y(-t)=-y(t)$ (odd), and $s=x+y$.

Use linearity and reversal (Prop 3)
$$X_k = \tfrac{S_k + S_{-k}}{2},\qquad Y_k = \tfrac{S_k - S_{-k}}{2}$$
Use real s (Prop 2): S-minus-k is S-k conjugate
$$X_k = \tfrac{S_k + S_k^{*}}{2} = \mathrm{Re}\{S_k\}$$ $$Y_k = \tfrac{S_k - S_k^{*}}{2} = \tfrac{2j\,\mathrm{Im}\{S_k\}}{2} = j\,\mathrm{Im}\{S_k\}$$

since $z+z^{*}=2\,\mathrm{Re}\,z$ and $z-z^{*}=2j\,\mathrm{Im}\,z$.

Symmetry

$X_k$ is real and $X_{-k}=X_k$ (even). $Y_k$ is purely imaginary and $Y_{-k}=-Y_k$ (odd). Real-and-even $\leftrightarrow$ real-and-even; real-and-odd $\leftrightarrow$ imaginary-and-odd.

ConceptTime Shift (Prop 4)
Time shift

Shift in time ⇒
linear phase ramp in $k$.

$$y(t) = x(t - T) \,\Rightarrow\, Y_k = e^{-j k \omega_0 T}\, X_k$$

$|Y_k| = |X_k|$ because $|e^{-j\phi}|=1$: magnitudes preserved.

The phase of $Y_k$ is $\angle X_k - k\omega_0 T$, tilting linearly in $k$. This is the continuous-time counterpart of the DFT shift property.

Derivation

Substitute $u=t-T$ ($t=u+T$) and use periodicity to keep a length-$T_0$ interval:

$$\begin{aligned}Y_k&=\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}x(t-T)e^{-jk\omega_0 t}dt\\&=\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}x(u)e^{-jk\omega_0 (u+T)}du\\&=e^{-jk\omega_0 T}\,\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}x(u)e^{-jk\omega_0 u}du\\&=e^{-jk\omega_0 T}X_k\end{aligned}$$

Worked ExampleTime Shift with Numbers
Worked example · numbers

Delay a pulse train by a quarter period.

$x(t)$: centered pulse train, duty $\alpha=\tfrac13$, so $X_0=\tfrac13$ and $X_k=\dfrac{\sin(k\pi/3)}{k\pi}$. Let $y(t)=x(t-T_0/4)$.

Phase factor

$\omega_0T=\tfrac{2\pi}{T_0}\cdot\tfrac{T_0}{4}=\tfrac\pi2$, so $Y_k=e^{-jk\pi/2}X_k$ with $e^{-j\pi/2}=-j$, $e^{-j\pi}=-1$, $e^{-j3\pi/2}=+j$, $e^{-j2\pi}=1$ (repeats every $4$ in $k$).

Sinc values

$X_1=\tfrac{0.8660}{3.1416}=0.2757$, $X_2=\tfrac{0.8660}{6.2832}=0.1378$, $X_3=\tfrac{\sin\pi}{3\pi}=0$, $X_4=\tfrac{-0.8660}{12.566}=-0.0689$, $X_5=\tfrac{-0.8660}{15.708}=-0.0551$.

Multiply row by row
$$\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}k&X_k&e^{-jk\pi/2}&Y_k\\\hline 0&0.3333&1&0.3333\\1&0.2757&-j&-0.2757j\\2&0.1378&-1&-0.1378\\3&0&j&0\\4&-0.0689&1&-0.0689\\5&-0.0551&-j&+0.0551j\end{array}$$

$k=5$: $e^{-j5\pi/2}=e^{-j\pi/2}=-j$, and $(-0.0551)(-j)=+0.0551j$.

Check

$|Y_k|=|X_k|$ in every row. Real signal: $Y_{-k}=Y_k^{*}$, e.g. $Y_{-1}=+0.2757j$. The notebook confirms this numerically (it uses $\alpha=0.3$).

Worked ExampleOdd Pulse Train
Worked example

Antisymmetric pulse train.

$x(t)=+1$ on $0\lt t\lt T_0/6$ and $-1$ on $-T_0/6\lt t<0$ (two pulses of width $T_0/6$, centered at $\pm T_0/12$), $0$ elsewhere in the period. Then $x(-t)=-x(t)$: real and odd.

Build from one centered pulse

Let $p(t)$ be the centered pulse train of height $1$ and duty $\alpha=1/6$. By the L19 result $P_k=\dfrac{\sin(k\pi/6)}{k\pi}$. Then $x(t)=p(t-\tfrac{T_0}{12})-p(t+\tfrac{T_0}{12})$.

Shift phases

$\omega_0\cdot\tfrac{T_0}{12}=\tfrac{2\pi}{12}=\tfrac{\pi}{6}$, so the shifts contribute $e^{\mp jk\pi/6}$.

Apply Prop 4 and linearity
$$X_k=\big(e^{-jk\pi/6}-e^{+jk\pi/6}\big)\tfrac{\sin(k\pi/6)}{k\pi}$$
Euler: e-to-minus-j-theta minus e-to-j-theta

$e^{-j\theta}-e^{j\theta}=-2j\sin\theta$ with $\theta=k\pi/6$:

$$X_k=-2j\sin\!\big(\tfrac{k\pi}{6}\big)\tfrac{\sin(k\pi/6)}{k\pi}=-j\,\tfrac{2}{k\pi}\sin^{2}\!\big(\tfrac{k\pi}{6}\big)$$
Check

$X_1=-j\tfrac{2}{\pi}\tfrac14=-j\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\approx-0.159j$; $X_2=-j\tfrac{2}{2\pi}\tfrac34\approx-0.239j$; $X_3=-j\tfrac{2}{3\pi}\approx-0.212j$; $X_0=0$ (zero mean). Purely imaginary and odd in $k$, as required for a real odd signal.

ConceptModulation (Prop 5)
Frequency shift / modulation

Multiply by a harmonic ⇒
shift the spectrum in $k$.

$$y(t) = x(t)\, e^{j K \omega_0 t} \,\Rightarrow\, Y_k = X_{k-K}$$

Each coefficient slides $K$ slots to the right (for $K>0$): the value that sat at index $k-K$ now sits at index $k$.

Useful for half-wave rectified signals, AM modulation, spectral shifting in filters.

Derivation

Combine the exponentials, $e^{jK\omega_0 t}e^{-jk\omega_0 t}=e^{-j(k-K)\omega_0 t}$:

$$\begin{aligned}Y_k&=\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}x(t)e^{jK\omega_0 t}e^{-jk\omega_0 t}dt\\&=\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}x(t)e^{-j(k-K)\omega_0 t}dt=X_{k-K}\end{aligned}$$

the last integral is the analysis equation evaluated at index $k-K$.

Worked ExampleHalf-Wave Rectified Cosine (1)
Worked example

Cosine times a half-duty pulse.

Let $s(t)$ be the centered pulse train of height $1$ and duty $\alpha = 1/2$ (it equals $1$ for $|t|\lt T_0/4$). Then $x(t)=s(t)\cos(\omega_0 t)$ keeps only the positive lobe of the cosine: a half-wave rectified cosine.

Euler on the cosine
$$x(t) = \tfrac12 s(t)e^{j\omega_0 t} + \tfrac12 s(t)e^{-j\omega_0 t}$$
Modulation with K equal to plus and minus 1
$$X_k \,=\, \tfrac12 S_{k-1} + \tfrac12 S_{k+1}$$
Input coefficients (L19, alpha one half)
$$S_0=\tfrac12,\qquad S_m=\tfrac{\sin(m\pi/2)}{m\pi}\ (m\neq0)$$

so $S_{\pm1}=\tfrac1\pi$ (as $\sin(\pm\pi/2)/(\pm\pi)$), and $S_m=0$ for every even $m\neq0$ because $\sin(m\pi/2)=0$. Only $m=0$ and odd $m$ are nonzero.

Cases k equals 0, plus or minus 1

$X_0=\tfrac12(S_{-1}+S_{1})=\tfrac12\big(\tfrac1\pi+\tfrac1\pi\big)=\tfrac1\pi$

$X_{\pm1}=\tfrac12(S_0+S_{\pm2})=\tfrac12\big(\tfrac12+0\big)=\tfrac14$ (for $k=1$: $S_0,S_2$; for $k=-1$: $S_{-2},S_0$)

Odd k with absolute value 3 or more

$k\pm1$ is even and nonzero, so $S_{k-1}=S_{k+1}=0$ and $X_k=0$.

Worked ExampleHalf-Wave Rectified Cosine (2)
Worked example, continued

Even $k$: use a shift identity.

Let $k=2m$ with $m$ an integer. Then $k\pm1$ are odd. Using $\sin(\phi\pm\pi/2)=\pm\cos\phi$ with $\phi=k\pi/2=m\pi$ and $\cos(m\pi)=(-1)^m$:

$$\begin{aligned}\sin\tfrac{(k+1)\pi}{2}&=\cos(m\pi)=(-1)^m\\ \sin\tfrac{(k-1)\pi}{2}&=-\cos(m\pi)=-(-1)^m\end{aligned}$$
Substitute into S
$$\begin{aligned}S_{k+1}&=\tfrac{(-1)^m}{(k+1)\pi}\\ S_{k-1}&=\tfrac{-(-1)^m}{(k-1)\pi}\end{aligned}$$
Average and combine the fractions
$$\begin{aligned}X_k&=\tfrac12\big(S_{k-1}+S_{k+1}\big)=\tfrac{(-1)^m}{2\pi}\Big[\tfrac{1}{k+1}-\tfrac{1}{k-1}\Big]\\&=\tfrac{(-1)^m}{2\pi}\cdot\tfrac{(k-1)-(k+1)}{k^2-1}=\tfrac{(-1)^m}{2\pi}\cdot\tfrac{-2}{k^2-1}\\&=\tfrac{(-1)^{k/2}}{\pi(1-k^{2})}\end{aligned}$$
Result
$$X_k = \begin{cases} 1/4, & k = \pm 1 \\ \dfrac{(-1)^{k/2}}{\pi(1-k^{2})}, & k \text{ even (incl. } 0) \\ 0, & \text{other odd } k\end{cases}$$

Check: $k=0$ gives $1/\pi$ (matches); $X_2=\tfrac{-1}{\pi(-3)}=\tfrac{1}{3\pi}\approx0.106$; $X_4=\tfrac{1}{\pi(-15)}\approx-0.021$.

ConceptConvolution Duality
Convolution properties

Pointwise vs convolution
swap between domains.

Time convolution
$$s(t) = \tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0} x(\tau)\, y(t - \tau)\,d\tau$$ $$S_k \,=\, X_k\, Y_k$$

Periodic convolution: the analogue of circular convolution. The factor $1/T_0$ makes the product exact.

Coefficient convolution
$$S_k \,=\, \sum_{\ell \in \mathbb{Z}} X_{\ell}\, Y_{k - \ell}$$ $$s(t) \,=\, x(t)\, y(t)$$

Infinite sum over $\mathbb{Z}$, not periodic in $k$.

ConceptENES-222 · L20
ConceptWhy the Duality Holds
Derivation 1 · time convolution
Insert the definition
$$S_k=\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}\Big[\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}x(\tau)y(t-\tau)d\tau\Big]e^{-jk\omega_0 t}dt$$
Swap integrals, split the exponential

With $e^{-jk\omega_0 t}=e^{-jk\omega_0 \tau}e^{-jk\omega_0 (t-\tau)}$ and $u=t-\tau$ (periodicity keeps the interval length $T_0$):

$$\begin{aligned}S_k&=\Big[\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}x(\tau)e^{-jk\omega_0\tau}d\tau\Big]\Big[\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}y(u)e^{-jk\omega_0 u}du\Big]\\&=X_kY_k\end{aligned}$$
Derivation 2 · product of signals
Expand y by synthesis
$$s(t)=x(t)\sum_{m}Y_m e^{jm\omega_0 t}$$
Apply the analysis equation
$$\begin{aligned}S_k&=\sum_m Y_m\,\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}x(t)e^{-j(k-m)\omega_0 t}dt\\&=\sum_m Y_m X_{k-m}\end{aligned}$$

each term is the modulation rule with $K=m$. Renaming $\ell=k-m$ gives $\sum_\ell X_\ell Y_{k-\ell}$.

ConceptTime Scaling (Prop 6)
Time scaling

Stretch the signal,
coefficients don't change.

$$y(t) = x(\beta t),\;\; \beta > 0 \,\Rightarrow\, Y_k = X_k$$

$y$ has period $T_0/\beta$ and fundamental $\beta\omega_0$: the harmonics ride along. The coefficient values are unchanged; only the frequency grid $k\beta\omega_0$ moves.

No DFT analogue: the DFT lives on a fixed grid of size $N$.

Derivation

Integrate over one period of $y$, $[0,T_0/\beta]$, with $u=\beta t$ ($dt=du/\beta$, $u$ runs $0$ to $T_0$):

$$\begin{aligned}Y_k&=\tfrac{\beta}{T_0}\int_0^{T_0/\beta}x(\beta t)e^{-jk(\beta\omega_0)t}dt\\&=\tfrac{\beta}{T_0}\int_0^{T_0}x(u)e^{-jk\omega_0 u}\tfrac{du}{\beta}\\&=X_k\end{aligned}$$

Solved ExampleLinearity and Shift
Solved Example A

Linearity and shift
combined.

Find the FS of $z(t)=2x(t)-y(t)$. Given: $x$ has period $T_0=4$ and $X_k=1/k$ for $k\neq0$ ($X_0$ not needed below); $y(t)=x(t-1)$, so the shift is $T=1$.

Tools

Prop 4: $y(t)=x(t-T)\Rightarrow Y_k=e^{-jk\omega_0 T}X_k$. Linearity: $z=2x-y\Rightarrow Z_k=2X_k-Y_k$. Euler: $e^{-j\phi}=\cos\phi-j\sin\phi$.

Step 1 · FS of $y$

$\omega_0=2\pi/4=\pi/2$, so $\omega_0T=\pi/2$:

$$Y_k=e^{-jk\pi/2}\cdot\frac{1}{k}$$
Step 2 · FS of $z$ by linearity
$$Z_k=2X_k-Y_k=\frac{2-e^{-jk\pi/2}}{k},\quad k\neq0$$
Step 3 · Evaluate

$Z_1=2-e^{-j\pi/2}=2-(\cos\tfrac\pi2-j\sin\tfrac\pi2)=2-(0-j)=2+j$

$Z_2=\tfrac{2-e^{-j\pi}}{2}=\tfrac{2-(-1)}{2}=\tfrac32$

$Z_3=\tfrac{2-e^{-j3\pi/2}}{3}=\tfrac{2-j}{3}$ (since $e^{-j3\pi/2}=0-j(-1)=j$); $Z_4=\tfrac{2-e^{-j2\pi}}{4}=\tfrac14$

Solved ExampleEven and Odd Parts of a Sawtooth
Solved Example B

Even and odd parts
of a sawtooth.

$x(t)=t/T_0$ for $0\le t\lt T_0$, repeated with period $T_0$ ($\omega_0=2\pi/T_0$). Find $x_e$, $x_o$ and their coefficients.

Definitions

$x_e(t)=\tfrac{x(t)+x(-t)}{2}$ (even part), $x_o(t)=\tfrac{x(t)-x(-t)}{2}$ (odd part).

Step 1 · Reverse the signal

For $0\lt t\lt T_0$, $-t$ is equivalent to $T_0-t\in(0,T_0)$ by periodicity, so $x(-t)=\tfrac{T_0-t}{T_0}=1-\tfrac{t}{T_0}$.

Step 2 · Even part
$$x_e(t)=\tfrac12\Big(\tfrac{t}{T_0}+1-\tfrac{t}{T_0}\Big)=\tfrac12$$
Step 3 · Odd part
$$x_o(t)=\tfrac12\Big(\tfrac{t}{T_0}-1+\tfrac{t}{T_0}\Big)=\tfrac{t}{T_0}-\tfrac12$$

a ramp from $-\tfrac12$ to $+\tfrac12$ over $(0,T_0)$.

Solved Example B, continued

Coefficients of $x$,
then split.

Step 4 · DC term
$$X_0=\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_0^{T_0}\tfrac{t}{T_0}dt=\tfrac{1}{T_0^2}\cdot\tfrac{T_0^2}{2}=\tfrac12$$
Step 5 · k not zero, integrate by parts

Take $u=t$, $dv=e^{-jk\omega_0 t}dt$; note $e^{-jk\omega_0T_0}=e^{-j2\pi k}=1$.

$$\begin{aligned}\int_0^{T_0}t\,e^{-jk\omega_0 t}dt&=\Big[\tfrac{t\,e^{-jk\omega_0 t}}{-jk\omega_0}\Big]_0^{T_0}+\tfrac{1}{jk\omega_0}\int_0^{T_0}e^{-jk\omega_0 t}dt\\&=\tfrac{jT_0}{k\omega_0}+0\end{aligned}$$

using $1/(-j)=j$; the last integral is $0$ over whole periods.

Step 6 · Divide
$$X_k=\tfrac{1}{T_0^{2}}\cdot\tfrac{jT_0}{k\omega_0}=\tfrac{j}{k\omega_0T_0}=\tfrac{j}{2\pi k},\quad k\neq0$$
Step 7 · Split with Re and Im
$$\mathrm{Re}\{X_k\}=\begin{cases}\tfrac12&k=0\\0&k\neq0\end{cases}$$$$j\,\mathrm{Im}\{X_k\}=\begin{cases}0&k=0\\ \tfrac{j}{2\pi k}&k\neq0\end{cases}$$

So $X^{(e)}_k$ is $\tfrac12$ at $k=0$ and $0$ elsewhere: exactly the constant $x_e=\tfrac12$ ✓. $X^{(o)}_k=\tfrac{j}{2\pi k}$ is imaginary and odd in $k$ ($x_o$ real, odd, zero mean) ✓.

Symmetry rule

Even real signal $\Leftrightarrow$ real, even coefficients. Odd real signal $\Leftrightarrow$ purely imaginary, odd coefficients.

SolutionModulation with a Sine: Checks
Solution · part 2

Two checks.

Symmetry

$s$ even, $\sin$ odd, so $y$ is real and odd: $Y_k$ purely imaginary and odd in $k$. Yes: $Y_{-1}=-Y_1$, $Y_0=0$.

Direct integral for k = 1

$\sin\cdot\cos$ is odd, so it integrates to $0$ over $[-T_0/4,T_0/4]$, and $\sin\pi=0$:

PitfallsCommon Mistakes
Common mistakes

Four property traps.

  1. Shift sign. $x(t-T)\leftrightarrow e^{-jk\omega_0T}X_k$: negative sign and the factor $k$. A delay makes the phase fall linearly with $k$.
  2. Modulation moves the index. $x\,e^{jK\omega_0t}\leftrightarrow X_{k-K}$: for $K>0$ the old line at $k=0$ ends up at $k=K$ (move right).
  3. Time scaling keeps $X_k$ but changes the fundamental. $\cos(\omega_0t)\to\cos(2\omega_0t)$ still has $X_{\pm1}=\tfrac12$ with respect to its own fundamental $2\omega_0$; on the old grid the lines sit at $k=\pm2$.
  4. Real is not even. Real: $X_{-k}=X_k^{*}$. Real and even: $X_k$ real. Real and odd: $X_k$ imaginary.
