By the end of this lecture you will

Translate signal operations
into coefficient operations.

  1. Use $x^{*}(t) \leftrightarrow X^{*}_{-k}$ and $x(-t) \leftrightarrow X_{-k}$.

ConceptConjugate & Reversal
Properties 1–3

Conjugation and reversal
act through index negation.

PROP 1
$y(t) = x^{*}(t)$
$Y_k = X^{*}_{-k}$.
PROP 2
$x$ real ⇒ $X_{-k} = X^{*}_{k}$.
Conjugate symmetric spectrum.
PROP 3
$y(t) = x(-t)$
$Y_k = X_{-k}$.
CT vs DFT
Reversals are not circular here.
$k \to -k$ in $\mathbb{Z}$, $t \to -t$ in $\mathbb{R}$.

Worked ExampleEven & Odd Decomposition
Worked example

Split $s$ into even + odd parts.

$$x(t) = \tfrac{s(t) + s(-t)}{2},\;\; y(t) = \tfrac{s(t) - s(-t)}{2}$$

For real $s$: $X_k$ is purely real and symmetric; $Y_k$ is purely imaginary and antisymmetric.

Coefficients
$$X_k = \tfrac{S_k + S_{-k}}{2} = \mathrm{Re}\{S_k\}$$ $$Y_k = \tfrac{S_k - S_{-k}}{2} = j\,\mathrm{Im}\{S_k\}$$

Real-and-even ↔ real-and-even. Real-and-odd ↔ imaginary-and-odd.

ConceptTime Shift (Prop 4)
Time shift

Shift in time ⇒
linear phase ramp in $k$.

$$y(t) = x(t - T) \,\Rightarrow\, Y_k = e^{-j k \Omega_0 T}\, X_k$$

$|Y_k| = |X_k|$ — magnitudes preserved.

The phase of $Y_k$ tilts linearly in $k$ — exactly the DT analogue of DFT 5.

Worked ExampleAsymmetric Pulse Train
Worked example

Antisymmetric pulse train.

Two rectangular pulses of width $T_0/6$, centered at $\pm T_0/6$, with opposite signs. The signal is real and odd: $x(-t) = -x(t)$.

Apply the time-shift property to the symmetric pulse train of width $T_0/3$.

Coefficients
$$X_k \,=\, (e^{-jk\pi/6} - e^{jk\pi/6})\cdot\tfrac{\sin(k\pi/6)}{k\pi}$$ $$=\, -j\,\tfrac{2}{k\pi}\sin^{2}\!\big(\tfrac{k\pi}{6}\big)$$

Purely imaginary, antisymmetric — as required for a real odd signal.

ConceptModulation (Prop 5)
Frequency shift / modulation

Multiply by a harmonic in time ⇒
shift the spectrum in $k$.

$$y(t) = x(t)\, e^{j K \Omega_0 t} \,\Rightarrow\, Y_k = X_{k-K}$$

Each Fourier coefficient slides $K$ slots to the right.

Useful for: half-wave rectified signals, AM modulation, spectral shifting in filters.

ConceptENES-222 · L20
Worked ExampleHalf-Wave Rectified Sinusoid
Worked example

Cosine times a half-duty pulse.

Let $s(t)$ be a symmetric pulse train of unit height, duty $\alpha = 1/2$.

$$x(t) = s(t)\cos(\Omega_0 t) = s(t)\,\tfrac{e^{j\Omega_0 t} + e^{-j\Omega_0 t}}{2}$$
Coefficients
$$X_k \,=\, \tfrac{S_{k-1} + S_{k+1}}{2}$$

With $S_k = \sin(k\pi/2)/(k\pi)$ from L19, this simplifies to

$$X_k \,=\, \begin{cases} 1/4, & k = \pm 1 \\ (-1)^{k/2}/(\pi(1-k^{2})), & k \text{ even, incl. 0} \\ 0, & \text{else}\end{cases}$$

ConceptConvolution Duality
Convolution properties

Pointwise vs convolution
swap between domains.

Time convolution
$$s(t) = \tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0} x(\tau)\, y(t - \tau)\,d\tau$$ $$S_k \,=\, X_k\, Y_k$$

Periodic — analogue of circular convolution.

Coefficient convolution
$$S_k \,=\, \sum_{\ell \in \mathbb{Z}} X_{\ell}\, Y_{k - \ell}$$ $$s(t) \,=\, x(t)\, y(t)$$

Not periodic — infinite sum over $\mathbb{Z}$.

ConceptTime Scaling (Prop 6)
Time scaling

Stretch the signal,
coefficients don't change.

$$y(t) = x(\beta t),\;\; \beta > 0 \,\Rightarrow\, Y_k = X_k$$

Squeezing the time axis squeezes the period too — the harmonics ride along with $\Omega_0 \to \beta\Omega_0$.

The coefficient values are unchanged. Only the underlying frequency grid shifts.

No DFT analogue — the DFT lives on a fixed grid of size $N$.

Solved Example A · §FS

FS of $z(t)=2x(t)-y(t)$
via linearity.

$X_k=1/k$ ($k\neq0$), $T_0=4$; $y(t)=x(t-1)$.

Step 1 · FS of $y$
$\Omega_0=2\pi/4=\pi/2$ $$Y_k=e^{-jk\pi/2}\cdot\frac{1}{k}$$
Step 2 · FS of $z$ by linearity
$$Z_k=2X_k-Y_k=\frac{2-e^{-jk\pi/2}}{k}$$
Step 3 · Evaluate
$Z_1=2-e^{-j\pi/2}=2-(-j)=2+j$ $Z_2=(2-e^{-j\pi})/2=(2+1)/2=3/2$

Solved Example B · §FS

Find the even and odd parts
of the triangular wave.

$x(t)$: triangular, period $T_0$, slope $+2/T_0$ on $[0,T_0/2]$.

Step 1 · Even part
$x_e(t)=\frac{x(t)+x(-t)}{2}$ — for triangular wave, $x_e(t)$ is a triangular wave symmetric about $t=0$.
Step 2 · Odd part
$x_o(t)=\frac{x(t)-x(-t)}{2}$ — antisymmetric.
Step 3 · FS relationship
$X_k$ real $\Rightarrow$ $x$ even. $X_k$ purely imaginary $\Rightarrow$ $x$ odd. For triangular wave: $X_k=-j\cdot c_k$ for odd $k$ (purely imaginary).
Symmetry rule

Even signal $\Leftrightarrow$ real FS coefficients. Odd signal $\Leftrightarrow$ purely imaginary FS coefficients.
