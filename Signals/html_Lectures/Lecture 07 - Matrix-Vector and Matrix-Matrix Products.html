ConceptMatrix–Vector Product
$A\,\mathbf{x}$ — two equivalent views

Rows dot $\mathbf{x}$, or $\mathbf{x}$ scales columns.

View A · Row dot product
$$y_i \,=\, \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij}\, x_j$$

The $i$-th entry of $\mathbf{y}$ is the dot product of $A$'s $i$-th row with $\mathbf{x}$.

View B · Column combination
$$\mathbf{y} \,=\, x_1\, \mathbf{a}^{(1)} + \cdots + x_n\, \mathbf{a}^{(n)}$$

$\mathbf{y}$ is a linear combination of $A$'s columns, with $x_j$ as coefficients.

Worked Example$A\mathbf{x}$ Two Ways
Worked example

Same result, two pictures.

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 & -1 \\ 2 & -1 & 5 \end{bmatrix},\;\; \mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ -4 \end{bmatrix}$$
Row view
$$y_1 = 3\cdot 2 + 1\cdot (-1) + (-1)(-4) = 9$$ $$y_2 = 2\cdot 2 + (-1)(-1) + 5\cdot (-4) = -15$$
Column view
$$\mathbf{y} = 2\!\begin{bmatrix}3\\2\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}1\\-1\end{bmatrix} - 4\!\begin{bmatrix}-1\\5\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}9\\-15\end{bmatrix}$$

ConceptLinearity / Superposition
Why matrices are linear systems

$A$ respects scaling and addition.

$$A\!\left(c_1 \mathbf{x}^{(1)} + c_2 \mathbf{x}^{(2)}\right) \,=\, c_1 A \mathbf{x}^{(1)} + c_2 A \mathbf{x}^{(2)}$$

An $m\times n$ matrix $A$ defines a linear transformation $\mathbb{R}^{n}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}^{m}$, also called a linear system:

$n$-dim input $\mathbf{x}$ ⟶ $m$-dim output $\mathbf{y} = A\mathbf{x}$.

PracticeUse Linearity
Practice

Given $A\mathbf{u}$ and $A\mathbf{v}$,
find $A(\mathbf{u} - 2\mathbf{v})$.

Setup
$$A\mathbf{u} = \begin{bmatrix}-1\\4\end{bmatrix},\;\; A\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix}5\\-2\end{bmatrix}$$

Don't compute with $A$. Use superposition.

PracticeENES-222 · L07
SolutionBy Superposition
Solution

Distribute, then combine.

Apply linearity
$$A(\mathbf{u} - 2\mathbf{v}) \,=\, A\mathbf{u} - 2 A\mathbf{v}$$
Substitute
$$= \begin{bmatrix}-1\\4\end{bmatrix} - 2\begin{bmatrix}5\\-2\end{bmatrix} \,=\, \begin{bmatrix}-11\\8\end{bmatrix}$$
Lesson

You never needed $A$ itself. Knowing how $A$ acts on a set of inputs is enough to know how it acts on every linear combination of them.

ConceptBuild $A$ from Its Action
Constructing the matrix

Columns of $A$ are where
the unit vectors $\mathbf{e}^{(j)}$ land.

$$\mathbf{x} = x_1 \mathbf{e}^{(1)} + \cdots + x_n \mathbf{e}^{(n)}$$
$$A\mathbf{x} = x_1\, A(\mathbf{e}^{(1)}) + \cdots + x_n\, A(\mathbf{e}^{(n)})$$

So the $j$-th column of $A$ is exactly the output $A(\mathbf{e}^{(j)})$.

To find the matrix of a transformation, feed it the standard basis and stack the results column-by-column.

Projection onto Re-axis
$$A\!\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix},\;\; A\!\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0\\0\end{bmatrix}$$ $$A \,=\, \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$$

Zeros out the second coordinate.

CCW rotation by $30^{\circ}$
$$B\!\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{3}/2\\1/2\end{bmatrix},\;\; B\!\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-1/2\\\sqrt{3}/2\end{bmatrix}$$ $$B \,=\, \begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{3}/2 & -1/2 \\ 1/2 & \sqrt{3}/2\end{bmatrix}$$

Columns = rotated images of the unit vectors.

ConceptMatrix–Matrix Product
Matrix–matrix product

Row $i$ of $A$ dots
column $j$ of $B$.

$$(AB)_{ij} \,=\, \sum_{k=1}^{p} a_{ik}\, b_{kj}$$

Inner dimension must match: $A$ is $m\times p$, $B$ is $p\times n$, product is $m\times n$.

Think of it as $n$ matrix-vector products — one per column of $B$.

ConceptENES-222 · L07
Worked ExampleCompute $AB$
Worked example

$A$ is $2\times 3$, $B$ is $3\times 2$.
Product is $2\times 2$.

$$A = \begin{bmatrix}2 & -1 & 4\\ 1 & 0 & -1\end{bmatrix},\;\; B = \begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 \\ 0 & 3 \\ 1 & 5\end{bmatrix}$$
Entry by entry
$$(AB)_{11} = 2(1) + (-1)(0) + 4(1) = 6$$ $$(AB)_{12} = 2(-2) + (-1)(3) + 4(5) = 13$$ $$(AB)_{21} = 1(1) + 0 + (-1)(1) = 0$$ $$(AB)_{22} = 1(-2) + 0 + (-1)(5) = -7$$
Result
$$AB \,=\, \begin{bmatrix}6 & 13 \\ 0 & -7\end{bmatrix}$$

Solved Example B · §2.2.3

Compute $AB$ and $BA$;
show $AB\neq BA$.

$A=\begin{bmatrix}2&{-1}\\1&3\end{bmatrix},\;B=\begin{bmatrix}1&4\\0&{-2}\end{bmatrix}$

Step 1 · $AB$
$$AB=\begin{bmatrix}2(1)+(-1)(0)&2(4)+(-1)(-2)\\1(1)+3(0)&1(4)+3(-2)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}2&10\\1&{-2}\end{bmatrix}$$
Step 2 · $BA$
$$BA=\begin{bmatrix}1(2)+4(1)&1(-1)+4(3)\\0(2)+(-2)(1)&0(-1)+(-2)(3)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}6&11\\{-2}&{-6}\end{bmatrix}$$
Non-commutativity

$AB\neq BA$ — matrix multiplication order matters.

Lecture 07Summary
Take with you

Four ways to see a matrix.

01 · ROW VIEW
$y_i = \sum_j a_{ij}\, x_j$
Each output is a dot product.
02 · COLUMN VIEW
$A\mathbf{x} = \sum_j x_j\, \mathbf{a}^{(j)}$
Output is a combination of columns.
03 · TRANSFORMATION
$A:\mathbb{R}^{n}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}^{m}$, linear.
Columns are $A(\mathbf{e}^{(j)})$.
04 · MATRIX PRODUCT
$(AB)_{ij} = \mathbf{a}_i^{\top} \mathbf{b}^{(j)}$
Inner dimension matches.
