ConceptSynthesis & Analysis
The DFT pair

One basis, two equations.

$$s \,=\, \tfrac{1}{N}\,V\,S \quad\text{(synthesis)}$$
$$S \,=\, V^{H}\,s \quad\text{(analysis)}$$

$s$ lives in the time domain, $S$ in the frequency domain.

$V$ is the $N\times N$ DFT matrix (written out on the last slide), $V_{nk} = e^{j(2\pi/N)kn}$. Its $k$-th column is the Fourier sinusoid $v^{(k)} = \big(e^{j(2\pi/N)k\cdot 0},\dots,e^{j(2\pi/N)k(N-1)}\big)^{\top}$, at frequency $\Omega_k = 2\pi k/N$.

Each entry $S[k] = \langle v^{(k)}, s\rangle = (v^{(k)})^{H}s = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} s[n]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$ measures how much of $v^{(k)}$ is present in $s$. (Inner product: $\langle a,b\rangle = a^{H}b$, conjugating the first vector.)

S = fft(s); s = ifft(S);

ConceptWhy It Works
Orthogonality of the Fourier columns

$V^{H} V = N\,I$ from the geometric sum.

$$\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} z^{n} \,=\, \begin{cases} N, & z = 1 \\ \tfrac{1 - z^{N}}{1 - z}, & z \neq 1 \end{cases}$$

Let $w = e^{j2\pi/N}$, so $w^{N} = 1$ and $v^{(k)}_n = w^{kn}$.

$$\langle v^{(k)}, v^{(\ell)}\rangle = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \overline{w^{kn}}\,w^{\ell n} = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \big(w^{\ell-k}\big)^{n}$$

So the sum is the geometric sum with $z = w^{\ell - k}$:

  • $k = \ell$: $z = w^{0} = 1$, every term is $1$; sum $= N$.
  • $k \neq \ell$: $\ell-k$ is a nonzero integer with $|\ell-k| < N$, not a multiple of $N$, so $z \neq 1$. Also $z^{N} = (w^{N})^{\ell-k} = 1$, so the sum is $\tfrac{1-1}{1-z} = 0$.

Hence $\langle v^{(k)}, v^{(\ell)}\rangle = N\,\delta_{k\ell}$, i.e. $V^{H}V = N I$.

Worked example · ≈ 5 min

A full DFT, $N=6$.

Signal: $s = (1,2,3,6,5,4)^{\top}$.

Let $w = e^{j2\pi/6} = e^{j\pi/3}$. The columns of $V$ are $v^{(k)}_n = w^{kn}$, at Fourier frequencies $\Omega_k = 2\pi k/6 = 0,\, \tfrac{\pi}{3},\, \tfrac{2\pi}{3},\, \pi,\, \tfrac{4\pi}{3},\, \tfrac{5\pi}{3}$.

Analysis: $S[k] = \langle v^{(k)}, s\rangle = \sum_{n=0}^{5} s[n]\,\bar w^{\,kn}$ with $\bar w = e^{-j\pi/3}$. Coefficients: $c_k = S[k]/6$.

Powers of w̄ (table)

$\bar w = e^{-j\pi/3} = \cos\tfrac{\pi}{3} - j\sin\tfrac{\pi}{3} = \tfrac12 - j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, and $\bar w^{n} = e^{-j\pi n/3}$:

$$\begin{array}{c|cccccc} n & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\ \hline \bar w^{n} & 1 & \tfrac12 - j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} & -\tfrac12 - j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} & -1 & -\tfrac12 + j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} & \tfrac12 + j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} \end{array}$$
k = 0 (DC)

Every weight is $\bar w^{0\cdot n} = 1$, so

$$S[0] = 1+2+3+6+5+4 = 21,\qquad c_0 = \tfrac{21}{6} = \tfrac{7}{2}$$

$c_0$ is the average of $s$.

SolutionDC Spectrum → Constant Signal
Solution

A constant in time.

Apply synthesis
$$s[n] = \tfrac{1}{8}\sum_{k=0}^{7} V_{nk}S[k] = \tfrac{1}{8}\,V_{n0}\cdot 1 = \tfrac{1}{8}\,e^{j2\pi\cdot 0\cdot n/8}$$ $$s = \tfrac{1}{8}\,v^{(0)}$$
Read off the first column

$V_{n0} = e^{0} = 1$ for every $n$: the $k=0$ column is all ones, frequency $\Omega_0 = 0$.

$$s \,=\, \tfrac{1}{8}\,(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1)^{\top}$$
Reverse direction (check)

Start from $s[n] = \tfrac18$. The analysis equation gives

$$S[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{7} \tfrac18\,e^{-j2\pi kn/8} = \tfrac18\sum_{n=0}^{7} z^{n},\quad z = e^{-j2\pi k/8}$$

$k = 0$: $z = 1$, so $S[0] = \tfrac18\cdot 8 = 1$.

$k = 1,\dots,7$: $z \neq 1$ and $z^{8} = 1$, so the sum is $0$.

$$S = (1,0,0,0,0,0,0,0)^{\top}\ \checkmark$$

A single-bin spectrum ⇔ a single Fourier sinusoid in time.

Why a real cosine uses two bins

Euler's formula, then the synthesis equation.

Split the cosine (N = 8)
$$\cos\tfrac{\pi n}{4}=\tfrac12e^{j\pi n/4}+\tfrac12e^{-j\pi n/4}$$$$e^{-j\pi n/4}=e^{j7\pi n/4}\;(\text{since }e^{j2\pi n}=1)$$$$\Rightarrow\;s=\tfrac12v^{(1)}+\tfrac12v^{(7)}$$

$$s=\tfrac1NVS\;\Rightarrow\;c_k=\tfrac{S[k]}{N}\;\Rightarrow\;S[1]=S[7]=\tfrac N2=4$$$$A\cos(\Omega_kn+\varphi):\;\;S[k]=\tfrac{NA}{2}e^{j\varphi},\;\;S[N-k]=\tfrac{NA}{2}e^{-j\varphi}$$

Common MistakesFour Traps
Common mistakes · ≈ 2 min

Four traps, each with a number that exposes it.

1 · Swapping analysis and synthesis

Analysis uses $V^{H}$ (conjugate weights), synthesis uses $V$. For $s=(1,2,3,4)$ the wrong sign gives $S[1]=-2-2j$ instead of $-2+2j$.

2 · An extra factor of N

fft gives $S=V^{H}s$ with no $1/N$ ($S[0]=21$ for $N=6$); ifft divides by $N$ itself.

3 · Expecting one bin for a real sinusoid

A single bin is a complex sinusoid ($S[3]=1$, $N=8$, slide 19). A real cosine needs $S[k]$ and $S[N-k]$, conjugates of each other.

4 · Mixing up bin and frequency

Bin $k$ is at $\Omega_k=2\pi k/N$ rad/sample, not at $k$ rad. For $N=64$, $k=8$ is $\pi/4$.

ReferenceThe DFT Matrix $V$
Reference · keep handy

The DFT matrix $V$ and its columns $v^{(k)}$.

$$V = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & \cdots & 1 \\ 1 & w & w^{2} & \cdots & w^{N-1} \\ 1 & w^{2} & w^{4} & \cdots & w^{2(N-1)} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots \\ 1 & w^{N-1} & w^{2(N-1)} & \cdots & w^{(N-1)^2} \end{bmatrix}$$

$w = e^{j2\pi/N}$, $\;V_{nk} = w^{kn}$ (row $n$, column $k$).

Column $k$ is the Fourier sinusoid $v^{(k)}$ at $\Omega_k = 2\pi k/N$; $V$ is symmetric ($V_{nk}=V_{kn}$), so $V^{H}=\overline{V}=W$.

Example, $N=4$ ($w = j$):

$$V_4 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & j & -1 & -j \\ 1 & -1 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & -j & -1 & j \end{bmatrix}$$

$V^{H}V = N I$, so $S = V^{H}s$ and $s = \tfrac{1}{N}VS$.
