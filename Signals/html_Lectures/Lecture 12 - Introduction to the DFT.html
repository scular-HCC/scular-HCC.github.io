MotivationWhy the DFT
Motivation

Hidden sinusoids,
visible in the spectrum.

Example signal (N = 200)

$s[n] = 4.7\cos(0.12\pi n - 1.3) + 3.8\cos(0.19\pi n + 0.8) + 5.1\cos(0.23\pi n + 2.4) + 2.0\,\eta[n]$, where $\eta[n]$ is random noise (standard normal samples).

The time plot looks like a smear, but $|S[k]|$ peaks at $k = \Omega N/2\pi = 12,\,19,\,23$ (on the plot axis, $k/N = 0.06,\,0.095,\,0.115$).

A cosine of amplitude $A$ gives a peak $\approx AN/2 = 470,\,380,\,510$: the DFT resolves $s$ into components whose amplitudes you read off the plot.

The time signal $s[n]$ (top) looks like a smear, but its DFT magnitude $|S[k]|$ (bottom) resolves it into three sharp peaks at the hidden frequencies $\Omega = 0.12\pi,\ 0.19\pi,\ 0.23\pi$.

ConceptSynthesis & Analysis
The DFT pair

One basis, two equations.

$$s \,=\, \tfrac{1}{N}\,V\,S \quad\text{(synthesis)}$$
$$S \,=\, V^{H}\,s \quad\text{(analysis)}$$

$s$ lives in the time domain, $S$ in the frequency domain.

$V$ is the $N\times N$ DFT matrix (written out on the last slide), $V_{nk} = e^{j(2\pi/N)kn}$. Its $k$-th column is the Fourier sinusoid $v^{(k)} = \big(e^{j(2\pi/N)k\cdot 0},\dots,e^{j(2\pi/N)k(N-1)}\big)^{\top}$, at frequency $\Omega_k = 2\pi k/N$.

Each entry $S[k] = \langle v^{(k)}, s\rangle = (v^{(k)})^{H}s = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} s[n]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$ measures how much of $v^{(k)}$ is present in $s$. (Inner product: $\langle a,b\rangle = a^{H}b$, conjugating the first vector.)

S = fft(s); s = ifft(S);

ConceptWhy It Works
Orthogonality of the Fourier columns

$V^{H} V = N\,I$ from the geometric sum.

$$\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} z^{n} \,=\, \begin{cases} N, & z = 1 \\ \tfrac{1 - z^{N}}{1 - z}, & z \neq 1 \end{cases}$$

Let $w = e^{j2\pi/N}$, so $w^{N} = 1$ and $v^{(k)}_n = w^{kn}$.

$$\langle v^{(k)}, v^{(\ell)}\rangle = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \overline{w^{kn}}\,w^{\ell n} = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \big(w^{\ell-k}\big)^{n}$$

So the sum is the geometric sum with $z = w^{\ell - k}$:

  • $k = \ell$: $z = w^{0} = 1$, every term is $1$; sum $= N$.
  • $k \neq \ell$: $\ell-k$ is a nonzero integer with $|\ell-k| < N$, not a multiple of $N$, so $z \neq 1$. Also $z^{N} = (w^{N})^{\ell-k} = 1$, so the sum is $\tfrac{1-1}{1-z} = 0$.

Hence $\langle v^{(k)}, v^{(\ell)}\rangle = N\,\delta_{k\ell}$, i.e. $V^{H}V = N I$.

Worked ExampleDFT for $N=6$ · Setup
Worked example

A full DFT, $N=6$.

Signal: $s = (1,2,3,6,5,4)^{\top}$.

Let $w = e^{j2\pi/6} = e^{j\pi/3}$. The columns of $V$ are $v^{(k)}_n = w^{kn}$, at Fourier frequencies $\Omega_k = 2\pi k/6 = 0,\, \tfrac{\pi}{3},\, \tfrac{2\pi}{3},\, \pi,\, \tfrac{4\pi}{3},\, \tfrac{5\pi}{3}$.

Analysis: $S[k] = \langle v^{(k)}, s\rangle = \sum_{n=0}^{5} s[n]\,\bar w^{\,kn}$ with $\bar w = e^{-j\pi/3}$. Coefficients: $c_k = S[k]/6$.

Powers of w̄ (table)

$\bar w = e^{-j\pi/3} = \cos\tfrac{\pi}{3} - j\sin\tfrac{\pi}{3} = \tfrac12 - j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, and $\bar w^{n} = e^{-j\pi n/3}$:

$$\begin{array}{c|cccccc} n & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\ \hline \bar w^{n} & 1 & \tfrac12 - j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} & -\tfrac12 - j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} & -1 & -\tfrac12 + j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} & \tfrac12 + j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} \end{array}$$
k = 0 (DC)

Every weight is $\bar w^{0\cdot n} = 1$, so

$$S[0] = 1+2+3+6+5+4 = 21,\qquad c_0 = \tfrac{21}{6} = \tfrac{7}{2}$$

$c_0$ is the average of $s$.

Worked ExampleDFT for $N=6$ · $c_1$ and $c_5$
Worked example, continued

Compute $c_1$ and $c_5$.

k = 1: weights w̄ⁿ from the table
$$S[1] = 1(1) + 2\big(\tfrac12 - j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\big) + 3\big(-\tfrac12 - j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\big) + 6(-1) + 5\big(-\tfrac12 + j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\big) + 4\big(\tfrac12 + j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\big)$$ $$\mathrm{Re}\,S[1] = 1 + 1 - \tfrac32 - 6 - \tfrac52 + 2 = -6,\qquad \mathrm{Im}\,S[1] = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}(-2 - 3 + 5 + 4) = 2\sqrt3$$ $$S[1] = -6 + 2\sqrt3\,j,\qquad c_1 = \tfrac{S[1]}{6} = -1 + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{3}\,j = -1 + \tfrac{j}{\sqrt3}$$
k = 5: conjugate weights

$\bar w^{5n} = e^{-j5\pi n/3} = e^{j\pi n/3}\,e^{-j2\pi n} = w^{n} = \overline{\bar w^{\,n}}$. Since $s$ is real,

$$S[5] = \overline{S[1]} = -6 - 2\sqrt3\,j,\qquad c_5 = -1 - \tfrac{j}{\sqrt3}$$

In general $c_k$ and $c_{N-k}$ are complex conjugates when $s$ is real — preview of L13.

PracticeSpectrum with a Single Peak
Practice

What is $s$ if $S = e_0$
at length $N=8$?

Setup
$$S \,=\, (1,\,0,\,0,\,0,\,0,\,0,\,0,\,0)^{\top}, \quad N = 8, \quad V_{nk} = e^{j2\pi kn/8}$$
  • Only one nonzero entry — only one column of $V$ contributes.
  • Which column is $v^{(0)}$, and which frequency is $\Omega_0 = 2\pi\cdot 0/8$?
  • Apply the synthesis equation $s = \tfrac{1}{8}VS$.
PracticeENES-222 · L12
SolutionDC Spectrum → Constant Signal
Solution

A constant in time.

Apply synthesis
$$s[n] = \tfrac{1}{8}\sum_{k=0}^{7} V_{nk}S[k] = \tfrac{1}{8}\,V_{n0}\cdot 1 = \tfrac{1}{8}\,e^{j2\pi\cdot 0\cdot n/8}$$ $$s = \tfrac{1}{8}\,v^{(0)}$$
Read off the first column

$V_{n0} = e^{0} = 1$ for every $n$: the $k=0$ column is all ones, frequency $\Omega_0 = 0$.

$$s \,=\, \tfrac{1}{8}\,(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1)^{\top}$$
Reverse direction (check)

Start from $s[n] = \tfrac18$. The analysis equation gives

$$S[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{7} \tfrac18\,e^{-j2\pi kn/8} = \tfrac18\sum_{n=0}^{7} z^{n},\quad z = e^{-j2\pi k/8}$$

$k = 0$: $z = 1$, so $S[0] = \tfrac18\cdot 8 = 1$.

$k = 1,\dots,7$: $z \neq 1$ and $z^{8} = 1$, so the sum is $0$.

$$S = (1,0,0,0,0,0,0,0)^{\top}\ \checkmark$$

A single-bin spectrum ⇔ a single Fourier sinusoid in time.

Solved Example A · §3.2.1

Compute $S[0]$, $S[1]$, $S[5]$.

Signal: $\mathbf{s}=(1,2,3,6,5,4)^\top$.

$N=6$, $\bar w = e^{-j\pi/3} = \tfrac12 - j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, so $S[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{5}s[n]\,\bar w^{\,kn}$.

Step 1 · S[0]
$$S[0]=1+2+3+6+5+4=21$$
Step 2 · S[1] by reducing powers

$\bar w^{3}=e^{-j\pi}=-1$, so $\bar w^{4}=-\bar w$ and $\bar w^{5}=-\bar w^{2}$:

$$S[1]=1+2\bar w+3\bar w^{2}-6-5\bar w-4\bar w^{2} = -5-3\bar w-\bar w^{2}$$

With $\bar w^{2}=-\tfrac12-j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$:

$$S[1]=-5-\tfrac32+j\tfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}+\tfrac12+j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=-6+2\sqrt3\,j\approx-6+3.464j$$
Step 3 · S[5] by the rule below (N − k = 5, k = 1)
$$S[5]=\overline{S[1]}=-6-2\sqrt3\,j\approx-6-3.464j$$
Rule

For real $\mathbf{s}$: $S[N-k]=\overline{S[k]}$, since $e^{-j2\pi(N-k)n/N} = e^{+j2\pi kn/N}$.

Solved Example B · §3.2.1

Invert: find $\mathbf{s}$ from
$S=(4,0,0,0)^\top$, $N=4$.

Synthesis: $s[n]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} S[k]\,e^{j2\pi kn/N}$, for $n=0,1,2,3$.

Step 1 · Only the DC bin is nonzero
$$s[n]=\tfrac{1}{4}\big(4\,e^{j2\pi\cdot 0\cdot n/4}+0+0+0\big)=\tfrac14\cdot 4\cdot 1 = 1$$

for each of $n=0,1,2,3$.

Step 2 · Result and check
$$\mathbf{s}=(1,1,1,1)^\top$$

Check: $S[0]=1+1+1+1=4$; for $k=2$: $1-1+1-1=0$ (similarly $k=1,3$).

DC interpretation

Energy only at $k=0$ $\Leftrightarrow$ constant signal. $S[0]/N$ equals the signal's average value.

Take with you

Six facts about the DFT.

01 · BASIS
$V_{nk} = e^{j(2\pi/N)kn}$
$N$ orthogonal complex sinusoids.
02 · PAIR
$S = V^{H} s,\;\; s = \tfrac{1}{N} V S$
$\mathtt{fft}$ / $\mathtt{ifft}$.
03 · ORTHOGONALITY
$V^{H} V = N I$
Geometric sum at $N$-th roots of unity.
04 · SINGLE BIN
$S[k] \neq 0$ alone ⇔ pure sinusoid.
Frequency $\Omega = k\,2\pi/N$.
05 · SYMMETRY
$V$ is symmetric.
$V^{H} = \overline{V} = W$, the conjugate matrix, $W_{nk}=e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$ — next lecture.
06 · MEANING
$|S[k]|$ — content at $\Omega_k$.
Peaks reveal hidden sinusoids.

ReferenceThe DFT Matrix $V$
Reference · keep handy

The DFT matrix $V$ and its columns $v^{(k)}$.

$$V = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & \cdots & 1 \\ 1 & w & w^{2} & \cdots & w^{N-1} \\ 1 & w^{2} & w^{4} & \cdots & w^{2(N-1)} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots \\ 1 & w^{N-1} & w^{2(N-1)} & \cdots & w^{(N-1)^2} \end{bmatrix}$$

$w = e^{j2\pi/N}$, $\;V_{nk} = w^{kn}$ (row $n$, column $k$).

Column $k$ is the Fourier sinusoid $v^{(k)}$ at $\Omega_k = 2\pi k/N$; $V$ is symmetric ($V_{nk}=V_{kn}$), so $V^{H}=\overline{V}=W$.

Example, $N=4$ ($w = j$):

$$V_4 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & j & -1 & -j \\ 1 & -1 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & -j & -1 & j \end{bmatrix}$$

$V^{H}V = N I$, so $S = V^{H}s$ and $s = \tfrac{1}{N}VS$.
