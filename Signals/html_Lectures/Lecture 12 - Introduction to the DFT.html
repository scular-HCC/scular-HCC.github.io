MotivationWhy the DFT
Motivation

Hidden sinusoids,
visible in the spectrum.

Example signal

A 200-point vector built from three real sinusoids at $\omega = 0.12\pi$, $0.19\pi$, $0.23\pi$ plus noise. The time plot looks like a smear; the magnitude spectrum $|S|$ shows three clear peaks on each half.

The DFT resolves $s$ into sinusoidal components whose amplitudes you can read directly off the plot. That is the core engineering reason it matters.

ConceptSynthesis & Analysis
The DFT pair

One basis, two equations.

$$s \,=\, \tfrac{1}{N}\,V\,S \quad\text{(synthesis)}$$
$$S \,=\, V^{H}\,s \quad\text{(analysis)}$$

$s$ lives in the time domain, $S$ in the frequency domain.

Each entry $S[k] = \langle v^{(k)}, s\rangle$ measures how much of the $k$-th Fourier sinusoid is present in $s$.

S = fft(s); s = ifft(S);

ConceptWhy It Works
Orthogonality of the Fourier columns

$V^{H} V = N\,I$ from the geometric sum.

$$\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} z^{n} \,=\, \begin{cases} N, & z = 1 \\ \tfrac{1 - z^{N}}{1 - z}, & z \neq 1 \end{cases}$$

Set $z = v^{\ell - k}$ where $v = e^{j\,2\pi/N}$. Since $v^{N} = 1$:

  • If $k = \ell$, every term is $1$; sum $= N$.
  • If $k \neq \ell$, $z^{N} = 1$ and $z \neq 1$; sum $= 0$.

So $\langle v^{(k)}, v^{(\ell)}\rangle = N\,\delta_{k\ell}$.

Worked ExampleDFT for $N=6$
Worked example

Compute $c_0$, $c_1$, $c_5$
for $s = (1,2,3,6,5,4)^{\top}$.

Fourier frequencies: $\omega = 0,\, \tfrac{\pi}{3},\, \tfrac{2\pi}{3},\, \pi,\, \tfrac{4\pi}{3},\, \tfrac{5\pi}{3}$.

Coefficients $c_k = \langle v^{(k)}, s\rangle / 6$.

$k = 0$ — DC
$$c_0 = \tfrac{1}{6}(1+2+3+4+5+6) = \tfrac{21}{6} = \tfrac{7}{2}$$
$k = 1$
$$c_1 = -1 + j\sqrt{3}$$
$k = 5$ — conjugate of $c_1$
$$c_5 = -1 - j\sqrt{3}$$

$c_k$ and $c_{N-k}$ are complex conjugates because $s$ is real — preview of L13.

PracticeENES-222 · L12
SolutionDC Spectrum → Constant Signal
Solution

A constant in time.

Apply synthesis
$$s \,=\, \tfrac{1}{8}\,V\,S \,=\, \tfrac{1}{8}\,v^{(0)}$$
Read off $v^{(0)}$

The $k=0$ column is the all-ones vector — its frequency is $\omega = 0$.

$$s \,=\, \tfrac{1}{8}\,(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1)^{\top}$$
Reverse direction

Starting from $s = \tfrac{1}{8}\,\mathbf{1}$, the analysis equation gives

$$S \,=\, V^{H} s \,=\, \tfrac{1}{8}\,V^{H}\,v^{(0)} \,=\, \tfrac{1}{8}\,(8,0,\ldots,0)^{\top}$$ $$=\, (1,0,0,0,0,0,0,0)^{\top}.$$

A single-bin spectrum ⇔ a single Fourier sinusoid in time.

Lecture 12Summary
Take with you

Five facts about the DFT.

01 · BASIS
$V_{nk} = e^{j(2\pi/N)kn}$
$N$ orthogonal complex sinusoids.
02 · PAIR
$S = V^{H} s,\;\; s = \tfrac{1}{N} V S$
$\mathtt{fft}$ / $\mathtt{ifft}$.
03 · ORTHOGONALITY
$V^{H} V = N I$
Geometric sum at $N$-th roots of unity.
04 · SINGLE BIN
$S[k] \neq 0$ alone ⇔ pure sinusoid.
Frequency $\omega = k\,2\pi/N$.
05 · SYMMETRY
$V$ is symmetric.
$V^{H} = V^{*} = W$ — next lecture.
06 · MEANING
$|S[k]|$ — content at $\omega_k$.
Peaks reveal hidden sinusoids.
