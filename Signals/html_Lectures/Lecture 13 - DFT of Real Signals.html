Worked ExampleSingle-Bin Inverses · $N=8$
Bin $k=4$ — Nyquist
$$S = e_4 \Rightarrow s[n] = \tfrac{1}{8}\,e^{j\pi n} = \tfrac{(-1)^{n}}{8}$$ $$s = \tfrac{1}{8}(1,-1,1,-1,1,-1,1,-1)^{\top}$$
Bin $k=3$ — complex sinusoid
$$S = e_3 \Rightarrow s[n] = \tfrac{1}{8}\,e^{j(3\pi/4)n}$$

A complex-valued vector — to get a real cosine we need two symmetric bins.

Worked ExampleSymmetric Pair → Real Cosine
Two symmetric bins

$S[k] = S[N-k]$ produces a real cosine.

Spectrum
$$S = (0,0,0,1,0,1,0,0)^{\top}$$

$k=3$ and $k=5 = N-3$.

Time-domain
$$s = v^{(3)} + v^{(5)} \to s[n] = \tfrac{1}{4}\cos(\tfrac{3\pi n}{4})$$ $$s = \tfrac{1}{8}(2, -\sqrt{2}, 0, \sqrt{2}, -2, \sqrt{2}, 0, -\sqrt{2})^{\top}$$

Combining $e^{j\omega n}$ and $e^{-j\omega n}$ yields $2\cos\omega n$. That's the key identity.

ConceptLinearity (DFT 1)
DFT 1 · Linearity

Decompose, transform, recombine.

$$\alpha x + \beta y \,\leftrightarrow\, \alpha X + \beta Y$$

Both analysis and synthesis are matrix-vector products — so superposition is automatic.

Combine known DFT pairs to handle complicated spectra without doing the integral.

Example
$$S = (1,0,0,3,-2,3,0,0)^{\top}$$ $$s[n] = \tfrac{1}{8} \,+\, \tfrac{3}{4}\cos(\tfrac{3\pi n}{4}) \,-\, \tfrac{1}{4}(-1)^{n}$$

ConceptCircular Conjugate Symmetry (DFT 2)
DFT 2 · Symmetry for real signals

Real $s$ ⇒ conjugate symmetric $S$.

$$S[N-k] \,=\, S^{*}[k],\;\; k = 1,\ldots,N-1$$
$$S[0],\, S[N/2] \in \mathbb{R}$$

$v^{(k)}$ and $v^{(N-k)}$ are complex conjugates — so the inner products with a real $s$ are too.

Indices $k$ and $N-k$ sit symmetrically across $k = 0$ when the bins are laid out around the unit circle.

ConceptENES-222 · L13
VisualBins on the Unit Circle
Bin layout

Pair $k$ with $N - k$.

Mark the bins $k = 0, 1, \ldots, N-1$ counter-clockwise around the unit circle, with $k=0$ at angle $0$. Then $k$ and $N-k$ are mirror images across the real axis.

That mirror is exactly the conjugate. $S[k]$ and $S[N-k]$ being complex conjugates is the same statement.

FIG. · For $N=10$, the conjugate pairs are $(1,9), (2,8), (3,7), (4,6)$. Bins $0$ and $5$ stand alone — real-valued.

ConceptReal Cosine Expansion
Real signal as a sum of cosines

Polar form + conjugate pairs
give the real series.

$$s[n] \,=\, \tfrac{1}{N}\,S[0] \;+\; \tfrac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N/2-1} |S[k]|\,\cos\!\Big(\tfrac{2\pi k n}{N} + \angle S[k]\Big) \;+\; \tfrac{1}{N}\,S[N/2]\,(-1)^{n}$$

The last term appears only when $N$ is even. Frequencies are restricted to $[0,\pi]$ — exactly the effective DT range from L04.

PracticeExpand a Real Signal · $N=8$
Practice

Write $s[n]$ as a sum of cosines.

DFT
$$S = (1,\, 1+2j,\, -4j,\, 3-j,\, -2,\, 3+j,\, 4j,\, 1-2j)^{\top}$$
  • Verify circular conjugate symmetry: $S[8-k] = S^{*}[k]$ for $k = 1, 2, 3$.
  • $S[0] = 1$ and $S[4] = -2$ are real — they become the DC and Nyquist terms.
  • Put each nonzero $S[k]$ in polar form: $|S[k]|$ and $\angle S[k]$.

SolutionReal Cosine Expansion
Solution

Three cosines, DC, Nyquist.

Magnitudes & phases
$$|S[1]| = \sqrt{5},\;\; \angle S[1] = 1.1071$$ $$|S[2]| = 4,\;\; \angle S[2] = -\tfrac{\pi}{2}$$ $$|S[3]| = \sqrt{10},\;\; \angle S[3] = -0.3218$$
DC and Nyquist
$$\tfrac{1}{8}\,S[0] = \tfrac{1}{8},\;\; \tfrac{1}{8}\,S[4]\,(-1)^{n} = -\tfrac{1}{4}(-1)^{n}$$
Final answer
$$s[n] \,=\, \tfrac{1}{8} \;+\; \tfrac{\sqrt{5}}{4}\cos\!\big(\tfrac{\pi n}{4} + 1.1071\big)$$ $$\quad +\, \cos\!\big(\tfrac{\pi n}{2} - \tfrac{\pi}{2}\big)$$ $$\quad +\, \tfrac{\sqrt{10}}{4}\cos\!\big(\tfrac{3\pi n}{4} - 0.3218\big)$$ $$\quad -\, \tfrac{1}{4}(-1)^{n}$$

Solved ExampleTwo Fully-Solved Examples
Solved Example A · §3.3.1

Verify conjugate symmetry
for $\mathbf{s}=(1,2,3,2)^\top$, $N=4$.

Step 1 · Full DFT ($w=e^{-j\pi/2}=-j$)
$S[0]=8$, $S[1]=1+2(-j)+3(-1)+2(j)=-2$ $S[2]=1-2+3-2=0$, $S[3]=-2$
Step 2 · Check $S[N-k]=S[k]^*$
$S[3]=S[4-1]=S[-1]=-2=(-2)^*=S[1]^*\;\checkmark$ $S[2]=S[2]=0=0^*\;\checkmark$
Consequence

Only $S[0],S[1],S[2]$ carry independent information (i.e., half the spectrum suffices for real signals).

Solved Example B · §3.3.1

Identify $s[n]$ from its
amplitude spectrum.

$N=8$; $|S[2]|=|S[6]|=4$, all other $|S[k]|=0$.

Step 1 · Conjugate-symmetric pair at $k=2,6$
Corresponds to a real sinusoid at Fourier frequency $k=2$.
Step 2 · Recover amplitude
$|S[2]|=4=NA/2=4A\Rightarrow A=1$. $\angle S[2]=0\Rightarrow\phi=0$.
Result
$$s[n]=\cos\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi\cdot2}{8}n\right)=\cos\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}n\right)$$
