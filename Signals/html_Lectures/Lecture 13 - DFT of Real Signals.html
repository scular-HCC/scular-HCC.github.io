RecapFrom Lecture 12
Where we are

Analysis and synthesis, one more time.

Fourier sinusoids
$v^{(k)}[n]=e^{j2\pi kn/N}=e^{j\Omega_k n}$, with $\Omega_k=k\,\tfrac{2\pi}{N}$ rad/sample. They are the columns of $V$.
Analysis and synthesis
$$S=V^{H}s,\qquad s=\tfrac1N\,V S$$ $$S[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}s[n]\,e^{-j\Omega_k n}$$

$S[k]$ measures how much of sinusoid $k$ the signal contains.

Today

Given $S$, what does $s$ look like? Given a real $s$, what must $S$ look like? We answer with examples first, then a proof.

Notation reminder
$\Omega$ (rad/sample) is discrete-time frequency; $\omega$ (rad/s) is continuous-time frequency. This lecture writes the pair as $s\leftrightarrow S$; Lectures 14 and 15 use $x\leftrightarrow X$.
Notation: three symbols
$V^{H}$ is the conjugate transpose: transpose $V$, then conjugate each entry, so $V^{H}[k,n]=e^{-j\Omega_k n}$. $e_k$ is the vector with a $1$ in position $k$ and $0$ elsewhere (for $N=4$, $e_2=(0,0,1,0)^{\top}$). For $z=1+j$: $|z|=\sqrt2$ (length), $\angle z=\pi/4$ (angle).

Worked ExampleSingle-Bin Inverses · N=8
Single bins

Inverse DFT of one nonzero bin

Synthesis formula
$$s=\tfrac1N V S\;\Longrightarrow\; s[n]=\tfrac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}S[k]\,e^{j\Omega_k n},\qquad \Omega_k=k\tfrac{2\pi}{N}$$

From $S=V^Hs$ and $V^HV=NI$. With a single nonzero bin $S=e_k$ only the term $k$ survives.

Bin 4, the Nyquist bin
$$\Omega_4=4\tfrac{2\pi}{8}=\pi\;\Rightarrow\; s[n]=\tfrac18e^{j\pi n}=\tfrac{(-1)^{n}}{8}$$ $$s = \tfrac{1}{8}(1,-1,1,-1,1,-1,1,-1)^{\top}$$
Bin 3, a complex sinusoid
$$\Omega_3=3\tfrac{2\pi}{8}=\tfrac{3\pi}{4}\;\Rightarrow\; s[n]=\tfrac18e^{j(3\pi/4)n}$$

Complex-valued, so a real cosine needs two symmetric bins.

Worked ExampleSymmetric Pair → Real Cosine
Two symmetric bins

Bins $k$ and $N-k$ give a real cosine.

Spectrum
$$S = (0,0,0,1,0,1,0,0)^{\top},\quad s=\tfrac18\big(v^{(3)}+v^{(5)}\big)$$

$k=3$ and $k=5=N-3$.

Conjugate columns
$$e^{j5\pi n/4}=e^{j2\pi n}e^{-j3\pi n/4}=e^{-j3\pi n/4}$$

since $e^{j2\pi n}=1$ for integer $n$.

ConceptLinearity (DFT 1)
DFT 1 · Linearity

Decompose, transform, recombine.

$$\alpha x + \beta y \,\leftrightarrow\, \alpha X + \beta Y$$

Analysis and synthesis are matrix-vector products, so superposition is automatic.

Combine known DFT pairs to invert complicated spectra.

Example, N = 8: S = (1,0,0,3,-2,3,0,0)
$$S[0]=1\Rightarrow\tfrac18,\qquad S[3]=S[5]=3\Rightarrow\tfrac38\big(e^{j3\pi n/4}+e^{-j3\pi n/4}\big)=\tfrac34\cos\tfrac{3\pi n}{4},\qquad S[4]=-2\Rightarrow-\tfrac28(-1)^n$$ $$s[n] = \tfrac{1}{8} \,+\, \tfrac{3}{4}\cos\big(\tfrac{3\pi n}{4}\big) \,-\, \tfrac{1}{4}(-1)^{n},\qquad s[0]=\tfrac18+\tfrac34-\tfrac14=\tfrac58$$

ConceptCircular Conjugate Symmetry (DFT 2)
DFT 2 · Symmetry for real signals

Real $s$ ⇒ conjugate symmetric $S$.

$$S[N-k] \,=\, S^{*}[k], $$ $$\;\; k = 1,\ldots,N-1$$
$$S[0],\, S[N/2] \in \mathbb{R}$$

$v^{(k)}$ and $v^{(N-k)}$ are complex conjugates — so the inner products with a real $s$ are too.

Indices $k$ and $N-k$ sit symmetrically across $k = 0$ when the bins are laid out around the unit circle.

ConceptENES-222 · L13
ConceptProof of DFT 2
Why real s forces symmetry

Proof, step by step.

Columns k and N-k are conjugates
$$V_{n,N-k}=e^{j2\pi n}e^{-j2\pi kn/N}=\overline{V_{nk}}$$
Analysis sums
$$\begin{aligned}S[k]&=\sum_{n}e^{-j2\pi kn/N}s[n]\\ S[N-k]&=\sum_{n}e^{+j2\pi kn/N}s[n]\end{aligned}$$
Conjugate S[k]; s[n] is real
$$\overline{S[k]}=\sum_{n}e^{+j2\pi kn/N}s[n]=S[N-k]$$
Special bins (k = 0 and N/2)
$$S[0]=\sum_n s[n],\;\; S[\tfrac N2]=\sum_n(-1)^ns[n]$$

Both are real sums of real numbers.

If the pair is not conjugate
$S[3]=3$, $S[5]=-1$ ($N=8$): $s[n]=\tfrac18(3e^{j3\pi n/4}-e^{-j3\pi n/4})$ has imaginary part $\tfrac12\sin\tfrac{3\pi n}{4}\neq0$ (e.g. $0.354$ at $n=1$), so $s$ is not real.

Worked ExampleDFT by hand: bins 0, 4, 1
Worked example with N = 8

Compute a real signal's DFT by hand.

Given
$$s=(2,\,1,\,-1,\,3,\,0,\,-2,\,1,\,2)^{\top}$$

$S[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{7}s[n]\,e^{-j\Omega_k n}$, $\;\Omega_k=k\pi/4$.

Bin 0: the sum
$$S[0]=2+1-1+3+0-2+1+2=6$$
Bin 4: the alternating sum
$e^{-j\pi n}=(-1)^{n}$, so $$S[4]=2-1-1-3+0+2+1-2=-2$$
Bin 1: cosine and sine values
$e^{-j\pi n/4}=\cos\tfrac{\pi n}{4}-j\sin\tfrac{\pi n}{4}$, for $n=0,\dots,7$: $$\begin{aligned}\cos&:\;1,\,\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\,0,\,-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\,-1,\,-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\,0,\,\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\\\sin&:\;0,\,\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\,1,\,\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\,0,\,-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},\,-1,\,-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\end{aligned}$$
Real part
$\sum_n s[n]\cos\tfrac{\pi n}{4}$: $$\begin{aligned}&2(1)+1\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}+(-1)0+3(-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2})+0\\&\;+(-2)(-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2})+1(0)+2\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\\&=2+\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}(1-3+2+2)=2+\sqrt2\end{aligned}$$
Imaginary part
$\sum_n s[n]\sin\tfrac{\pi n}{4}=\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}(1+3+2-2)-1-1=2\sqrt2-2$, so $$S[1]=(2+\sqrt2)-j(2\sqrt2-2)\approx3.414-0.828j$$

Worked ExampleDFT by hand (cont.): the rest
Worked example with N = 8 (cont.)
Bin 2: powers of minus j
$e^{-j\pi n/2}=(-j)^{n}=1,-j,-1,j,1,-j,-1,j$. $$\begin{aligned}S[2]&=2+(-j)-1(-1)+3j+0\\&\quad-2(-j)+1(-1)+2j\\&=(2+1-1)+j(-1+3+2+2)\\&=2+6j\end{aligned}$$
Bin 3: the eight factors
With $c=\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$, the factors $e^{-j3\pi n/4}$ are $1,\,-c-jc,\,j,\,c-jc,\,-1,\,c+jc,\,-j,\,-c+jc$. $$\begin{aligned}\mathrm{Re}&=2-c+3c-2c-2c=2-\sqrt2\\\mathrm{Im}&=-c-1-3c-2c-1+2c=-2-2\sqrt2\end{aligned}$$

$S[3]=(2-\sqrt2)-j(2+2\sqrt2)\approx0.586-4.828j$

Bins 5, 6, 7 from DFT 2
$S[8-k]=\overline{S[k]}$: $$S[5]=\overline{S[3]},\;\;S[6]=\overline{S[2]}=2-6j,\;\;S[7]=\overline{S[1]}$$
All eight bins
$$\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}k&S[k]&|S[k]|&\angle S[k]\ \text{(rad)}\\\hline0&6&6&0\\1&3.414-0.828j&3.513&-0.238\\2&2+6j&6.325&1.249\\3&0.586-4.828j&4.864&-1.450\\4&-2&2&\pi\\5&0.586+4.828j&4.864&1.450\\6&2-6j&6.325&-1.249\\7&3.414+0.828j&3.513&0.238\end{array}$$
Check by synthesis
$s[0]=\tfrac18\sum_k S[k]$: imaginary parts cancel in pairs, real parts give $$6-2+2(2+\sqrt2)+4+2(2-\sqrt2)=16$$ so $s[0]=\tfrac{16}{8}=2$ ✓

Pair $k$ with $N - k$.

ApplicationCounting numbers and reading bins
Why only half the spectrum matters

Real signals need half the bins.

Count the real numbers, N = 8
A real $s$ has $8$ real numbers. Its spectrum: $S[0]$ and $S[4]$ are real ($2$ numbers); $S[1],S[2],S[3]$ are complex ($3\times2=6$ numbers); $S[5],S[6],S[7]$ add nothing new. $$2+6=8\;\checkmark$$

In general: $N$ even gives $2+2(\tfrac N2-1)=N$; $N$ odd gives $1+2\cdot\tfrac{N-1}{2}=N$.

From bins to hertz
Sampling at $f_s$ Hz turns a tone of $f$ Hz into $\Omega=2\pi f/f_s$ rad/sample. Bin $k$ has $\Omega_k=2\pi k/N$, so $$f_k=\frac{k}{N}\,f_s\ \text{Hz},\qquad \omega_k=2\pi f_k\ \text{rad/s}$$
Example with 8 kHz sampling and N = 8
$f_k=1000\,k$ Hz: bins $0,1,2,3,4$ are $0,\,1000,\,2000,\,3000,\,4000$ Hz. Bin $4$ is the Nyquist frequency $f_s/2$. Bins $5,6,7$ are the mirror images of $3,2,1$ (negative frequencies $-3000,-2000,-1000$ Hz).

For the signal above the largest bar, $|S[2]|=6.32$, sits at $2000$ Hz.

ConceptReal Cosine Expansion
Real signal as a sum of cosines

Polar form + conjugate pairs
give the real series.

$$s[n] \,=\, \tfrac{1}{N}\,S[0] \;+\; \tfrac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N/2-1} |S[k]|\,\cos\!\Big(\tfrac{2\pi k n}{N} + \angle S[k]\Big) \;+\; \tfrac{1}{N}\,S[N/2]\,(-1)^{n}$$

The last term appears only when $N$ is even (for odd $N$ the sum runs to $(N-1)/2$). Frequencies lie in $[0,\pi]$, the effective discrete-time range from L04.

ConceptENES-222 · L13
ConceptDeriving the Cosine Series
Pair up bins k and N-k

Each pair becomes one cosine.

Write S[k] in polar form
$$S[k]=|S[k]|e^{j\angle S[k]},\qquad S[N-k]=\overline{S[k]}=|S[k]|e^{-j\angle S[k]}$$
Add the two synthesis terms; the second exponent is the conjugate of the first
$$\tfrac1N\Big(S[k]e^{j2\pi kn/N}+S[N-k]e^{-j2\pi kn/N}\Big)=\tfrac{|S[k]|}{N}\Big(e^{j(\frac{2\pi kn}{N}+\angle S[k])}+e^{-j(\frac{2\pi kn}{N}+\angle S[k])}\Big)=\tfrac{2}{N}|S[k]|\cos\!\big(\tfrac{2\pi kn}{N}+\angle S[k]\big)$$

$S[N-k]e^{j2\pi(N-k)n/N}=\overline{S[k]}\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$. The bins $k=0$ and $k=N/2$ pair with themselves and give $\tfrac1NS[0]$ and $\tfrac1NS[N/2](-1)^n$.

SolutionSymmetry and Polar Form
Solution, part 1
Conjugate symmetry S[8-k] = S*[k]
$$S[7]=1-2j=\overline{1+2j}=\overline{S[1]}$$ $$S[6]=4j=\overline{-4j}=\overline{S[2]}$$ $$S[5]=3+j=\overline{3-j}=\overline{S[3]}\;\checkmark$$
Real bins
$$S[0]=1,\qquad S[4]=-2$$
Polar form: modulus and angle
$$|S[1]|=\sqrt{1^2+2^2}=\sqrt5$$ $$\angle S[1]=\arctan\tfrac21=1.1071$$ $$|S[2]|=\sqrt{0^2+(-4)^2}=4$$ $$\angle S[2]=-\tfrac{\pi}{2}$$ $$|S[3]|=\sqrt{3^2+(-1)^2}=\sqrt{10}$$ $$\angle S[3]=\arctan\tfrac{-1}{3}=-0.3218$$

Angles in radians. $S[1]$, $S[3]$ lie in quadrants I and IV, so $\arctan$ is correct; $S[2]=-4j$ points down the imaginary axis.

SolutionAssemble the Cosines
Solution, part 2
Coefficients 2|S[k]|/8 and frequencies 2πk/8
$$k=1:\;\tfrac{2\sqrt5}{8}=\tfrac{\sqrt5}{4},\quad \tfrac{2\pi}{8}=\tfrac{\pi}{4}$$ $$k=2:\;\tfrac{2\cdot4}{8}=1,\quad \tfrac{4\pi}{8}=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$$ $$k=3:\;\tfrac{2\sqrt{10}}{8}=\tfrac{\sqrt{10}}{4},\quad \tfrac{6\pi}{8}=\tfrac{3\pi}{4}$$
DC and Nyquist
$$\tfrac{1}{8}S[0]=\tfrac{1}{8},\;\; \tfrac{1}{8}S[4](-1)^{n} = -\tfrac{1}{4}(-1)^{n}$$
Final answer
$$s[n] = \tfrac{1}{8} + \tfrac{\sqrt{5}}{4}\cos\!\big(\tfrac{\pi n}{4} + 1.1071\big)$$ $$\; + \cos\!\big(\tfrac{\pi n}{2} - \tfrac{\pi}{2}\big)$$ $$\; + \tfrac{\sqrt{10}}{4}\cos\!\big(\tfrac{3\pi n}{4} - 0.3218\big)$$ $$\; - \tfrac{1}{4}(-1)^{n}$$

Check $n=0$: $\cos1.1071=\tfrac1{\sqrt5}$, $\cos0.3218=\tfrac3{\sqrt{10}}$, so $s[0]=\tfrac18+\tfrac14+0+\tfrac34-\tfrac14=0.875$. Check $n=4$: $\tfrac18-\tfrac14+0-\tfrac34-\tfrac14=-1.125$. Both match the inverse DFT.

Common mistakesFive things that go wrong
Common mistakes

Check these before you hand in.

  1. $S[0]$ is the sum, not the mean. The mean of $s$ is $S[0]/N$. In the example $S[0]=6$ but the mean is $6/8=0.75$.
  2. Cosine amplitude is $2|S[k]|/N$, not $|S[k]|/N$: the factor 2 comes from the pair $k$ and $N-k$. Only the DC and Nyquist terms carry $1/N$.
  3. The $(-1)^{n}$ term exists only for even $N$. For odd $N$ there is no bin $N/2$ and the sum runs to $(N-1)/2$.
  4. $S[N-k]$ must equal $\overline{S[k]}$. Take $S[3]=3$, $S[5]=-1$: the inverse DFT has imaginary part $\tfrac12\sin\tfrac{3\pi n}{4}\neq0$. A real signal needs the conjugate partner.
  5. Use the correct quadrant for the angle. $\arctan(\mathrm{Im}/\mathrm{Re})$ is right only if $\mathrm{Re}>0$. For $-1+j$ the angle is $3\pi/4$, not $-\pi/4$.

Solved ExampleTwo Fully-Solved Examples
Solved Example A · conjugate symmetry check

Verify conjugate symmetry for $N=4$.

$\mathbf{s}=(1,2,3,2)^\top$. DFT: $S[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{3}s[n]W^{kn}$ with $W=e^{-j2\pi/4}=-j$, so $W^2=-1$, $W^3=j$.

Step 1 · Full DFT
$$S[0]=1+2+3+2=8$$ $$S[1]=1+2(-j)+3(-1)+2(j)=-2$$ $$S[2]=1+2(-1)+3(1)+2(-1)=0$$ $$S[3]=1+2(j)+3(-1)+2(-j)=-2$$
Step 2 · Check S[N-k] = S[k]*
$$S[3]=-2=\overline{-2}=\overline{S[1]}\;\checkmark,\qquad S[2]=0=\overline{0}\;\checkmark$$

$S[0]=8$ is also real (sum of samples).

Consequence

Only $S[0],S[1],S[2]$ carry independent information: half the spectrum suffices for real signals.

Solved Example B · amplitude spectrum

Identify $s[n]$ from its amplitude spectrum.

$N=8$; $|S[2]|=|S[6]|=4$, all other $|S[k]|=0$; assume $\angle S[2]=0$ (the amplitude alone does not fix the phase).

Step 1 · Conjugate pair
$$S[2]=4,\quad S[6]=\overline{S[2]}=4$$

A real sinusoid at $\Omega_2=2\pi\cdot2/8=\pi/2$.

Step 2 · Amplitude and phase

For $s[n]=A\cos(\Omega_2n+\phi)$ the series gives $|S[2]|=NA/2$:

$$4=\tfrac{8A}{2}=4A\Rightarrow A=1,\qquad \phi=\angle S[2]=0$$
Result
$$s[n]=\cos\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi\cdot2}{8}n\right)=\cos\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}n\right)$$

Check: $\tfrac28\cdot4=1$ matches the series coefficient $\tfrac2N|S[2]|$.

SolutionA sine from imaginary bins
Solution
(a) Symmetry
$\overline{S[1]}=\overline{-4j}=4j=S[7]$ ✓. The other bins are $0$; bins $0$ and $4$ are real.
(b) Polar form
$|S[1]|=\sqrt{0^{2}+(-4)^{2}}=4$. The point $-4j$ lies on the negative imaginary axis, so $\angle S[1]=-\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ ($=-90^{\circ}$).
(c) Series
$$s[n]=\tfrac{2}{8}\cdot4\cos\!\big(\tfrac{\pi n}{4}-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\big)=\cos\!\big(\tfrac{\pi n}{4}-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\big)$$ Since $\cos(\theta-\tfrac\pi2)=\sin\theta$: $\;s[n]=\sin\tfrac{\pi n}{4}$.

($e^{j7\pi/2}=e^{-j\pi/2}=-j$.) Values: $0,\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},1,\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},0,-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2},-1,-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$.
