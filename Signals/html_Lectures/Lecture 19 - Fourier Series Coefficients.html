and read off $S_k$.

  1. Define the inner product $\langle f, g\rangle = \tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0} f^{*}(t) g(t)\,dt$.

RecapFrom the DFT to the Fourier Series
Recap · what carries over from the DFT

Same plan: project onto each basis function.

DFT · finite vectors
$$v^{(k)}[n]=e^{j2\pi kn/N},\quad n=0,\dots,N-1$$ $$\langle v^{(k)},v^{(\ell)}\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\big(v^{(k)}[n]\big)^{*}v^{(\ell)}[n]=N\,\delta_{k\ell}$$ $$S[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}s[n]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$$ $$s[n]=\tfrac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}S[k]\,e^{j2\pi kn/N}$$
Fourier series · periodic signals
$$v^{(k)}(t)=e^{jk\omega_0 t},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},\ \omega_0=\tfrac{2\pi}{T_0}\ \text{rad/s}$$ $$\langle v^{(k)},v^{(\ell)}\rangle=\tfrac1{T_0}\int_{T_0}\big(v^{(k)}(t)\big)^{*}v^{(\ell)}(t)\,dt=\delta_{k\ell}$$ $$S_k=\tfrac1{T_0}\int_{T_0}s(t)\,e^{-jk\omega_0 t}\,dt$$ $$s(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}S_k\,e^{jk\omega_0 t}$$

Sum over $n$ becomes integral over $t$; $1/T_0$ plays the role of $1/N$. Today: derive the analysis line on the right.

RecapENES-222 · L19
ConceptContinuous-Time Inner Product
Inner product

Sum becomes integral —
average over one period.

$$\langle f, g\rangle \,\stackrel{\text{def}}{=}\, \tfrac{1}{T_0}\,\int_{T_0} f^{*}(t)\, g(t)\,dt$$

Same axioms as the vector version: conjugate symmetric, sesquilinear, $\langle f, f\rangle \geq 0$.

Integral may be taken over any interval of length $T_0$ — the integrand is periodic.

ConceptENES-222 · L19
ConceptOrthonormal Harmonics
Orthonormality

The harmonics are pairwise orthonormal.

Here $v^{(k)}(t) = e^{jk\omega_0 t}$ with $\omega_0 = 2\pi/T_0$, and $\delta_{k\ell}$ is the Kronecker delta ($1$ if $k=\ell$, else $0$). Conjugating, $\big(v^{(k)}(t)\big)^{*} = e^{-jk\omega_0 t}$, so $\langle v^{(k)}, v^{(\ell)}\rangle$ equals $$\langle v^{(k)}, v^{(\ell)}\rangle \,=\, \tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0} e^{-jk\omega_0 t}\,e^{j\ell\omega_0 t}\,dt \,=\, \tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0} e^{j(\ell-k)\omega_0 t}\,dt .$$

Case k equals l
$$\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{0}^{T_0} e^{j\cdot 0}\,dt = \tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_0^{T_0} 1\,dt = \tfrac{T_0}{T_0} = 1$$
Case k differs from l

Let $m = \ell - k \neq 0$ (an integer). Then

$$\tfrac{1}{T_0}\Big[\tfrac{e^{jm\omega_0 t}}{jm\omega_0}\Big]_0^{T_0} = \tfrac{e^{j2\pi m}-1}{j m\, \omega_0 T_0} = \tfrac{1-1}{j2\pi m} = 0$$

because $\omega_0 T_0 = 2\pi$ and $e^{j2\pi m} = \cos 2\pi m + j\sin 2\pi m = 1$ (Euler's formula).

ConceptAnalysis Equation
Analysis equation

Project $s$ onto each harmonic.

Start from synthesis
$$s(t) = \sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty} S_\ell\, v^{(\ell)}(t)$$
Take inner product with v-k
$$\langle v^{(k)}, s\rangle = \sum_{\ell} S_\ell \langle v^{(k)}, v^{(\ell)}\rangle$$

(the inner product is linear in its second argument, and the integral passes through the sum)

Use orthonormality
$$\sum_{\ell} S_\ell\,\delta_{k\ell} = S_k$$

every term with $\ell \neq k$ is multiplied by $0$; only $\ell=k$ survives.

$$S_k \,=\, \langle v^{(k)}, s\rangle \,=\, \tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0} s(t)\, e^{-j k \omega_0 t}\,dt$$

Any interval of length $T_0$ works — pick the one that makes the integral easiest.

Worked ExampleCosine and Sine by the Analysis Integral
Worked example · a first integral

Cosine by the integral.

$x(t)=\cos(\omega_0 t)$ with period $T_0=2\pi/\omega_0$. Use $X_k=\tfrac1{T_0}\int_{T_0}x(t)e^{-jk\omega_0 t}dt$.

Step 1 · Euler on the cosine
$$\cos(\omega_0 t)=\tfrac12 e^{j\omega_0 t}+\tfrac12 e^{-j\omega_0 t}$$
Step 2 · Put it in the integral
$$X_k=\tfrac12\Big[\tfrac1{T_0}\!\int_{T_0}\!e^{-j(k-1)\omega_0 t}dt+\tfrac1{T_0}\!\int_{T_0}\!e^{-j(k+1)\omega_0 t}dt\Big]$$
Step 3 · Whole periods

$\tfrac1{T_0}\int_{T_0}e^{jm\omega_0 t}dt=\delta_{0m}$ (orthonormality with $v^{(0)}=1$): the first term survives only for $k=1$, the second only for $k=-1$.

$$X_{\pm1}=\tfrac12,\qquad X_k=0\ \text{otherwise}$$
Same steps

Sine by the integral.

$x(t)=\sin(\omega_0 t)$, same $T_0$ and $\omega_0$.

Step 1 · Euler on the sine
$$\sin(\omega_0 t)=\tfrac1{2j}\big(e^{j\omega_0 t}-e^{-j\omega_0 t}\big)$$
Step 2 · Orthonormality picks the terms
$$X_k=\tfrac1{2j}\big(\delta_{k1}-\delta_{k,-1}\big)$$ $$X_1=\tfrac1{2j}=-\tfrac j2,\qquad X_{-1}=-\tfrac1{2j}=+\tfrac j2$$
Check

$1/j=-j$. Real signal, so $X_{-1}=X_1^{*}$ ✓. And $\sin\omega_0t=\cos(\omega_0t-\tfrac\pi2)$ gives $X_1=\tfrac12e^{-j\pi/2}=-\tfrac j2$ ✓.

Worked ExampleRectangular Pulse Train
Worked example

Pulse train of duty $\alpha$,
height $1$.

Let $0<\alpha<1$. Over the period $-T_0/2 \le t < T_0/2$ the signal is $s(t)=1$ for $|t| < \alpha T_0/2$ (a pulse of width $\alpha T_0$) and $0$ elsewhere; it then repeats with period $T_0$, $\omega_0 = 2\pi/T_0$.

Integrate over $[-T_0/2,\,T_0/2]$; $s=0$ outside the pulse, so only the pulse interval contributes.

DC coefficient, k equals 0
$$\begin{aligned}S_0 &= \tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{-\alpha T_0/2}^{\alpha T_0/2} 1\,dt\\ &= \tfrac{1}{T_0}\Big(\tfrac{\alpha T_0}{2} - \big(-\tfrac{\alpha T_0}{2}\big)\Big) = \tfrac{\alpha T_0}{T_0} = \alpha\end{aligned}$$

the average value of $s$: height $1$ for a fraction $\alpha$ of the period.

General k, set up the integral
$$S_k = \tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{-\alpha T_0/2}^{\alpha T_0/2} e^{-j k \omega_0 t}\,dt$$

for $k \neq 0$ the antiderivative of $e^{-jk\omega_0 t}$ is $\dfrac{e^{-jk\omega_0 t}}{-jk\omega_0}$.

Worked ExampleENES-222 · L19
Worked ExampleRectangular Pulse Train, k nonzero
Worked example, continued

Evaluate and simplify
for $k \neq 0$.

Substitute the limits
$$S_k = \tfrac{1}{T_0}\cdot\tfrac{e^{-jk\omega_0 \alpha T_0/2} - e^{+jk\omega_0 \alpha T_0/2}}{-jk\omega_0}$$
Use omega-0 times T-0 equals 2 pi

Let $\theta = k\alpha\pi$. Then $k\omega_0\alpha T_0/2 = k\alpha\pi=\theta$ and $T_0\,k\omega_0 = 2\pi k$:

$$S_k = \tfrac{e^{-j\theta} - e^{j\theta}}{-j\,2\pi k}$$
Apply Euler's identity

$e^{j\theta}-e^{-j\theta} = 2j\sin\theta$, so $e^{-j\theta}-e^{j\theta} = -2j\sin\theta$:

$$S_k = \tfrac{-2j\sin\theta}{-j\,2\pi k} = \tfrac{\sin(k\alpha\pi)}{k\pi}$$
Check

As $k\to 0$, $\sin(k\alpha\pi)\approx k\alpha\pi$, so $S_k \to \alpha = S_0$ — the general formula contains the DC case. For $\alpha=\tfrac12$, $k=1$: $S_1 = \sin(\pi/2)/\pi = 1/\pi \approx 0.318$.

Note

$S_{-k} = \sin(-k\alpha\pi)/(-k\pi) = S_k$: real and even in $k$, because $s(t)$ is real and even.

Worked ExampleENES-222 · L19
VisualSinc Envelope
Reading the coefficients

$S_k = \alpha\,\mathrm{sinc}(k\alpha)$

With the normalized $\mathrm{sinc}(x)=\sin(\pi x)/(\pi x)$: $\alpha\,\mathrm{sinc}(k\alpha) = \dfrac{\sin(\pi k\alpha)}{\pi k}$, exactly the result just derived.

The first zero is at $k = 1/\alpha$ — the narrower the pulse, the wider the spectrum. As $\alpha \to 1$ the pulse fills the period and only $S_0$ survives ($\sin k\pi = 0$ for integer $k$).

Numbers from the formula

$\alpha=\tfrac12$: $S_0=0.5$, $S_{\pm1}=\tfrac{1}{\pi}=0.318$, $S_{\pm2}=0$, $S_{\pm3}=-\tfrac{1}{3\pi}=-0.106$, $S_{\pm5}=\tfrac{1}{5\pi}=0.064$.

$\alpha=\tfrac14$: $S_0=0.25$, $S_{\pm1}=\tfrac{\sin(\pi/4)}{\pi}=0.225$, $S_{\pm2}=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}=0.159$, $S_{\pm3}=\tfrac{\sin(3\pi/4)}{3\pi}=0.075$, $S_{\pm4}=0$.

A negative real $S_k$ is a magnitude with phase $\pi$: $S_3=-0.106=0.106\,e^{j\pi}$ for $\alpha=\tfrac12$.

Worked ExampleDo the Coefficients Add Back Up?
Check by synthesis

Add the harmonics up.

Step 1 · Real, even s: pair k with minus k

$s(t)=S_0+2\sum_{k\ge1}S_k\cos(k\omega_0 t)$; at $t=0$ every cosine is $1$. For $\alpha=\tfrac12$: $S_1=0.3183$, $S_3=-0.1061$, $S_5=0.0637$, $S_7=-0.0455$, $S_9=0.0354$, even $k$: $0$.

Step 2 · Running total, start at S-0 = 0.5
$$\begin{aligned}K=1&:\ 0.5+2(0.3183)=1.1366\\ K=3&:\ 1.1366+2(-0.1061)=0.9244\\ K=5&:\ 0.9244+2(0.0637)=1.0517\\ K=7&:\ 1.0517+2(-0.0455)=0.9608\\ K=9&:\ 0.9608+2(0.0354)=1.0315\end{aligned}$$
Green $K=1$ (peak $1.14$ at the star), orange $K=3$ (dips to $0.92$), red $K=9$ (close to $1$). All curves pass through $0.5$ at the squares (the jumps).

The total swings around $s(0)=1$ and the swings shrink. At the jump $t=T_0/4$, $\cos(k\pi/2)=0$ for odd $k$, so every partial sum equals $S_0=0.5$.

Signal

SolutionDecomposition and DC
Solution · part 1

A constant plus two pulse trains.

Decomposition

Let $A(t)=1$ for $|t|<2$ (width $4$, duty $\alpha_A = 4/6 = 2/3$) and $B(t)=1$ for $|t|<1$ (width $2$, duty $\alpha_B=2/6=1/3$), both $0$ elsewhere in the period. Claim: $x = 2 + A + 2B$ (the constant $2$ is the level outside the pulses).

Check: $|t|<1$: $2+1+2=5$ ✓; $1\le|t|<2$: $2+1+0=3$ ✓; $2\le|t|<3$: $2$ ✓.

DC coefficient from the pulse trains
$$\begin{aligned}X_0 &= 2 + \alpha_A + 2\alpha_B = 2 + \tfrac{2}{3} + 2\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}\\ &= 2+\tfrac{2}{3}+\tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{10}{3}\end{aligned}$$
Independent check: average of x
$$X_0 = \tfrac{1}{6}\big(5\cdot 2 + 3\cdot 2 + 2\cdot 2\big) = \tfrac{20}{6} = \tfrac{10}{3}$$

widths: $|t|<1$ has length $2$; $1\le|t|<2$ has total length $2$ (both sides); $2\le|t|<3$ has total length $2$.

SolutionCoefficients for k nonzero
Solution · part 2

Linearity gives $X_k$.

Pulse-train formula for each part
$$A_k = \tfrac{\sin(k\alpha_A\pi)}{k\pi} = \tfrac{\sin(2k\pi/3)}{k\pi},\qquad B_k = \tfrac{\sin(k\pi/3)}{k\pi}$$
Linearity of the analysis integral
$$X_k = \tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}(2+A+2B)e^{-jk\omega_0 t}dt = 0 + A_k + 2B_k$$

the constant $2$ gives $\tfrac{2}{T_0}\int_{T_0}e^{-jk\omega_0 t}dt=0$ for $k\neq0$ (whole periods).

$$X_k = \tfrac{\sin(2k\pi/3)}{k\pi} + \tfrac{2\sin(k\pi/3)}{k\pi},\quad k\neq 0$$
Numerical sanity check

$k=1$: $\tfrac{\sqrt3/2+2(\sqrt3/2)}{\pi} = \tfrac{3\sqrt3}{2\pi} \approx 0.827$

$k=2$: $\tfrac{\sin(4\pi/3)+2\sin(2\pi/3)}{2\pi} = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{4\pi}\approx 0.138$

$k=3$: $\sin 2\pi=\sin\pi=0$, so $X_3=0$.

These match direct numerical integration of the analysis equation.

Takeaway

Never integrate twice if a known pair will do. $X_{-k}=X_k$ since $x$ is real and even.

ConceptParseval: Why Power Adds Up
Derivation

Power is the sum of $|X_k|^2$.

$$P=\tfrac1{T_0}\int_{T_0}|x(t)|^2dt=\langle x,x\rangle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}|X_k|^2$$
Step 1 · Write x twice by synthesis
$$\langle x,x\rangle=\Big\langle\sum_k X_k v^{(k)},\ \sum_\ell X_\ell v^{(\ell)}\Big\rangle$$
Step 2 · Pull out the constants

The first slot conjugates, the second is linear:

$$=\sum_k\sum_\ell X_k^{*}X_\ell\,\langle v^{(k)},v^{(\ell)}\rangle$$
Step 3 · Orthonormality

$\langle v^{(k)},v^{(\ell)}\rangle=\delta_{k\ell}$ kills every term with $\ell\neq k$:

$$=\sum_k X_k^{*}X_k=\sum_k|X_k|^2$$
Numerical check · half-duty pulse train

Average of $s^2$ is $\tfrac12$. Sum: $S_0^2+2(S_1^2+S_3^2+S_5^2)=0.25+2(0.1013+0.0113+0.0041)=0.4833$; adding $k=7,9$ gives $0.4899$, creeping up to $0.5$.

Solved ExampleParseval Power
Solved Example

Power via Parseval's theorem.

Signal: $x(t)=3\cos(\omega_0 t)+4\sin(2\omega_0 t)$, fundamental frequency $\omega_0$.

Parseval's theorem

Average power $P=\tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0}|x(t)|^2dt=\sum_k|X_k|^2$. Each harmonic contributes independently, and $|X_{-k}|=|X_k|$ for real $x$.

Check in the time domain

$\overline{\cos^2}=\overline{\sin^2}=\tfrac12$ and the cross term averages to $0$, so $P=9\cdot\tfrac12+16\cdot\tfrac12=12.5$ ✓

Step 1 · FS coefficients via Euler
$$3\cos(\omega_0 t)=\tfrac32 e^{j\omega_0 t}+\tfrac32 e^{-j\omega_0 t}$$ $$\begin{aligned}4\sin(2\omega_0 t)&=\tfrac{4}{2j}\big(e^{j2\omega_0 t}-e^{-j2\omega_0 t}\big)\\&=-2j\,e^{j2\omega_0 t}+2j\,e^{-j2\omega_0 t}\end{aligned}$$

using $1/j=-j$. Reading off the coefficient of $e^{jk\omega_0 t}$: $X_{\pm1}=3/2$, $X_2=-2j$, $X_{-2}=2j$, all others $0$.

Step 2 · Parseval
$$|X_{\pm1}|^2=\tfrac94,\quad |X_{\pm2}|^2=|\mp 2j|^2=4$$ $$P=\sum_k|X_k|^2=2\cdot\tfrac94+2\cdot 4=\tfrac{9}{2}+8=12.5$$

SolutionNot Centered: the Integral
Solution · part 1

Integrate over $[0,2]$.

DC coefficient
$$X_0=\tfrac14\int_0^2 1\,dt=\tfrac24=\tfrac12$$
Set up, k nonzero

$x=0$ on $[2,4)$, so only $[0,2]$ contributes:

$$X_k=\tfrac14\int_0^2 e^{-jk\pi t/2}\,dt$$
Antiderivative and limits
$$\begin{aligned}X_k&=\tfrac14\Big[\tfrac{e^{-jk\pi t/2}}{-jk\pi/2}\Big]_0^2=\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac{e^{-jk\pi}-1}{-jk\pi/2}\\&=\tfrac{1-e^{-jk\pi}}{j\,2\pi k}\end{aligned}$$
Use e to the minus j k pi

$e^{-jk\pi}=\cos k\pi-j\sin k\pi=(-1)^k$, so

$$X_k=\tfrac{1-(-1)^k}{j\,2\pi k}$$
Even k and odd k

$k$ even, $k\ne0$: $1-1=0$, so $X_k=0$.

$k$ odd: $1-(-1)=2$, so $X_k=\tfrac{2}{j2\pi k}=\tfrac1{j\pi k}=-\tfrac{j}{\pi k}$.

Values
$$X_1=-\tfrac j\pi=-0.318j,\quad X_{-1}=+0.318j$$ $$X_2=0,\quad X_3=-\tfrac{j}{3\pi}=-0.106j$$ $$X_5=-\tfrac{j}{5\pi}=-0.064j$$

SolutionNot Centered: Checks
Solution · part 2

Two checks.

Check 1 · Conjugate symmetry, not evenness

$x$ is real, so $X_{-k}=X_k^{*}$: $X_{-1}=(-0.318j)^{*}=+0.318j$ ✓. It is not even in $t$, so $X_{-k}\neq X_k$; the coefficients are purely imaginary (odd $k$) instead of real.

Check 2 · A shifted centered pulse

Preview of L20: $x(t)=s(t-1)$, where $s$ is the centered pulse of width $2$ (duty $\tfrac12$) with $S_1=\tfrac1\pi$, $S_3=-\tfrac1{3\pi}$. A delay $T=1$ multiplies $S_k$ by $e^{-jk\omega_0 T}=e^{-jk\pi/2}$:

$$X_1=e^{-j\pi/2}\tfrac1\pi=-\tfrac j\pi\ \checkmark$$ $$X_3=e^{-j3\pi/2}\big(-\tfrac1{3\pi}\big)=-\tfrac j{3\pi}\ \checkmark$$

$e^{-j3\pi/2}=\cos\tfrac{3\pi}2-j\sin\tfrac{3\pi}2=0-j(-1)=j$.

Top: the reconstruction hugs the pulse (Gibbs at the edges). Bottom: all stems at odd $k$ are imaginary and odd in $k$.

PitfallsCommon Mistakes
Common mistakes

Four ways to lose points.

  1. Dropping the $1/T_0$. The harmonics have norm $1$ only because of $1/T_0$ (the DFT has $N$). Without it you compute $T_0 X_k$. Test: $X_0$ must equal the average value.
  2. Wrong exponent sign. Analysis uses $e^{-jk\omega_0 t}$, synthesis uses $e^{+jk\omega_0 t}$. With the wrong sign, $X_k$ and $X_{-k}$ swap: a sine would get $+j/2$ at $k=1$.
  3. Not integrating over exactly one period. Any interval of length $T_0$ works (choose $[-T_0/2,T_0/2]$ for even pulses); $[0,T_0/2]$ does not.
  4. $X_{-k}=X_k$ for every real signal. That needs real and even. Real alone gives $X_{-k}=X_k^{*}$ (practice problem 2: $X_1=-0.318j$, $X_{-1}=+0.318j$).
