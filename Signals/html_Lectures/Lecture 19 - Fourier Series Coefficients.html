The inner product is an integral. Project onto each harmonic to extract its coefficient.

Lecture 19Objectives
By the end of this lecture you will

Take the analysis integral
and read off $S_k$.

  1. Define the inner product $\langle f, g\rangle = \tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0} f^{*}(t) g(t)\,dt$.
  2. Verify orthonormality of the harmonics: $\langle v^{(k)}, v^{(\ell)}\rangle = \delta_{k\ell}$.

ConceptContinuous-Time Inner Product
Inner product

Sum becomes integral —
average over one period.

$$\langle f, g\rangle \,\stackrel{\text{def}}{=}\, \tfrac{1}{T_0}\,\int_{T_0} f^{*}(t)\, g(t)\,dt$$

Same axioms as the vector version: conjugate symmetric, sesquilinear, $\langle f, f\rangle \geq 0$.

Integral may be taken over any interval of length $T_0$ — the integrand is periodic.

ConceptENES-222 · L19
ConceptOrthonormal Harmonics
Orthonormality

The harmonics are
pairwise orthonormal.

$$\langle v^{(k)}, v^{(\ell)}\rangle \,=\, \delta_{k\ell}$$

For $k = \ell$, the integrand is $1$, average over the period is $1$.

For $k \neq \ell$, the integrand is a complex sinusoid with nonzero integer frequency $(\ell - k)\Omega_0$ — averages to zero over one full period.

DFT analogue: $V^{H}V = N\,I$. Here we get $I$ directly because the inner product is normalised.

ConceptENES-222 · L19
ConceptAnalysis Equation
Analysis equation

Project $s$ onto each harmonic.

$$S_k \,=\, \langle v^{(k)}, s\rangle \,=\, \tfrac{1}{T_0}\,\int_{T_0} s(t)\, e^{-j k \Omega_0 t}\,dt$$

Take inner products of both sides of the synthesis equation with $v^{(k)}$. Orthonormality kills all but the $k$-th term, leaving $S_k$.

Any interval of length $T_0$ — pick the one that makes the integral easiest.

Worked ExampleRectangular Pulse Train
Worked example

Pulse train of width $\alpha T_0$,
height $1$.

The signal is $1$ for $|t| < \alpha T_0/2$ and $0$ elsewhere within one period.

DC offset
$$S_0 \,=\, \tfrac{1}{T_0}\,\int_{-\alpha T_0/2}^{\alpha T_0/2} dt \,=\, \alpha$$
For $k \neq 0$
$$S_k \,=\, \tfrac{1}{T_0}\,\int_{-\alpha T_0/2}^{\alpha T_0/2} e^{-j k \Omega_0 t}\,dt$$
Integrate and simplify
$$S_k \,=\, \tfrac{2\sin(k\alpha\Omega_0 T_0 /2)}{k\Omega_0 T_0} \,=\, \tfrac{\sin(k\alpha\pi)}{k\pi}$$
Note

$S_k$ is real and even in $k$ — because the pulse train is real and symmetric.

VisualSinc Envelope
Reading the coefficients

$S_k = \mathrm{sinc}(k\alpha)$
up to normalization.

Width $\alpha$ controls the envelope. As $\alpha \to 1$, the pulse fills the period and only $S_0$ is nonzero. As $\alpha \to 0$, the spectrum spreads out — many harmonics.

The first zero of $S_k$ is at $k = 1/\alpha$ — the "narrower the pulse, the wider the spectrum".

PracticePiecewise-Constant Signal
Practice

Compute $X_0$ and $X_k$
for $k \neq 0$.

Signal

Period $T_0 = 6$. On $[-3,3)$: takes value $3$ for $|t| < 1$; value $2$ for $1 \leq |t| < 2$; value $0$ otherwise.

  • Decompose as a sum of two pulse trains plus a constant.
  • Use linearity of the analysis equation.
  • The pulse-train formula from the worked example does the rest.
PracticeENES-222 · L19
SolutionAdd Two Sincs
Solution

Stack a tall inner pulse
on a shorter outer one.

Decomposition

Write $x(t) = 2\,p_{2}(t) + p_{1}(t)$ where $p_w$ is a pulse train of width $w$ inside period $T_0=6$.

$X_0$
$$X_0 \,=\, 2 \cdot \tfrac{2}{3} + 1 \cdot \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot 2 + 2 = \tfrac{10}{3}$$

(Mean of the piecewise-constant signal over one period.)

For $k \neq 0$

Each pulse contributes a sinc-like term. With $\alpha_1 = 1/3$ and $\alpha_2 = 2/3$:

$$X_k \,=\, \frac{\sin(2k\pi/3)}{k\pi} \,+\, \frac{2\sin(k\pi/3)}{k\pi}$$

Linearity does all the heavy lifting — never integrate twice if a known pair will do.

Take with you

Three things.

01 · INNER PRODUCT
$\langle f, g\rangle = \tfrac{1}{T_0}\int_{T_0} f^{*} g\, dt$

Harmonics form an orthonormal basis.
