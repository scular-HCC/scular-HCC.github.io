Inner Products, Norms,
Angles & Projection
Geometry returns: length is $\sqrt{\langle a,a\rangle}$, angle comes from the dot product, and projection finds the closest point in a subspace.

ConceptInner Product · Norm
Inner product and norm

Length is the inner product
of a vector with itself.

$$\langle a,b\rangle \,=\, a^{\top}b \,=\, \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_i b_i$$
$$\|a\| \,=\, \sqrt{\langle a,a\rangle} \,=\, \sqrt{\textstyle\sum_i a_i^{2}}$$

Symmetric: $\langle a,b\rangle = \langle b,a\rangle$.

Linear in either slot: $\langle c_1 a^{(1)} + c_2 a^{(2)},\, b\rangle = c_1\langle a^{(1)},b\rangle + c_2\langle a^{(2)},b\rangle$.

Scaling: $\|ca\| = |c|\,\|a\|$.

ConceptOrthogonality & Angle
Orthogonality and angle

$\langle a,b\rangle = 0$ means perpendicular.
Otherwise it tells you the angle.

Orthogonality
$$a \perp b \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \langle a,b\rangle = 0$$

Then $\|a+b\|^{2} = \|a\|^{2} + \|b\|^{2}$ — Pythagoras.

Angle
$$\cos\theta \,=\, \frac{\langle a,b\rangle}{\|a\|\,\|b\|},\;\; \theta \in [0,\pi]$$

ConceptProjection of $b$ onto $a$
Projection

Find $\lambda a$ so that $b - \lambda a$
is perpendicular to $a$.

$$\lambda \,=\, \frac{\langle a,b\rangle}{\|a\|^{2}}$$

Geometrically: $\|b\|\cos\theta = \lambda\,\|a\|$. The projection is the shadow of $b$ along $a$.

FIG. · $\lambda a$ is the foot of the perpendicular from $b$ onto the line of $a$.

PracticeNorms, Orthogonality, Angle
Practice

Verify the four claims.

Setup
$$v^{(1)} = (5, 1, 3, 1)^{\top},\quad v^{(2)} = (1, 3, -1, -5)^{\top},\quad v^{(3)} = (-6, 6, -6, 6)^{\top}$$
  1. $\|v^{(1)}\| = \|v^{(2)}\| = \|v^{(3)}\|/2 = 6$.
  2. $v^{(1)} \perp v^{(2)}$.
  3. The projection of $v^{(3)}$ onto $v^{(1)}$ equals $-v^{(1)}$.
  4. The angle between $v^{(1)}$ and $v^{(3)}$ is $2\pi/3$.

$$\|v^{(1)}\|^{2} = 25+1+9+1 = 36 \Rightarrow \|v^{(1)}\| = 6$$ $$\|v^{(2)}\|^{2} = 1+9+1+25 = 36 \Rightarrow \|v^{(2)}\| = 6$$ $$\|v^{(3)}\|^{2} = 4(36) = 144 \Rightarrow \|v^{(3)}\| = 12$$

ConceptRange & Projection onto $\mathcal{R}(V)$
Range of a matrix

The image of $V$ is the set
of all linear combinations of its columns.

$$\mathcal{R}(V) \,=\, \{V c \,:\, c \in \mathbb{R}^{n}\}$$

$\mathcal{R}(V)$ is a linear subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{m}$ — a flat through the origin (line, plane, hyperplane).

If the columns of $V$ are pairwise orthogonal, projection onto $\mathcal{R}(V)$ reduces to summing the one-column projections.

Closest point in $\mathcal{R}(V)$ to $s$
$$\hat{s} \,=\, \sum_{j=1}^{n} c_j\, v^{(j)},\;\; c_j \,=\, \frac{\langle v^{(j)},\, s\rangle}{\|v^{(j)}\|^{2}}$$

Worked ExampleProjection of $s$ onto $\mathcal{R}(V)$
Worked example

Two orthogonal columns,
three-dim target $s$.

$$V = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ -2 & 4 \\ 1 & 5\end{bmatrix},\;\; \|v^{(1)}\|^{2}=6,\;\; \|v^{(2)}\|^{2}=50$$ $$s = (-6,\, 2,\, 7)^{\top}$$
Per-column coefficients
$$c_1 = \frac{-6 - 4 + 7}{6} = -\tfrac{1}{2}$$ $$c_2 = \frac{-18 + 8 + 35}{50} = \tfrac{1}{2}$$
Projection & error
$$\hat{s} = -\tfrac{1}{2} v^{(1)} + \tfrac{1}{2} v^{(2)} = (1, 3, 2)^{\top}$$ $$s - \hat{s} = (-7, -1, 5)^{\top} \perp v^{(1)},\,v^{(2)}\;\checkmark$$

Lecture 10Summary
Take with you

Four geometric facts.

01 · DOT PRODUCT
$\langle a,b\rangle = a^{\top}b$
Length $\|a\| = \sqrt{\langle a,a\rangle}$.
02 · ORTHOGONALITY
$a\perp b \Leftrightarrow \langle a,b\rangle = 0$
Pythagoras: $\|a+b\|^{2} = \|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}$.
03 · PROJECTION
$\lambda = \langle a,b\rangle/\|a\|^{2}$
Closest point on line through $a$.
04 · ORTHOGONAL BASIS
$\hat{s} = \sum_j c_j v^{(j)}$
Project onto each column, sum.
