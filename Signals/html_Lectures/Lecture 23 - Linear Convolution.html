Linear Convolution
& Impulse Response
The impulse response $h[n]$ characterises an LTI system completely. The output is $y = h * x$ — a true (non-circular) convolution sum.

ConceptImpulse Response
Universal characterization

One signal — $h[n]$ — defines
the entire LTI system.

$$x[n] = \delta[n] \;\Rightarrow\; y[n] = h[n]$$
$$y[n] \,=\, \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} h[k]\, x[n-k] \,=\, (h * x)[n]$$

For an FIR filter with coefficients $b_0, \ldots, b_M$, $h[n] = b_n$ for $n = 0:M$, zero otherwise.

Convolution is commutative: $h * x = x * h$.

ConceptENES-222 · L23
DerivationWhy the Convolution Sum
Where the convolution sum comes from

Impulses in, shifted copies of $h$ out.

1 · Split the input into impulses
$$x[n]=\sum_{k}x[k]\,\delta[n-k]$$

Example $x=(1,2,3)$: $x[n]=1\,\delta[n]+2\,\delta[n-1]+3\,\delta[n-2]$.

2 · Respond to each impulse

Time invariance: $\delta[n-k]\to h[n-k]$. Linearity: $x[k]\,\delta[n-k]\to x[k]\,h[n-k]$.

3 · Add up the responses
$$y[n]=\sum_{k}x[k]\,h[n-k]\;=\;\sum_{m}h[m]\,x[n-m]\quad(m=n-k)$$

Numbers, $h=(1,1)$: $\;1\cdot(1,1,0,0)+2\cdot(0,1,1,0)+3\cdot(0,0,1,1)=(1,3,5,3)$.

DerivationENES-222 · L23
ConceptFlip, Shift, Dot
Computing one output sample

$y[n]$ = dot product of $h$
with a flipped & shifted $x$.

  1. Reverse $x$ in time: $\tilde{x}[k] = x[-k]$.
  2. Delay by $n$: $\tilde{x}[k - n] = x[n - k]$.
  3. Dot-product with $h$: $y[n] = \sum_k h[k]\, x[n-k]$.

Repeat for every $n$.

One sample: $y[3]$.

Worked ExampleLinear Convolution Table
Worked example

$b * x$ for length-4 vectors.

Filter $b=(2,-1,1,-2)^{\top}$ ($K=4$, entries $b_0,\dots,b_3$); input $x=(1,2,3,-1)^{\top}$ ($L=4$, entries $x_0,\dots,x_3$, and $x_m=0$ elsewhere). Output: $K+L-1=7$ samples.

Sum of b_k x_(n-k) over k, keeping 0 ≤ n−k ≤ 3
$$\begin{aligned} y[0] &= 2\cdot1 = 2\\ y[1] &= 2\cdot2 + (-1)\cdot1 = 4 - 1 = 3\\ y[2] &= 2\cdot3 + (-1)\cdot2 + 1\cdot1 = 6 - 2 + 1 = 5\\ y[3] &= 2\cdot(-1) + (-1)\cdot3 + 1\cdot2 + (-2)\cdot1 = -2 - 3 + 2 - 2 = -5\\ y[4] &= (-1)\cdot(-1) + 1\cdot3 + (-2)\cdot2 = 1 + 3 - 4 = 0\\ y[5] &= 1\cdot(-1) + (-2)\cdot3 = -1 - 6 = -7\\ y[6] &= (-2)\cdot(-1) = 2 \end{aligned}$$

Result: $b*x=(2,3,5,-5,0,-7,2)^{\top}$. Check: its entries sum to $0=(\sum b)(\sum x)=0\cdot 5$.

Worked ExampleSecond Method: Product Grid
Same example, no flipping

Products on anti-diagonals.

Write every product $b_k x_m$ in a grid. The product with $k+m=n$ contributes to $y[n]$, so $y[n]$ is the sum along the anti-diagonal $k+m=n$.

Grid of products
$$\begin{array}{c|cccc} & x_0=1 & x_1=2 & x_2=3 & x_3=-1\\\hline b_0=2 & 2 & 4 & 6 & -2\\ b_1=-1 & -1 & -2 & -3 & 1\\ b_2=1 & 1 & 2 & 3 & -1\\ b_3=-2 & -2 & -4 & -6 & 2 \end{array}$$
Sum along k + m = n
$$\begin{aligned} y[0]&=2\\ y[1]&=4-1=3\\ y[2]&=6-2+1=5\\ y[3]&=-2-3+2-2=-5\\ y[4]&=1+3-4=0\\ y[5]&=-1-6=-7\\ y[6]&=2 \end{aligned}$$

Same result $(2,3,5,-5,0,-7,2)$. This grid is exactly polynomial multiplication, the topic of Lecture 24.

ConceptOutput Length
Length of $h * x$

$h$ of length $K$, $x$ of length $L$
⇒ output of length $K + L - 1$.

$$\text{activity interval: } [0,\, K + L - 2]$$

The first output occurs as soon as $h$ and $x$ overlap; the last occurs just before they separate.

This formula tells you how much to zero-pad before using a circular convolution.

ConceptENES-222 · L23
ConceptConvolution Rules
Algebra of convolution

It behaves like multiplication.

  • Commutative: $h*x=x*h$. Either signal may play the filter.
  • Associative: $(h_1*h_2)*x=h_1*(h_2*x)$. This is the cascade rule of Lecture 22.
  • Distributive: $h*(x_1+x_2)=h*x_1+h*x_2$. Parallel filters add; block processing uses it.
  • Identity and delay: $x*\delta=x$ and $x*\delta[n-d]=x[n-d]$. Example: $(1,2,3)*\delta[n-2]=(0,0,1,2,3)$.
  • Sum rule: $\sum_n y[n]=\big(\sum_n h[n]\big)\big(\sum_n x[n]\big)$. A quick check of every hand computation.

SolutionConvolve Two Short Vectors
Solution

$b*x=(2,2,1,2,2,3)$.

Length

$K+L-1=3+4-1=6$ samples, $n=0,\dots,5$.

Sum rule

$\sum b=3$, $\sum x=2+0-1+3=4$, so $\sum y$ must be $12$. Our values: $2+2+1+2+2+3=12$ ✓

Every sample
$$\begin{aligned} y[0]&=1\cdot2=2\\ y[1]&=1\cdot0+1\cdot2=2\\ y[2]&=1\cdot(-1)+1\cdot0+1\cdot2=1\\ y[3]&=1\cdot3+1\cdot(-1)+1\cdot0=2\\ y[4]&=1\cdot3+1\cdot(-1)=2\\ y[5]&=1\cdot3=3 \end{aligned}$$

Each interior sample is the sum of three consecutive inputs: a running sum.

ConceptConvolve via Padded DFT
Trick

Linear $\to$ circular —
just zero-pad enough.

$$b * s \,=\, [b;\, 0_{L-1}] \,\circledast\, [s;\, 0_{K-1}]$$

Pad each vector to length $K + L - 1$ (or more). The circular convolution of the padded vectors equals the linear convolution of the originals.

B = fft(b, K+L-1); S = fft(s, K+L-1); y = ifft(B .* S);

ConceptWhy Padding Is Needed
Too short a DFT

Circular convolution wraps the tail.

Same b and x, DFT length 4

The linear result $c=(2,3,5,-5,0,-7,2)$ has 7 samples, but a length-4 circular convolution has only 4 slots: samples $n=4,5,6$ wrap onto $n=0,1,2$.

Wrapped values
$$\begin{aligned}n=0:&\ 2+0=2\\n=1:&\ 3+(-7)=-4\\n=2:&\ 5+2=7\\n=3:&\ -5\end{aligned}$$

Circular result $(2,-4,7,-5)$: the linear one cannot be recovered.

Top: linear $b*x$, shaded tail (indices 4–6) wraps. Bottom: circular result of length 4. With $N\ge K+L-1=7$ nothing wraps.

Worked ExampleLinear Convolution via the DFT
Worked example

Pad, multiply, invert.

$b=(1,2)^{\top}$ ($K=2$), $x=(3,-1,2)^{\top}$ ($L=3$). Need $N\ge K+L-1=4$; with $N=4$, $e^{-j2\pi kn/4}=(-j)^{kn}$.

Step 1 · Padded DFTs
$$\begin{aligned}B[k]&=1+2(-j)^{k}:\ (3,\ 1-2j,\ -1,\ 1+2j)\\X[0]&=3-1+2=4\\X[1]&=3+(-1)(-j)+2(-1)=1+j\\X[2]&=3+(-1)(-1)+2(1)=6\\X[3]&=3+(-1)(j)+2(-1)=1-j\end{aligned}$$
Step 2 · Multiply
$$\begin{aligned}Y[0]&=3\cdot4=12\\Y[1]&=(1-2j)(1+j)=3-j\\Y[2]&=(-1)(6)=-6\\Y[3]&=(1+2j)(1-j)=3+j\end{aligned}$$
Step 3 · Inverse DFT
$$\begin{aligned}y[0]&=\tfrac14(12+3-j-6+3+j)=3\\y[1]&=\tfrac14(12+(3j+1)+6+(-3j+1))=5\\y[2]&=\tfrac14(12-(3-j)-6-(3+j))=0\\y[3]&=\tfrac14(12+(-3j-1)+6+(3j-1))=4\end{aligned}$$

Direct check: $b*x=(3,\,-1+6,\,2-2,\,4)=(3,5,0,4)$ ✓

ConceptBlock Convolution
Long inputs, fixed-size FFTs

Split, convolve each block,
add overlapping tails.

$$b * [s^{(1)};\, s^{(2)}] \,=\, [b * s^{(1)};\, 0_{L_2}] \,+\, [0_{L_1};\, b * s^{(2)}]$$

Real-time filters use this to run with fixed-length FFTs no matter how long the input. The overlap region is where consecutive blocks' tails sum.

SolutionBlock 1

Step 1 of 3 · convolve $b$ with $s^{(1)}$.

$b_0,\dots,b_3=1,-3,-3,1$; $s^{(1)}=(1, -2, 3, 4, 4, -2)$, so $y^{(1)}=b*s^{(1)}$ has $4+6-1=9$ samples; each term is $b_k s_{n-k}$ with $0\le n-k\le 5$.

$$\begin{aligned} y^{(1)}[0] &= 1\cdot1 = 1\\[-2pt] y^{(1)}[1] &= 1\cdot(-2) + (-3)\cdot1 = -2 - 3 = -5\\[-2pt] y^{(1)}[2] &= 1\cdot3 + (-3)\cdot(-2) + (-3)\cdot1 = 3 + 6 - 3 = 6\\[-2pt] y^{(1)}[3] &= 1\cdot4 + (-3)\cdot3 + (-3)\cdot(-2) + 1\cdot1 = 4 - 9 + 6 + 1 = 2\\[-2pt] y^{(1)}[4] &= 1\cdot4 + (-3)\cdot4 + (-3)\cdot3 + 1\cdot(-2) = 4 - 12 - 9 - 2 = -19\\[-2pt] y^{(1)}[5] &= 1\cdot(-2) + (-3)\cdot4 + (-3)\cdot4 + 1\cdot3 = -2 - 12 - 12 + 3 = -23\\[-2pt] y^{(1)}[6] &= (-3)\cdot(-2) + (-3)\cdot4 + 1\cdot4 = 6 - 12 + 4 = -2\\[-2pt] y^{(1)}[7] &= (-3)\cdot(-2) + 1\cdot4 = 6 + 4 = 10\\[-2pt] y^{(1)}[8] &= 1\cdot(-2) = -2 \end{aligned}$$

So $y^{(1)}=(1, -5, 6, 2, -19, -23, -2, 10, -2)^{\top}$.

SolutionENES-222 · L23
SolutionOverlap-Add
Solution · step 3 of 3

Overlap-add reassembles
the long output.

Place the blocks

$s^{(2)}$ starts at index $L_1=6$ of $s$, so $b*s^{(2)}$ is delayed by 6 samples. Total length $12+4-1=15$ (indices $0$ to $14$).

$$b * s = [y^{(1)};\, 0_{6}] + [0_{6};\, y^{(2)}]$$

$y^{(1)}$ occupies indices 0–8 and the delayed $y^{(2)}$ occupies 6–14, so indices 6, 7, 8 overlap (that is $K-1=3$ samples).

Sum in the overlap
$$\begin{aligned} n=6:&\;\, -2 + 7 = 5\\ n=7:&\;\, 10 + (-16) = -6\\ n=8:&\;\, -2 + (-35) = -37 \end{aligned}$$

Indices 0–5 come only from $y^{(1)}$; indices 9–14 come only from $y^{(2)}$ (its entries 3–8).

Result
$$\begin{aligned}b*s=(&1,-5,6,2,-19,-23,5,-6,\\&-37,-8,-8,-9,18,11,-4)^{\top}\end{aligned}$$

Matches the direct 15-sample convolution of $b$ with all of $s$.

Solved ExampleTwo Fully-Solved Examples
Solved Example A

Length-3 filter, length-5 input.

$b=(3,-2,1)^{\top}$ ($K=3$), $x=(1,0,2,-1,4)^{\top}$ ($L=5$). Length $K+L-1=7$. Define $c[n]=\sum_k b_k x_{n-k}$ with $x_m=0$ outside $0\le m\le4$.

$$\begin{aligned} c[0] &= 3\cdot1 = 3\\ c[1] &= 3\cdot0 + (-2)\cdot1 = -2\\ c[2] &= 3\cdot2 + (-2)\cdot0 + 1\cdot1 = 6 + 0 + 1 = 7\\ c[3] &= 3\cdot(-1) + (-2)\cdot2 + 1\cdot0 = -3 - 4 + 0 = -7\\ c[4] &= 3\cdot4 + (-2)\cdot(-1) + 1\cdot2 = 12 + 2 + 2 = 16\\ c[5] &= (-2)\cdot4 + 1\cdot(-1) = -8 - 1 = -9\\ c[6] &= 1\cdot4 = 4 \end{aligned}$$

So $c=(3,-2,7,-7,16,-9,4)^{\top}$. Check: $\sum c=12=(3-2+1)(1+0+2-1+4)=2\cdot6$ ✓

Solved ExampleTwo Fully-Solved Examples
Solved Example B

Find the impulse response
of a cascade.

$h^{(1)}=(1,-1)^\top$, $h^{(2)}=(1,2,1)^\top$ are the impulse responses of two filters in series. $H_i(z)=\sum_n h^{(i)}[n]z^{-n}$ is the polynomial in $z^{-1}$ with those coefficients. Find the overall $h$.

Verify by convolving directly
$$\begin{aligned} h[0]&=1\cdot1=1\\ h[1]&=1\cdot2+(-1)\cdot1=1\\ h[2]&=1\cdot1+(-1)\cdot2=-1\\ h[3]&=(-1)\cdot1=-1 \end{aligned}$$

Same as the polynomial result. $\checkmark$

Step 1 · Cascade rule

Delta through filter 1 gives $h^{(1)}$, which filter 2 turns into $h^{(1)}*h^{(2)}$; z-transforms of convolutions multiply.

$$H(z)=H_1(z)H_2(z)=(1-z^{-1})(1+2z^{-1}+z^{-2})$$
Step 2 · Distribute and collect
$$\begin{aligned} H(z)&=1\,(1+2z^{-1}+z^{-2})-z^{-1}(1+2z^{-1}+z^{-2})\\ &=1+2z^{-1}+z^{-2}-z^{-1}-2z^{-2}-z^{-3}\\ &=1+(2-1)z^{-1}+(1-2)z^{-2}-z^{-3}\\ &=1+z^{-1}-z^{-2}-z^{-3} \end{aligned}$$
Result
$$h=(1,1,-1,-1)^\top$$

ReviewCommon Mistakes
Check before you hand in

Six slips to avoid.

1 · Not flipping

$b_0$ multiplies $x[n]$, $b_1$ multiplies $x[n-1]$, and so on: the index of $x$ runs backwards.

2 · Wrong length

The length is $K+L-1$, not $K+L$ and not $\max(K,L)$. Example: $4+4-1=7$.

3 · Dropping edge terms

Terms exist only where $0\le k\le K-1$ and $0\le n-k\le L-1$. Outside, $x$ is zero; do not wrap.

4 · Padding too little

With DFT length $N < K+L-1$ the tail wraps: $(2,-4,7,-5)$ instead of $(2,3,5,-5,0,-7,2)$.

5 · Overwriting the overlap

In overlap-add, block 2 starts at index $L_1$ and the $K-1$ shared samples are added, not replaced.

6 · Skipping the check

Always test $\sum y=(\sum h)(\sum x)$ and the length. Both take ten seconds.
