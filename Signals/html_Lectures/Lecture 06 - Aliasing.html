Aliasing
Two different continuous-time sinusoids can produce identical samples. The fix — the Nyquist rate.
Reading · Textbook §1.6
Lecture 06Objectives
By the end of this lecture you will

Detect aliases and
choose a safe sampling rate.

  1. Define aliasing: $f \pm f' = k\,f_s$ for some integer $k$.
  2. Enumerate all aliases of a given frequency at a given sampling rate.
  3. Reconstruct $x(t)$ from $x[n]$ when the original frequency band is known.
  4. Apply the Nyquist rate: $f_s > 2 f_B$ avoids aliasing for a band-limited signal.

ConceptWhen are $f$ and $f'$ Aliases?
Aliasing — the definition

Two frequencies are aliases
if their samples coincide.

$$f \pm f' \,=\, k\, f_s$$

Start from $\Omega = 2\pi f/f_s$ and $\Omega' = 2\pi f'/f_s$, then use the DT equivalence $\Omega' = \pm\Omega + 2k\pi$.

The plus and minus give two families of aliases; both must be checked.

Worked ExampleReconstruction from Samples
Reconstructing $x(t)$ from $x[n]$

Pin down the true frequency
using the prior band knowledge.

Given the band $f \in [600,\, 800]$ Hz, sampled at $f_s = 400$ samples/sec, we observe

$$x[n] \,=\, 4.2\cos(0.75\pi\, n - 0.3)$$
Step 1 · Bare $f$ from $\Omega$
$$2\pi\,\frac{f}{f_s} = 0.75\pi \;\Rightarrow\; f = 150 \text{ Hz}$$

Outside the band.

Step 2 · Pick the alias in band

The only alias of $150$ Hz in $[600, 800]$ at $f_s = 400$ is $f' = 650$ Hz (from $-150 + 2\cdot 400$).

Step 3 · Reconstruct
$$x(t) \,=\, 4.2\cos(1300\pi\, t + 0.3)$$

The phase flips sign because the alias came from the negative branch ($\Omega \mapsto -\Omega$).

ConceptWhy Two Components Break
Why aliasing ruins reconstruction

If two components alias to the same $\Omega$,
$x[n]$ no longer pins down $x(t)$.

Try it

Suppose the same $x[n] = 4.2\cos(0.75\pi n - 0.3)$ is now claimed to come from two sinusoids — at $150$ Hz and $650$ Hz — sampled at $f_s = 400$. Can you write a unique $x(t)$ for it?

Answer

No. The two components fold onto the same $\Omega = 0.75\pi$; the samples alone cannot tell you the amplitude and phase split between $150$ Hz and $650$ Hz. Reconstruction fails.

ConceptENES-222 · L06
ConceptThe Nyquist Rate
Nyquist rate

Sample faster than twice
the highest frequency.

$$f_s \,>\, 2 f_B$$

If every component of $x(t)$ lies in $[0,\, f_B]$, then $f_s > 2 f_B$ guarantees that no two distinct components alias to each other.

$2 f_B$ is the Nyquist rate. You must strictly exceed it — equality is the edge case.

ConceptENES-222 · L06
VisualSpectrum Replicas & Overlap
Spectrum replicas

Sampling tiles the frequency axis
with copies of the baseband.

Plot two families of aliases: $f' = f + k f_s$ (top) and $f' = -f + k f_s$ (bottom). Each integer $k$ slides a copy of $[0, f_B]$ along the frequency axis.

Aliasing happens whenever two of these bands overlap. As long as the bands stay separated — except right at multiples of $f_s$ — reconstruction is possible.

No overlap ⇔ $f_s > 2 f_B$.

PracticeJust Below Nyquist
Practice

Show aliasing kicks in
as soon as $f_s < 2 f_B$.

  • Let $f_s = 2 f_B - \delta$, with $0 < \delta < f_B$.
  • Find two frequencies in $[0,\, f_B]$ that are aliases of each other at this $f_s$.
  • Hint: use $f + f' = f_s$.

Solution$f_s = 2 f_B - \delta$
Solution

The folded bands meet
at $f_B - \delta/2$.

Apply $f + f' = f_s$
$$f + f' = 2 f_B - \delta$$
Choose the symmetric pair
$$f = f_B - \tfrac{\delta}{2},\;\; f' = f_B - \tfrac{\delta}{2}$$

Both equal — they're the same frequency, where the bands first touch.

A non-trivial pair
$$f = f_B \,-\, \delta,\;\; f' = f_B$$

Both in $[0,\, f_B]$; their sum equals $f_s$; therefore aliases.

Why it matters

Any decrease of $f_s$ below $2 f_B$ — by however small a $\delta$ — already creates ambiguity in the band. The Nyquist criterion is sharp.

In practice

Real ADC systems include an anti-aliasing low-pass filter before sampling to enforce a known $f_B$.

Four facts on aliasing.
