Aliasing
Two different continuous-time sinusoids can produce identical samples. The fix — the Nyquist rate.
Reading · Textbook §1.6
Lecture 06Objectives
By the end of this lecture you will

Detect aliases and
choose a safe sampling rate.

  1. Define aliasing: $f \pm f' = k\,f_s$ for some integer $k$.
  2. Enumerate all aliases of a given frequency at a given sampling rate.
  3. Reconstruct $x(t)$ from $x[n]$ when the original frequency band is known.
  4. Apply the Nyquist rate: $f_s > 2 f_B$ avoids aliasing for a band-limited signal.

IntuitionWhy Wheels Run Backwards
Intuition · ≈ 2 min

A spinning wheel on film.

Film samples the world $f_s=24$ times per second. A spoked wheel turns $f=23.33$ rev/s.

Step 1 · Turn per frame
$$\tfrac{f}{f_s}\cdot360^\circ=\tfrac{23.33}{24}\cdot360^\circ=350^\circ$$
Step 2 · What the eye sees
$$350^\circ\equiv350^\circ-360^\circ=-10^\circ\ \text{per frame}$$
Step 3 · Apparent speed
$$-10^\circ\cdot24\ \text{frames/s}=-240^\circ/\text{s}=-0.667\ \text{rev/s}$$

The wheel appears to creep backwards at 0.667 rev/s, although it spins 23.33 rev/s forwards.

Same thing in our notation

$\Omega=2\pi\tfrac{f}{f_s}=1.9444\pi$ rad/sample $\equiv1.9444\pi-2\pi=-0.0556\pi$ (that is $-10^\circ$). Two different true speeds, same samples: aliasing.

ConceptWhen Are Two Frequencies Aliases?
Aliasing, the definition

Two frequencies are aliases
if their samples coincide.

$$f \pm f' \,=\, k\, f_s$$

$f,f'$ are CT frequencies (Hz), $f_s$ the sampling rate, $k$ any integer. Sampled DT frequencies: $\Omega = 2\pi f/f_s$, $\Omega' = 2\pi f'/f_s$. The samples coincide when $\Omega' = \pm\Omega + 2k\pi$ (L04):

$$\tfrac{2\pi f'}{f_s}=\pm\tfrac{2\pi f}{f_s}+2k\pi\;\Rightarrow\; f'=\pm f+k f_s$$

Hence $f'\mp f=kf_s$. Both signs give families of aliases; check both.

Worked ExampleAliases of 150 Hz at 400
Worked example

All aliases of 150 Hz at 400 samples/s.

All aliases in $[0,2000]$ Hz.

Step 1 · The two families
$$\begin{aligned}f'&=150+400k\\f'&=-150+400k\end{aligned}\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}$$
Step 2 · Range of k

$0\le150+400k\le2000$ gives $-0.375\le k\le4.625$, so $k=0,\dots,4$.

$0\le-150+400k\le2000$ gives $0.375\le k\le5.375$, so $k=1,\dots,5$.

Step 3 · Table
$$\footnotesize\def\arraystretch{1.2}\begin{array}{c|cc}k&150+400k&-150+400k\\\hline0&150&-150\ (\text{out})\\1&550&250\\2&950&650\\3&1350&1050\\4&1750&1450\\5&2150\ (\text{out})&1850\end{array}$$
Result (10 aliases)
$$\begin{aligned}&150,\,250,\,550,\,650,\,950,\\&1050,\,1350,\,1450,\,1750,\,1850\ \text{Hz}\end{aligned}$$

Check 650: $\Omega'=2\pi\tfrac{650}{400}=3.25\pi\equiv-0.75\pi$ and $2\pi\tfrac{150}{400}=0.75\pi$: same $|\Omega|$ ✓

ConceptFolding into the Baseband
Folding rule

Every tone lands in $[0,f_s/2]$.

Step 1 · Split off whole sampling rates
$$f=k f_s+d,\qquad k=\operatorname{round}(f/f_s),\quad|d|\le\tfrac{f_s}{2}$$
Step 2 · Apparent frequency
$$f_a=|d|\in[0,\tfrac{f_s}{2}]$$
Step 3 · Why, and the phase
$$\Omega=\tfrac{2\pi f}{f_s}=2\pi k+\tfrac{2\pi d}{f_s}\equiv\tfrac{2\pi d}{f_s}$$

If $d<0$: $\cos(\tfrac{2\pi d}{f_s}n+\phi)=\cos(\tfrac{2\pi|d|}{f_s}n-\phi)$, the phase flips.

Examples at 400 samples/s
$$\small\def\arraystretch{1.5}\begin{array}{c|cccc}f&k&d&f_a&\text{flip?}\\\hline150&0&150&150&\text{no}\\550&1&150&150&\text{no}\\250&1&-150&150&\text{yes}\\650&2&-150&150&\text{yes}\\1050&3&-150&150&\text{yes}\end{array}$$

All five are aliases of 150 Hz: they land on the same $f_a$.

SolutionFold These Tones
Solution

Three of the four are aliases.

$$\small\def\arraystretch{1.5}\begin{array}{c|cccc}f&k=\operatorname{round}(f/1000)&d=f-1000k&f_a=|d|&\text{flip?}\\\hline300&0&300&300&\text{no}\\700&1&-300&300&\text{yes}\\1250&1&250&250&\text{no}\\1700&2&-300&300&\text{yes}\end{array}$$
Reading

300, 700 and 1700 Hz all fold to 300 Hz ($\Omega=0.6\pi$); 700 and 1700 with negated phase. 1250 Hz folds to 250 Hz, a different $\Omega=0.5\pi$. Check 700: $700+300=1000=f_s$ ✓ ($f+f'=kf_s$).

SolutionENES-222 · L06
Worked ExampleReconstruction: Find the Alias
Reconstructing from samples

Use the known band to pick the alias.

Known: $f\in[600,800]$ Hz, $f_s=400$ samples/s. Observed:

$$x[n]=4.2\cos(0.75\pi n-0.3)$$
Step 1 · Frequency from the samples
$$\tfrac{2\pi f}{400}=0.75\pi\Rightarrow f=\tfrac{0.75\cdot400}{2}=150\ \text{Hz}$$

Outside $[600,800]$, so 150 Hz is not the answer.

Step 2 · Which alias is in the band?

$600\le150+400k\le800$ gives $1.125\le k\le1.625$: no integer.

$600\le-150+400k\le800$ gives $1.875\le k\le2.375$: $k=2$.

$$f'=-150+2\cdot400=650\ \text{Hz}$$
Worked ExampleENES-222 · L06
Worked ExampleReconstruction: Phase and Check
Reconstructing (cont.)

The $-$ branch negates the phase.

Step 3 · DT frequency of 650 Hz
$$\Omega'=2\pi\tfrac{650}{400}=3.25\pi=-0.75\pi+4\pi\equiv-\Omega$$
Step 4 · Match the samples
$$\begin{aligned}x[n]&=\cos(\Omega n-0.3)\\&=\cos(-\Omega n+0.3)\\&=\cos(\Omega'n+0.3)\end{aligned}$$

So the 650 Hz sinusoid needs phase $\phi'=+0.3$.

Answer
$$x(t)=4.2\cos(1300\pi t+0.3)$$

That is $4.2\cos(2\pi\cdot650\,t+0.3)$.

Check at sample n = 1

From the samples ($n=1$):

$$x[1]=4.2\cos(2.0562)=-1.9596$$

From the 650 Hz signal at $t=\tfrac{1}{400}$ s:

$$x(\tfrac1{400})=4.2\cos(10.509)=-1.9596\ \checkmark$$

With the wrong sign, $4.2\cos(3.25\pi-0.3)=-3.7149\neq x[1]$.

ConceptWhy Two Components Break
Why aliasing ruins reconstruction

If two components alias to the same $\Omega$,
$x[n]$ no longer pins down $x(t)$.

Try it

Suppose the same $x[n] = 4.2\cos(0.75\pi n - 0.3)$ is now claimed to come from two sinusoids — at $150$ Hz and $650$ Hz — sampled at $f_s = 400$. Can you write a unique $x(t)$ for it?

Answer

No. The two components fold onto the same $\Omega = 0.75\pi$; the samples alone cannot tell you the amplitude and phase split between $150$ Hz and $650$ Hz. Reconstruction fails.

ConceptENES-222 · L06
ConceptThe Nyquist Rate
Nyquist rate

Sample faster than twice
the highest frequency.

$$f_s \,>\, 2 f_B$$

If every component lies in $[0,\, f_B]$, then $f_s > 2 f_B$ guarantees no two distinct components alias. Take $f_1\neq f_2$ in $[0,f_B]$:

$|f_1-f_2|\le f_B< f_s$, so $f_1-f_2=kf_s$ forces $k=0$, i.e. $f_1=f_2$. And $0< f_1+f_2\le2f_B< f_s$ lies strictly between $0$ and $f_s$, so it is not a multiple of $f_s$.

$2 f_B$ is the Nyquist rate. Equality is the edge case: at $f_s=2f_B$, $f_B$ aliases to $-f_B$.

PracticeENES-222 · L06
SolutionJust Below Nyquist
Solution

Pairs summing to $f_s$ are aliases.

Alias condition

The $-$ branch with $k=1$: $f'=-f+f_s$, i.e.

$$f + f' = f_s = 2 f_B - \delta$$
Choose a pair in the band
$$f = f_B - \delta,\;\; f' = f_B$$

Sum $=2f_B-\delta=f_s$ ✓. Both lie in $[0,f_B]$ since $0< \delta< f_B$ gives $f_B-\delta>0$.

Numeric check

$f_B=100,\ \delta=20,\ f_s=180$: $f=80$, $f'=100$.

$$\begin{aligned}\Omega&=\tfrac{2\pi\cdot80}{180}=\tfrac{8\pi}{9}\\\Omega'&=\tfrac{2\pi\cdot100}{180}=\tfrac{10\pi}{9}=2\pi-\tfrac{8\pi}{9}\equiv-\Omega\end{aligned}$$
More pairs

Any $f=f_B-\tfrac\delta2-\varepsilon$, $f'=f_B-\tfrac\delta2+\varepsilon$ with $0\le\varepsilon\le\tfrac\delta2$ works. $\varepsilon=0$ is the fold point $f_B-\delta/2$, an alias of itself; $\varepsilon=\delta/2$ is the pair above.

Why it matters

Any $f_s<2f_B$, however slightly, creates ambiguity in the band. Real ADCs add an anti-aliasing low-pass filter before sampling to enforce $f_B$.

SolutionENES-222 · L06
ApplicationChoosing the Sampling Rate
Application · ≈ 3 min

Nyquist plus a guard band.

Real filters cannot cut off sharply, so $f_s$ is chosen above $2f_B$.

$$\small\def\arraystretch{1.5}\begin{array}{c|cc}&\text{telephone}&\text{audio CD}\\\hline f_B&3400&20\,000\\2f_B&6800&40\,000\\f_s\ \text{used}&8000&44\,100\\f_s/2-f_B&600&2050\end{array}$$

All frequencies in Hz.

Why filter before sampling

Suppose $f_s=10$ kHz and a 7 kHz noise tone is present. Fold: $k=1$, $d=7000-10\,000=-3000$, so $f_a=3000$ Hz (phase flipped): the noise lands in the wanted 0 to 5 kHz band.

After sampling it is indistinguishable from a genuine 3 kHz signal. Therefore an analog anti-aliasing low-pass filter removes everything above $f_s/2$ before the sampler.

Worked ExampleTwo Components Merge
Worked example

Aliasing in a sum of two sinusoids.

$$x(t)=\cos(2\pi\cdot50\,t)+\cos(2\pi\cdot350\,t)$$

Sampled at $f_s=400$ samples/s.

Step 1 · DT frequencies
$$\Omega_1=\tfrac{2\pi\cdot50}{400}=\tfrac{\pi}{4},\qquad\Omega_2=\tfrac{2\pi\cdot350}{400}=\tfrac{7\pi}{4}$$
Step 2 · Check for equivalence
$$\Omega_2=\tfrac{7\pi}{4}=2\pi-\tfrac{\pi}{4}\equiv-\Omega_1\pmod{2\pi}$$ $$\cos(\Omega_2 n)=\cos(-\Omega_1 n+2\pi n)=\cos(\Omega_1 n)$$

Same as $f_1+f_2=50+350=400=f_s$.

Step 3 · Consequence
$$x[n]=\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{4}n)+\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{4}n)=2\cos(\tfrac{\pi}{4}n)$$

The components merged: $x(t)$ cannot be reconstructed from $x[n]$.

PitfallsCommon Mistakes
Common mistakes

Six ways aliasing goes wrong.

01 · ONE FAMILY
Check $\pm f+kf_s$.
Both signs give aliases; missing one loses half of them.
02 · PHASE
$-$ branch: $\phi\to-\phi$.
650 Hz needs $+0.3$, not $-0.3$.
03 · RATE vs FREQUENCY
Nyquist rate $=2f_B$.
Nyquist frequency $=f_s/2$. Different things.
04 · STRICT INEQUALITY
$f_s>2f_B$, not $\ge$.
$\sin(2\pi ft)$ sampled at $2f$ gives $\sin(\pi n)=0$.
05 · TOO LATE
Filter before the sampler.
Afterwards 7 kHz and 3 kHz look the same.
06 · SYMBOLS
$f$ in Hz, $\Omega$ per sample.
$\Omega=2\pi f/f_s$; no Hz inside a DT formula.

Lecture 06Summary
Take with you

Four facts on aliasing.

01 · DEFINITION
$f \pm f' = k f_s$
Both families must be checked.
02 · CONSEQUENCE
Distinct $f$'s produce the same $x[n]$.
$x(t)$ no longer recoverable from $x[n]$.
03 · NYQUIST
$f_s > 2 f_B$
Necessary and sufficient for band $[0,\, f_B]$.
04 · ENGINEERING
Anti-aliasing filter first.
Enforce $f_B$ before the ADC.
