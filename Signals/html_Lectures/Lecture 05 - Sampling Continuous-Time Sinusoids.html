Sampling
Continuous-Time Sinusoids
Every $T_s$ seconds, we read off one value of $x(t)$. The resulting sequence is a DT sinusoid whose frequency is set by the ratio of the two periods.
Lecture 05Objectives
By the end of this lecture you will

Sample a sinusoid and
predict the discrete result.

  1. Apply the sampling formula $x[n] = x(n T_s)$ with the right units.
  2. Compute the DT frequency $\Omega = \omega T_s = 2\pi f/f_s = 2\pi T_s/T$.
  3. Find the period: $\Omega/2\pi = k/N$ means $N$ samples span $k$ periods.
  4. List all sampling periods giving the same $x[n]$: $T_s' = \pm T_s + kT$.

ConceptThe Sampling Formula
From continuous to discrete

Read $x(t)$ every $T_s$ seconds.

$$x[n] \,=\, x(n T_s)$$
$T_s$
sampling period (sec)
$f_s = 1/T_s$
sampling rate (samples/sec)
$n$
integer sample index
Sampling a CT sinusoid

Here $\omega$ is the CT frequency (rad/s) and $\phi$ the phase. Substitute $t=nT_s$ and regroup:

$$x(t) = \cos(\omega t + \phi) \;\Longrightarrow\; x[n] = \cos(\omega\, nT_s + \phi) = \cos\big((\omega T_s)\,n + \phi\big)$$

ContextSampling in Practice
Where the numbers come from

A sampler reads $x(t)$
every $T_s$ seconds.

Telephone
$f_s=8000$ Hz, $T_s=125\ \mu$s
Audio CD
$f_s=44\,100$ Hz, $T_s=22.7\ \mu$s
Studio audio
$f_s=48\,000$ Hz, $T_s=20.8\ \mu$s
Video
30 frames/s, $T_s=33.3$ ms
ECG
$f_s=500$ Hz, $T_s=2$ ms
Keep the units straight

$f$ in Hz, $\omega=2\pi f$ in rad/s, $T=1/f$ in s. $f_s=1/T_s$ in samples/s. Only $\Omega=\omega T_s$ is in rad per sample.

Convert ms and $\mu$s to seconds before multiplying by $\omega$. After sampling, only the ratio $f/f_s=T_s/T$ matters.

ConceptDT Frequency from Sampling
Discrete-time frequency

$\Omega$ is set by the ratio
of the two periods.

$$\Omega \,=\, \omega T_s \,=\, 2\pi\,\frac{f}{f_s} \,=\, 2\pi\,\frac{T_s}{T}$$

The coefficient of $n$ is $\Omega=\omega T_s$ (radians of phase advance per sample). With $f$ the CT frequency in Hz and $T=1/f$ its period:

$$\omega=2\pi f\;\Rightarrow\;\Omega=2\pi f\,T_s=2\pi\frac{f}{f_s}\quad(T_s=1/f_s)$$ $$f=\frac1T\;\Rightarrow\;\Omega=2\pi\frac{T_s}{T}$$

Small $T_s/T$ means small $\Omega$: samples densely track $x(t)$.

Worked ExampleTone at Telephone Rate
Worked example

A 1200 Hz tone sampled at 8 kHz.

$$x(t)=\cos(2\pi\cdot1200\,t+0.4),\qquad f_s=8000\ \text{samples/s}$$
Step 1 · The CT signal
$$f=1200\ \text{Hz},\quad\omega=2\pi f=2400\pi\approx7539.8\ \text{rad/s},\quad T=\tfrac1f=833.3\ \mu\text{s}$$
Step 2 · The sampling period
$$T_s=\tfrac{1}{f_s}=\tfrac{1}{8000}=125\ \mu\text{s}=0.000125\ \text{s}$$
Step 3 · DT frequency, three ways
$$\begin{aligned}\Omega&=\omega T_s=2400\pi(0.000125)=0.3\pi\\\Omega&=2\pi\tfrac{f}{f_s}=2\pi\tfrac{1200}{8000}=2\pi(0.15)=0.3\pi\\\Omega&=2\pi\tfrac{T_s}{T}=2\pi\tfrac{125}{833.3}=2\pi(0.15)=0.3\pi\end{aligned}$$

$0.3\pi=0.9425$ rad/sample $=54^\circ$ per sample, already in $[0,\pi]$.

Step 4 · The DT sinusoid
$$x[n]=\cos(0.3\pi\,n+0.4)$$
Check

$n=1$: $x(T_s)=\cos(2\pi\cdot1200\cdot0.000125+0.4)=\cos(1.3425)=0.2263$ and $x[1]=\cos(0.3\pi+0.4)=\cos(1.3425)=0.2263$ ✓

Worked ExampleSamples by Hand, 3 ms
Hand-computed samples

$T_s=3$ ms: five samples per period.

$x(t)=\cos(\tfrac{400\pi}{3}t-\tfrac{\pi}{8})$, $\omega=\tfrac{400\pi}{3}$ rad/s, $\phi=-22.5^\circ$.

Step 1 · Turn per sample
$$\begin{aligned}\Omega&=\omega T_s=\tfrac{400\pi}{3}(0.003)\\&=0.4\pi\ \text{rad}=72^\circ\end{aligned}$$
Step 2 · Angle of each sample
$$\begin{aligned}\theta_n&=\Omega n+\phi\\&=72^\circ n-22.5^\circ\end{aligned}$$
Step 3 · The table
$$\footnotesize\def\arraystretch{1.5}\begin{array}{c|cccccc}n&0&1&2&3&4&5\\\hline t_n\,(\text{ms})&0&3&6&9&12&15\\\theta_n&-22.5^\circ&49.5^\circ&121.5^\circ&193.5^\circ&265.5^\circ&337.5^\circ\\x[n]=\cos\theta_n&0.9239&0.6494&-0.5225&-0.9724&-0.0785&0.9239\end{array}$$
Reading it

Each angle is the previous plus $72^\circ$ ($49.5=-22.5+72$, $121.5=49.5+72$, ...). After 5 samples the angle has advanced $5\cdot72^\circ=360^\circ$: $x[5]=x[0]$. So $N=5$ samples per period, and $5T_s=15$ ms $=T$. These are the blue circles of the figure.

ConceptPeriodicity from Sampling
Periodicity from sampling

$N$ samples span $k$ periods.

$$\frac{\Omega}{2\pi}=\frac{T_s}{T}=\frac{f}{f_s}=\frac{k}{N}\ \ (\text{lowest terms})$$

Then $N T_s=kT$: after $N$ samples the sampler has run exactly $k$ periods of $x(t)$, so the values start over.

Examples so far

$3$ ms: $T_s/T=\tfrac{3}{15}=\tfrac15$, so $N=5$, $k=1$.
$5$ ms: $\tfrac{5}{15}=\tfrac13$, so $N=3$, $k=1$.
Tone: $\tfrac{1200}{8000}=\tfrac{3}{20}$, so $N=20$, $k=3$.
$T_s/T=\tfrac23$: $N=3$, $k=2$.

Notebook figure. The 20 samples $n=0,\dots,19$ cover exactly three periods (dashed lines) of the 1200 Hz tone. The red triangles mark $n=0$ and $n=20$: the same value, the pattern restarts.

SolutionSpecial Sampling Periods
Constant sequence

$\cos(\Omega n+\phi)$ is the same for all $n$ iff $\Omega$ is a whole number of turns:

$$\Omega = 2\pi k \;\Leftrightarrow\; 2\pi\tfrac{T_s}{T}=2\pi k \;\Leftrightarrow\; T_s = kT$$

$k=1,2,\ldots$ Every sample lands on the same phase of the sinusoid.

Alternating sequence

Signs alternate iff $\Omega=\pi$ (mod $2\pi$):

$$\Omega = \pi+2\pi k \;\Leftrightarrow\; T_s = (k + \tfrac{1}{2})\,T$$

$k=0,1,\ldots$ Consecutive samples are half a period (plus whole periods) apart, so each lands at the opposite phase.

Periodic in n

Periodic iff $\Omega = \tfrac{k}{N}2\pi$ for integers $k,N$ (Lecture 4). Since $\Omega=2\pi T_s/T$:

$$\frac{T_s}{T} = \frac{k}{N} \;\;\text{(rational)}$$

The fundamental period is the denominator $N$ in lowest terms.

Equivalent rates

Same $\Omega$ up to $\pm$ and $2\pi k$ (derived on the next slide):

$$T_s \pm T_s' = kT$$

Two sampling periods giving the same DT signal differ by a whole number of $T$ (or sum to one).

ConceptEquivalent Sampling Rates
Equivalent sampling periods

Infinitely many $T_s$
produce the same $\Omega$.

$$\begin{aligned}\Omega' &= \pm \Omega + 2k\pi\\ &\Updownarrow\\ T_s' &= \pm T_s + kT\end{aligned}$$

Substitute $\Omega=2\pi T_s/T$ and $\Omega'=2\pi T_s'/T$ and divide by $2\pi/T$:

$$\frac{2\pi T_s'}{T}=\pm\frac{2\pi T_s}{T}+2k\pi\;\Rightarrow\;T_s'=\pm T_s+kT$$

The $+$ branch is the $2\pi$ equivalence; the $-$ branch is the $\Omega\leftrightarrow-\Omega$ symmetry from L04. Rearranged, $T_s\mp T_s'=-kT$, i.e. $T_s\pm T_s'=k'T$.

Worked ExampleSampling Periods for Signal 1
Worked example

Sampling periods for signal 1.

Sample $x(t)=\cos(\tfrac{400\pi}{3}t-\tfrac{\pi}{8})$ so that $x_1[n]=\cos(\tfrac{2\pi}{5}n-\tfrac{\pi}{8})$. Which $T_s>0$ work?

Step 1 · Period of $x(t)$
$$\begin{aligned}T&=\tfrac{2\pi}{\omega}=\tfrac{2\pi\cdot3}{400\pi}=\tfrac{6}{400}\\&=0.015\ \text{s}=15\ \text{ms}\end{aligned}$$
Step 2 · Direct solution
$$\begin{aligned}T_s&=\tfrac{\Omega}{\omega}=\tfrac{2\pi/5}{400\pi/3}=\tfrac{6}{2000}\\&=0.003\ \text{s}=3\ \text{ms}\end{aligned}$$
Step 3 · Add whole turns

Keeping the phase $-\tfrac{\pi}{8}$ needs $\Omega'=\Omega+2\pi k$, i.e. $T_s'=T_s+kT$:

$$T_s'=3+15k\ \text{ms},\quad k=0,1,2,\ldots$$

That is $3,\ 18,\ 33,\ 48,\ldots$ ms.

Negative $k$ would give $T_s'\le0$.

Check with k = 1
$$\Omega'=\tfrac{400\pi}{3}(0.018)=2.4\pi=0.4\pi+2\pi\ \checkmark$$

Same angle every sample, so the same $x_1[n]$. The notebook prints $\Omega\equiv0.400\pi$ for 18, 33, 48, 63 ms.

Worked example (cont.)

Signal 3: flip the phase.

Target $x_3[n]=\cos(\tfrac{2\pi}{5}n+\tfrac{\pi}{8})$ from the same $x(t)$ ($T=15$ ms, $T_s=3$ ms is the base solution).

Step 1 · Cosine is even
$$x_3[n]=\cos\!\left(-\tfrac{2\pi}{5}n-\tfrac{\pi}{8}\right)$$

The phase is now $-\tfrac{\pi}{8}$, like $x(t)$, but the frequency is $-\tfrac{2\pi}{5}$.

Step 2 · The $-$ branch
$$\Omega'=-\Omega+2\pi k\iff T_s'=-T_s+kT=-3+15k\ \text{ms}$$

$k=0$ gives $-3$ ms (rejected), so $$T_s'=12,\ 27,\ 42,\ 57,\ldots\ \text{ms}$$

$$\Omega'=\tfrac{400\pi}{3}(0.012)=1.6\pi=-0.4\pi+2\pi$$

$x[n]=\cos(1.6\pi n-\tfrac{\pi}{8})=\cos(-0.4\pi n-\tfrac{\pi}{8})=\cos(0.4\pi n+\tfrac{\pi}{8})=x_3[n]$ ✓

$T_s=5$ ms ($\Omega=2\pi/3$) is in neither list: it gives a different signal.

PitfallsCommon Mistakes
Common mistakes

Six ways sampling goes wrong.

01 · UNITS
Seconds, not ms.
$\omega T_s$ with $T_s=3$ is wrong; use $0.003$ s.
02 · RANGE
$\omega T_s$ may exceed $\pi$.
Reduce mod $2\pi$; if $>\pi$ use $2\pi-\Omega$ and flip $\phi$.
03 · PERIOD
$N\neq T/T_s$ in general.
$T_s/T=\tfrac23$ gives $N=3$, not $1.5$.
04 · BRANCH
Two families: $\pm T_s+kT$.
The $-$ branch negates the phase.
05 · POSITIVITY
Keep only $T_s'>0$.
$-3+15k$ starts at $k=1$.
06 · SYMBOLS
$\omega$ rad/s, $\Omega$ rad/sample.
$f$ in Hz; never mix them in one formula.

Lecture 05Summary
Take with you

Four facts about $x[n] = x(n T_s)$.

01 · FORMULA
$x[n] = x(n T_s)$
Read $x(t)$ every $T_s$ seconds (use seconds).
02 · FREQUENCY
$\Omega = \omega T_s = 2\pi f/f_s$
DT frequency = phase advance per sample.
03 · PERIOD
$\Omega/2\pi = T_s/T = k/N$
$N$ samples span $k$ periods; $N$ is the lowest-terms denominator.
04 · EQUIVALENCE
$T_s' = \pm T_s + kT$
Different rates can give the same $x[n]$.
