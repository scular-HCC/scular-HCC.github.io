Sampling
Continuous-Time Sinusoids
Every $T_s$ seconds, we read off one value of $x(t)$. The resulting sequence is a DT sinusoid whose frequency is set by the ratio of the two periods.

ConceptThe Sampling Formula
From continuous to discrete

Read $x(t)$ every $T_s$ seconds.

$$x[n] \,=\, x(n T_s)$$
$T_s$
sampling period (sec)
$f_s = 1/T_s$
sampling rate (samples/sec)
$n$
integer sample index
Sampling a CT sinusoid
$$x(t) = \cos(\omega t + \phi) \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; x[n] = \cos(\omega T_s\, n + \phi)$$
ConceptENES-222 · L05
ConceptDT Frequency from Sampling
Discrete-time frequency

$\Omega$ is set by the ratio
of the two periods.

$$\Omega \,=\, \omega T_s \,=\, 2\pi\,\frac{f}{f_s} \,=\, 2\pi\,\frac{T_s}{T}$$

Three equivalent forms of the same statement: how many radians of phase advance happen between samples.

Small $T_s/T$ ⇒ small $\Omega$ ⇒ samples densely track $x(t)$.

PracticeSpecial Sampling Periods
Practice

Use $\Omega = 2\pi T_s/T$
to answer four questions.

  1. For what values of $T_s$ is $x[n]$ constant in $n$?
  2. For what values of $T_s$ does $x[n]$ alternate sign every sample, $\ldots,a,-a,a,-a,\ldots$?
  3. When is the resulting DT sinusoid periodic in $n$?
  4. What is the analogous condition on $T_s/T$?
PracticeENES-222 · L05
SolutionSpecial $T_s$
$x[n]$ constant
$$\Omega = 0 \;\Leftrightarrow\; T_s = kT$$

Sampling at integer multiples of the CT period — you keep landing at the same phase.

$x[n]$ alternates
$$\Omega = \pi \;\Leftrightarrow\; T_s = (k + \tfrac{1}{2})\,T$$

Sampling exactly between zero crossings flips the sign every sample.

Periodic in $n$
$$\frac{T_s}{T} = \frac{k}{N} \;\;\text{(rational)}$$

$\Omega/(2\pi)$ must be rational. The fundamental period is the denominator in lowest terms.

Equivalent rates
$$T_s \pm T_s' = kT$$

Two sampling periods producing the same DT signal differ by an integer multiple of $T$.

SolutionENES-222 · L05
ConceptEquivalent Sampling Rates
Equivalent sampling periods

Infinitely many $T_s$
produce the same $\Omega$.

$$\Omega' \,=\, \pm \Omega + 2k\pi \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; T_s \pm T_s' \,=\, k\,T$$

Combine the $2\pi$ equivalence and the $\Omega \leftrightarrow -\Omega$ symmetry from L04, then translate back into sampling periods.

This is exactly why undersampling causes ambiguity — and is the door to aliasing in the next lecture.

Worked ExampleAll $T_s$ producing $x_1$ and $x_3$
Worked example

List the sampling periods.

Continuous sinusoid (same as before):

$$x(t) \,=\, \cos\!\left(\tfrac{400\pi}{3}\, t - \tfrac{\pi}{8}\right)$$

so $T = 15.0$ ms and $f = 200/3$ Hz.

$x_1[n] = \cos(\tfrac{2\pi}{5}n - \tfrac{\pi}{8})$
$$T_s \,=\, \frac{\Omega}{\omega} \,=\, \frac{2\pi/5}{400\pi/3} \,=\, 3.0\ \text{ms}$$ $$T_s = 3.0 + 15.0\,k\ \text{ms},\;\; k=0,1,\ldots$$
$x_3[n] = \cos(\tfrac{2\pi}{5}n + \tfrac{\pi}{8})$

Use $\cos(-\mu) = \cos\mu$ to flip the sign of the frequency. Negative $T_s$ is illegal, so shift by integer $T$:

$$T_s = 12.0 + 15.0\,k\ \text{ms},\;\; k=1,2,\ldots$$

Solved ExampleTwo Fully-Solved Examples
Solved Example A · §1.6.2

Sample $x(t)=\cos(400\pi t/3)$
at $f_s=200$ Hz.

Find the discrete-time frequency $\Omega$.

Step 1 · Continuous-time frequency
$\omega=400\pi/3$ rad/s, so $f=\omega/2\pi=200/3\approx66.7$ Hz
Step 2 · Apply $\Omega=\omega T_s$
$$\Omega=\frac{400\pi}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{200}=\frac{2\pi}{3}\text{ rad/sample}$$
Step 3 · Discrete-time sinusoid
$$x[n]=\cos\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{3}n\right)$$
Formula

$\Omega=2\pi f/f_s$. Here $f/f_s=(200/3)/200=1/3$, so $\Omega=2\pi/3$.

Solved Example B · §1.6.2

Reconstruct from samples:
$x[n]=4.2\cos(0.75\pi n-0.3)$.

Known: $f\in[600,800]$ Hz, $f_s=400$ samples/s.

Step 1 · Find baseband frequency
$0.75\pi=2\pi f/400\Rightarrow f=0.375\times400=150$ Hz
Step 2 · Find alias in $[600,800]$ Hz
$f'=-150+k\cdot400$: $k=2\Rightarrow f'=650$ Hz $\in[600,800]\;\checkmark$
Step 3 · Reconstruct (phase flips above $f_s/2$)
$$x(t)=4.2\cos(2\pi\cdot650\cdot t+0.3)=4.2\cos(1300\pi t+0.3)$$

Lecture 05Summary
Take with you

Four facts about $x[n] = x(n T_s)$.

01 · FORMULA
$x[n] = x(n T_s)$
Read $x(t)$ every $T_s$ seconds.
02 · FREQUENCY
$\Omega = \omega T_s = 2\pi\, f/f_s$
DT frequency = phase advance per sample.
03 · EQUIVALENCE
$T_s \pm T_s' = kT$
Different rates can give the same $x[n]$.
04 · WHY IT MATTERS
$f_s$ too small ⇒ ambiguity.
