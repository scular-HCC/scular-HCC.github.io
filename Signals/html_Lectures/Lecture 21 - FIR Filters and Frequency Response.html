FIR Filters &
Frequency Response
A few coefficients $b_0, \ldots, b_M$ define an LTI system. Feed it a sinusoid — out comes the same sinusoid, scaled and phase-shifted by $H(e^{j\Omega})$.
Reading · FIR filters and frequency response
Lecture 21Objectives
By the end of this lecture you will

Read off the frequency response
of an FIR filter.

  1. Recognise the FIR input-output relationship $y[n] = \sum_{k=0}^{M} b_k\, x[n-k]$.
  2. Derive the frequency response $H(e^{j\Omega}) = \sum_k b_k\, e^{-j\Omega k}$.
  3. Extract amplitude $|H(e^{j\Omega})|$ and phase $\angle H(e^{j\Omega})$, especially for symmetric $b$.
  4. Compute $H$ at $N$ uniformly spaced frequencies by taking fft(b, N).

ConceptFIR Input-Output
FIR filter

A weighted moving sum
over $M+1$ recent samples.

$$y[n] \,=\, \sum_{k=0}^{M} b_k\, x[n-k]$$

$b = (b_0, b_1, \ldots, b_M)^{\top}$ — the coefficient vector.

Linear: superposition holds.
Time-invariant: delay in ⇒ same delay out.

Worked ExampleRunning the Filter by Hand
Worked example · direct computation

Compute the output by hand.

$b=(1,2,2,1)$, $M=3$: $y[n]=x[n]+2x[n-1]+2x[n-2]+x[n-3]$. Input $x[0]=1$, $x[1]=0$, $x[2]=2$, $x[3]=3$, and $x[n]=0$ for all other $n$.

One line per output sample
$$\begin{aligned}y[0]&=1+0+0+0=1\\y[1]&=x[1]+2x[0]=0+2=2\\y[2]&=x[2]+2x[1]+2x[0]=2+0+2=4\\y[3]&=x[3]+2x[2]+2x[1]+x[0]\\&=3+4+0+1=8\end{aligned}$$
Continue until the input runs out
$$\begin{aligned}y[4]&=2x[3]+2x[2]+x[1]=6+4+0=10\\y[5]&=2x[3]+x[2]=6+2=8\\y[6]&=x[3]=3\end{aligned}$$

Terms with $x[m]$, $m<0$ or $m>3$, are $0$. Also $y[n]=0$ for $n<0$ and $n\ge7$.

Check: $\sum y=(\sum b_k)(\sum x)=6\cdot6=36$ and $1+2+4+8+10+8+3=36$ ✓. Output length $4+M=7$.

ConceptComplex Exponentials Are Eigenfunctions
Sinusoid in, sinusoid out

$x[n] = e^{j\Omega n} \,\Rightarrow\, y[n] = H(e^{j\Omega})\, e^{j\Omega n}$.

$$H(e^{j\Omega}) \,=\, b_0 + b_1\, e^{-j\Omega} + \cdots + b_M\, e^{-j M\Omega}$$

The output is the same complex sinusoid, scaled by the complex factor $H(e^{j\Omega})$, which depends on $\Omega$ only.

Why: $x[n-k]=e^{j\Omega(n-k)}=e^{j\Omega n}\,e^{-j\Omega k}$, so $y[n]=\sum_{k=0}^{M} b_k e^{-j\Omega k}\,e^{j\Omega n}$. Here $\Omega$ (rad/sample) is the discrete-time frequency and $j=\sqrt{-1}$.

System function — anywhere in $\mathbb{C}$
$$H(z) \,=\, b_0 + b_1\, z^{-1} + \cdots + b_M\, z^{-M}$$

$z$ is any complex number; $H(e^{j\Omega})$ is just $H(z)$ on the unit circle $|z|=1$.

Worked ExampleEigen-Property with Numbers
Worked example · numerical check

Check the eigen-property.

$b=(1,2,2,1)$, $\Omega=\pi/2$, input $x[n]=e^{j\pi n/2}$: $x[0],\dots,x[4]=1,\ j,\ -1,\ -j,\ 1$.

Step 1 · Evaluate H

$e^{-j\pi/2}=-j$, $e^{-j\pi}=-1$, $e^{-j3\pi/2}=+j$:

$$H=1+2(-j)+2(-1)+j$$ $$=(1-2)+(-2j+j)=-1-j$$
Step 2 · Polar form

$|H|=\sqrt{1+1}=1.414$. Both parts negative: third quadrant, $\angle H=-135^\circ=-\tfrac{3\pi}{4}$ rad (not $-45^\circ$).

Step 3 · Compare with the closed form

$A(\pi/2)=4\cos\tfrac\pi4+2\cos\tfrac{3\pi}4=2.828-1.414=1.414>0$, so $H=1.414\,e^{-j3\pi/4}$ ✓.

Step 4 · Time-domain check
$$y[3]=x[3]+2x[2]+2x[1]+x[0]$$ $$=-j-2+2j+1=-1+j$$ $$H\,x[3]=(-1-j)(-j)=-1+j\ \checkmark$$ $$y[4]=1-2j-2+j=-1-j=H\,x[4]\ \checkmark$$

Worked ExampleSymmetric Coefficients
Worked example

Symmetric coefficients ⇒ linear phase.

$b=(1,2,2,1)^{\top}$, so $M=3$ and $b_k=b_{3-k}$. Pair equal coefficients and factor out the midpoint phase $e^{-j3\Omega/2}$ (Euler: $e^{j\theta}+e^{-j\theta}=2\cos\theta$).

$$H(e^{j\Omega}) = 1+2e^{-j\Omega}+2e^{-j2\Omega}+e^{-j3\Omega}$$
Outer pair
$$1+e^{-j3\Omega}=e^{-j3\Omega/2}\big(e^{j3\Omega/2}+e^{-j3\Omega/2}\big)=e^{-j3\Omega/2}\,2\cos\tfrac{3\Omega}{2}$$
Inner pair
$$2\big(e^{-j\Omega}+e^{-j2\Omega}\big)=2e^{-j3\Omega/2}\big(e^{j\Omega/2}+e^{-j\Omega/2}\big)=e^{-j3\Omega/2}\,4\cos\tfrac{\Omega}{2}$$
Sum
$$H(e^{j\Omega}) = e^{-j3\Omega/2}\Big(4\cos\tfrac{\Omega}{2}+2\cos\tfrac{3\Omega}{2}\Big)$$

Worked ExampleAmplitude, Phase, Nulls
Worked example · continued

Amplitude, phase, nulls.

Let $A(\Omega)=4\cos\tfrac{\Omega}{2}+2\cos\tfrac{3\Omega}{2}$, a real number, so $H=e^{-j3\Omega/2}A$ and $|e^{-j3\Omega/2}|=1$.

Amplitude response
$$|H(e^{j\Omega})|=|A(\Omega)|$$
Phase response
$$\angle H=-\tfrac{3\Omega}{2}\ \text{if } A>0,\qquad -\tfrac{3\Omega}{2}\pm\pi\ \text{if } A<0$$

A negative real factor is $e^{\pm j\pi}$ times its magnitude.

Factor A

Put $c=\cos(\Omega/2)$. Triple-angle identity $\cos 3\theta=4\cos^3\theta-3\cos\theta$ with $\theta=\Omega/2$:

$$\begin{aligned}A&=4c+2(4c^3-3c)=8c^3-2c\\&=2c(2c-1)(2c+1)\end{aligned}$$
Zeros in [0, 2π)

Here $\Omega/2\in[0,\pi)$:
$c=0\Rightarrow\Omega=\pi$
$c=\tfrac12\Rightarrow\Omega/2=\pi/3\Rightarrow\Omega=2\pi/3$
$c=-\tfrac12\Rightarrow\Omega/2=2\pi/3\Rightarrow\Omega=4\pi/3$

Check

$\Omega=0$: $c=1$, $A=2\cdot1\cdot1\cdot3=6=\sum b_k$. $A$ changes sign at each zero, giving three $\pi$ jumps in the phase.

IntuitionLinear Phase Means Delay
Intuition · what the phase does

Linear phase is a delay.

Real input

$b$ real, so $x[n]=\cos(\Omega n)$ gives $y[n]=|H|\cos(\Omega n+\angle H)$.

At Omega = pi/8
$$y[n]=5.586\cos\big(\tfrac{\pi n}8-\tfrac{3\pi}{16}\big)=5.586\cos\big(\tfrac\pi8(n-1.5)\big)$$

$\tfrac{3\pi}{16}=\tfrac\pi8\cdot1.5$: the cosine is the same one, delayed by $M/2=1.5$ samples and scaled by $5.586$.

Bottom curve is $y[n]/|H|$. Peaks sit at $n\approx17.5$ instead of $16$. The first $M=3$ outputs (gray) are start-up while the filter fills.
IntuitionENES-222 · L21
ConceptSymmetry of $H$
Real coefficients

$|H|$ even, $\angle H$ odd
about $\Omega = 0$ and $\pi$.

$$H(e^{-j\Omega}) \,=\, H^{*}(e^{j\Omega})$$

Why: $b_k$ real $\Rightarrow$ $\big(b_k e^{-j\Omega k}\big)^{*}=b_k e^{j\Omega k}$, so $H^{*}(e^{j\Omega})=H(e^{-j\Omega})$. Magnitudes of conjugates agree and angles negate: $|H|$ even, $\angle H$ odd. Like the DFT of a real signal, so plotting $[0,\pi]$ suffices.

Also $e^{-j(\Omega+2\pi)k}=e^{-j\Omega k}$ for integer $k$, so $H(e^{j(\Omega + 2\pi)}) = H(e^{j\Omega})$: period $2\pi$.

ConceptENES-222 · L21
ConceptSample $H$ via FFT
Computing $H$ on a grid

$\mathtt{fft(b, N)}$ samples $H(e^{j\Omega})$
at $N$ equally spaced frequencies.

$$H(e^{j\,2\pi k/N}) \,=\, [\mathrm{DFT}\ \text{of}\ (b;\, 0_{N-M-1})][k]$$

Why: padded DFT is $\sum_{m=0}^{N-1} b_m e^{-j2\pi km/N}$ with $b_m=0$ for $m>M$; that is $H$ at $\Omega_k=2\pi k/N$, $k=0,\ldots,N-1$. Pad $b$ to length $N \geq M+1$.

For smooth plots, take $N \gg M$ — zero-padding is interpolation here too.

H = fft(b, 512);
A = abs(H); q = angle(H);

SolutionJust Evaluate H(z)
Why plug in z

$x[n-k]=z^{n-k}=z^{-k}z^{n}$, so $y[n]=\sum_{k=0}^{3} b_k z^{-k}\,z^{n}=H(z)\,z^{n}$.

Case 1 · z = 3
$$\begin{aligned}H(3)&=1+2\cdot3^{-1}+2\cdot3^{-2}+3^{-3}\\&=1+\tfrac{2}{3}+\tfrac{2}{9}+\tfrac{1}{27}=\tfrac{27+18+6+1}{27}=\tfrac{52}{27}\end{aligned}$$ $$y[n]=\tfrac{52}{27}\,3^{n}$$
Case 2 · z = −3
$$H(-3)=1+2(-3)^{-1}+2(-3)^{-2}+(-3)^{-3}$$ $$=1-\tfrac{2}{3}+\tfrac{2}{9}-\tfrac{1}{27}=\tfrac{27-18+6-1}{27}=\tfrac{14}{27}$$ $$y[n]=\tfrac{14}{27}\,(-3)^{n}$$

Powers: $(-3)^{-1}=-\tfrac13$, $(-3)^{-2}=\tfrac19$, $(-3)^{-3}=-\tfrac1{27}$.

SolutionJust Evaluate H(z)
Case 3 · z = 1/3
$$H(\tfrac13)=1+2\cdot3+2\cdot3^{2}+3^{3}$$ $$=1+6+18+27=52$$ $$y[n]=52\,(1/3)^{n}$$

Since $(1/3)^{-k}=3^{k}$.

Lesson

$H(z)$ delivers the response to any exponential — real or complex, growing or decaying. Plug in $z$, multiply.

Check at n = 3 (direct sum)

$y[3]=x[3]+2x[2]+2x[1]+x[0]$

$z=3$: $27+18+6+1=52=\tfrac{52}{27}\cdot27$ ✓

$z=-3$: $-27+18-6+1=-14=\tfrac{14}{27}\cdot(-27)$ ✓

$z=\tfrac13$: $\tfrac1{27}+\tfrac29+\tfrac23+1=\tfrac{52}{27}=52\cdot\tfrac1{27}$ ✓

Solved ExampleTwo Fully-Solved Examples
Solved Example A

Find the frequency response.

$$y[n]=x[n]-x[n-1]+x[n-2]$$
Step 1 · Read the coefficients

Coefficient of $x[n-k]$ is $b_k$: $b=(1,-1,1)$, $M=2$.

$$H(e^{j\Omega})=1-e^{-j\Omega}+e^{-j2\Omega}$$
Step 2 · Factor out the delay term
$=e^{-j\Omega}\big(e^{j\Omega}-1+e^{-j\Omega}\big)$ since $e^{-j\Omega}e^{j\Omega}=1$, $e^{-j\Omega}e^{-j\Omega}=e^{-j2\Omega}$. Euler: $e^{j\Omega}+e^{-j\Omega}=2\cos\Omega$, so $$H(e^{j\Omega})=e^{-j\Omega}(2\cos\Omega-1)$$ $$|H(e^{j\Omega})|=|2\cos\Omega-1|$$

$|e^{-j\Omega}|=1$ and $2\cos\Omega-1$ is real.

Step 3 · Key values
$\Omega=0$: $2\cos0-1=1$, so $|H|=1$.
$\Omega=\pi/3$: $2\cdot\tfrac12-1=0$, so $|H|=0$ (null).
$\Omega=\pi$: $2(-1)-1=-3$, so $|H|=3$.

PitfallsCommon Mistakes
Common mistakes

Four traps with $H$.

  1. Wrong powers. $H(z)=b_0+b_1z^{-1}+\cdots$ uses negative powers of $z$; $H(e^{j\Omega})$ has $e^{-j\Omega k}$.
  2. $|H|$ is not $A$. $A(\Omega)$ can be negative: $|H|=|A|$, and a negative $A$ adds $\pm\pi$ to the phase (3-point average at $\Omega=\pi$).
  3. Quadrant of the angle. $H=-1-j$ has $\angle H=-135^\circ$, not $-45^\circ$: look at both signs.
  4. fft index is not frequency. fft(b,N) gives $H$ at $\Omega_k=2\pi k/N$, $k=0,\dots,N-1$; pad to $N\ge M+1$ (use $N\gg M$ for a smooth plot).
