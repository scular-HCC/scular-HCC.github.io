Shift, Modulation,

Shifting in one domain modulates the other. Every DFT pair has a free dual — buy one, get one.

ConceptDFT 5 — Time Shift
DFT 5 · Time shift

$P^{m} x \,\leftrightarrow\, F^{-m} X$

$$Y[k] \,=\, e^{-j(2\pi/N) m k}\, X[k]$$

Each bin picks up a phase factor — linear in $k$ — proportional to the shift.

Magnitude spectrum is unchanged: $|Y[k]| = |X[k]|$. A shift in time only rotates the phase.

$F^{-m} = F^{N-m}$ — equivalent statement.

ConceptENES-222 · L15
ConceptDFT 6 — Modulation
DFT 6 · Modulation

$F^{m} x \,\leftrightarrow\, P^{m} X$

$$y[n] = e^{j(2\pi/N) m n}\,x[n] \;\Rightarrow\; Y = P^{m} X$$

The dual of DFT 5: multiply each time sample by the $m$-th Fourier sinusoid ⇒ circularly shift the spectrum by $m$.

For $m = N/2$: alternating sign flip $(-1)^{n}$ swaps the two halves of $X$.

Worked ExampleThree Derived Pairs
Worked example

Given one pair,
derive three more.

Given · $N = 6$
$$(a, b, c, d, e, f)^{\top} \,\leftrightarrow\, (A, B, C, D, E, F)^{\top}$$
Circular shift by 3 (time)
$$(d,e,f,a,b,c)^{\top} \,\leftrightarrow\, (A,-B,C,-D,E,-F)^{\top}$$

$F^{-3}$ at $N=6$ has alternating signs.

Modulate by $(-1)^{n}$ (time)
$$(a,-b,c,-d,e,-f)^{\top} \,\leftrightarrow\, (D,E,F,A,B,C)^{\top}$$

$P^{3}$ swaps halves.

ConceptDFT 7 — Duality
DFT 7 · Duality

Swap domains.
Reverse and scale by $N$.

$$x \,\leftrightarrow\, X \;\Longrightarrow\; X \,\leftrightarrow\, N\,R\,x$$

Take the spectrum, treat it as a time signal — its DFT is just the original signal circularly reversed and multiplied by $N$.

Every DFT theorem comes with a dual partner — derive one, get the other for free.

One DFT, two pairs.

Given
$$(a,b,c,d,e,f)^{\top} \,\leftrightarrow\, (3,\, 1-j,\, 2+j,\, -1,\, 2-j,\, 1+j)^{\top}$$
By DFT 7

Read off the dual pair: time signal becomes the spectrum, spectrum becomes the reversed-and-scaled time signal.

$$(3,\, 1-j,\, 2+j,\, -1,\, 2-j,\, 1+j)^{\top}$$ $$\quad\leftrightarrow\quad 6\,(a,\, f,\, e,\, d,\, c,\, b)^{\top}$$

ConceptReal & Symmetric
Mutual implications

Real ⟷ circular symmetry,
by domain.

REAL TIME
$x = x^{*}$
⇔ $X$ circularly conjugate-symmetric.
REAL SPECTRUM
$X = X^{*}$
⇔ $x$ circularly conjugate-symmetric.
BOTH REAL & SYM
$x = x^{*} = R x$
⇔ same holds for $X$.
DUAL FACT
$x = R x^{*}$
⇔ $X = X^{*}$.

SolutionBoth Real and Symmetric
Solution

Real and even ⇒ real cosines, no phase.

Symmetry check
$$x[1] = 3 = x[7],\;\; x[2] = 2 = x[6],\;\; x[3] = 1 = x[5]\;\checkmark$$
By the implications

$x$ real and $x = R x$ ⇒ $X$ real and $X = R X$.

Cosine representation

With $N = 8$ there are five Fourier frequencies in $[0, \pi]$: $0, \tfrac{\pi}{4}, \tfrac{\pi}{2}, \tfrac{3\pi}{4}, \pi$. All five $X[k]$ are real and have zero phase (or $\pi$).

So $x$ is a sum of five cosines at those frequencies, each with phase $0$ or $\pi$ — no sine terms, no general phase.

This is the discrete analogue of an even continuous function having a pure cosine Fourier series.
