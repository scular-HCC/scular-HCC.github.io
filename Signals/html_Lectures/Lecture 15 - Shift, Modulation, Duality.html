Shift, Modulation,
& Duality
Shifting in one domain modulates the other. Every DFT pair has a free dual: buy one, get one.
Lecture 15Objectives
By the end of this lecture you will

Trade shifts for modulations,
and pairs for duals.

  1. Apply DFT 5 — circular shift in time ⇔ modulation in frequency: $P^{m} x \leftrightarrow F^{-m} X$.
  2. Apply DFT 6 — modulation in time ⇔ circular shift in frequency: $F^{m} x \leftrightarrow P^{m} X$.
  3. Use the duality DFT 7: $X \leftrightarrow N R x$.
  4. Recognise when a signal is both real and circularly symmetric — its DFT is too.

ConceptDFT 5 — Time Shift
DFT 5 · Time shift

$P^{m} x \,\leftrightarrow\, F^{-m} X$

$$Y[k] \,=\, e^{-j(2\pi/N)\, m k}\, X[k]$$

Each bin picks up a phase factor, linear in $k$. The magnitude is unchanged: $|Y[k]| = |X[k]|$.

$F^{-m} = F^{N-m}$ is the same statement.

Result
$$Y[k]=e^{-j2\pi mk/N}X[k]=(F^{-m}X)[k]$$
Setup
$y=P^{m}x$ means $y[n]=x[(n-m)\bmod N]$, and $X[k]=\sum_{n}x[n]e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$.
Substitute r equals n minus m
$$Y[k]=\sum_{n}x[n-m]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$$ $$=e^{-j2\pi km/N}\sum_{r}x[r]\,e^{-j2\pi kr/N}$$

The summand is $N$-periodic in $r$, so the wrap-around does not change the sum.

ConceptDFT 6 — Modulation
DFT 6 · Modulation

$F^{m} x \,\leftrightarrow\, P^{m} X$

$$y[n] = e^{j(2\pi/N) m n}\,x[n]$$ $$\Rightarrow\; Y = P^{m} X$$

The dual of DFT 5: multiply by the $m$-th Fourier sinusoid in time $\Rightarrow$ circularly shift the spectrum down by $m$.

For $m = N/2$ the factor is $(-1)^{n}$ and the two halves of $X$ swap.

Transform
$$Y[k]=\sum_{n}e^{j2\pi mn/N}x[n]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$$
Combine the exponentials
$$Y[k]=\sum_{n}x[n]\,e^{-j2\pi(k-m)n/N}$$ $$=X[(k-m)\bmod N]$$
Read as a matrix
$(P^{m}X)[k]=X[(k-m)\bmod N]$, since $P^{m}$ moves every entry down $m$ slots. So $Y=P^{m}X$.

Given one pair, derive three more.

Given
$$(a, b, c, d, e, f)^{\top} \,\leftrightarrow\, (A, B, C, D, E, \mathrm{F})^{\top}$$

Pair 1 · shift by 3 in time
$P^{3}x=(d,e,f,a,b,c)^{\top}$. DFT 5 gives $F^{-3}X$, with diagonal $e^{-j3(2\pi/6)k}=e^{-j\pi k}=(-1)^{k}$: $$(d,e,f,a,b,c)^{\top}\leftrightarrow(A,-B,C,-D,E,-\mathrm{F})^{\top}$$

ConceptDFT 7 — Duality
DFT 7 · Duality

Swap domains. Reverse and scale by $N$.

$$x \,\leftrightarrow\, X \;\Longrightarrow\; X \,\leftrightarrow\, N\,R\,x$$

Treat the spectrum as a time signal: its DFT is the original signal circularly reversed and multiplied by $N$. Every DFT theorem therefore has a dual partner.

Key fact
Columns of $V$ are orthogonal with squared length $N$, so $VV^{*}=NI$. Since $V^{*}=VR$: $VVR=NI$, hence $VV=NR$ (multiply by $R$). Conjugate ($R$ is real): $WW=NR$.
Apply it
$$Y=W X=W(Wx)=WW\,x=N\,R\,x$$

Worked ExampleDual example: check one entry
Worked example with N = 6 (cont.)

Is $f=\tfrac12$? Check by hand.

What duality says
$X\leftrightarrow 6Rx$, so bin 1 of the DFT of $X$ equals $6\,(Rx)[1]=6\,x[5]=6f$: $$6f=\sum_{k=0}^{5}X[k]\,e^{-j\pi k/3}$$
Pair the conjugate bins
$X[6-k]e^{-j\pi(6-k)/3}=\overline{X[k]\,e^{-j\pi k/3}}$, so the pairs add to $2\,\mathrm{Re}$.
Evaluate
$$\begin{aligned}k=0&:\;3\\k=1,5&:\;2\,\mathrm{Re}\big[(1-j)(\tfrac12-j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2})\big]=1-\sqrt3\\k=2,4&:\;2\,\mathrm{Re}\big[(2+j)(-\tfrac12-j\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2})\big]=-2+\sqrt3\\k=3&:\;(-1)(-1)=1\end{aligned}$$
Total
$$6f=3+(1-\sqrt3)+(-2+\sqrt3)+1=3\;\Rightarrow\;f=\tfrac12\;\checkmark$$

Full signal (numpy): $x=(\tfrac43,\,\tfrac12,\,0.411,\,1,\,-0.744,\,\tfrac12)$, real as predicted.

ConceptStandard pairs and their duals
Duality as a lookup table

Learn one column, get the other.

Pairs for any N
$$\begin{array}{c|c}x&X\\\hline e^{(0)}=\delta&\mathbf 1\\\mathbf 1&N\,e^{(0)}\\P^{m}e^{(0)}=e^{(m)}&F^{-m}\mathbf 1\\v^{(m)}=F^{m}\mathbf 1&N\,e^{(m)}\end{array}$$
Why each row
Row 1: only $n=0$ survives the sum. Row 2: DFT 7 with $R e^{(0)}=e^{(0)}$. Row 3: DFT 5. Row 4: DFT 6 applied to row 2: $P^{m}(N e^{(0)})=N e^{(m)}$.
Check with N = 4, m = 1
$v^{(1)}=(1,j,-1,-j)$: $X[k]=\sum_n j^{n}(-j)^{kn}$ is $4$ at $k=1$ (every term is 1) and $0$ elsewhere, e.g. $X[0]=1+j-1-j=0$ ✓

Dual theorems: DFT 5 (shift to modulation) and DFT 6 (modulation to shift) are partners; DFT 3 and 4 are their own duals.

ConceptReal & Symmetric
Mutual implications

Real ⟷ circular symmetry,
by domain.

REAL TIME
$x = x^{*}$
⇔ $X$ circularly conjugate-symmetric.
REAL SPECTRUM
$X = X^{*}$
⇔ $x = R x^{*}$ (circularly conjugate-symmetric).
BOTH REAL & SYM
$x = x^{*} = R x$
⇔ same holds for $X$.
SPECIAL CASE
Real and even $x$
⇒ $X$ real and even: cosines only.
ConceptENES-222 · L15
ConceptWhy the implications hold
Three lines each

Proof of the real and symmetric facts.

Real signal gives a conjugate-symmetric spectrum
$x=x^{*}\Rightarrow X=\mathrm{DFT}(x^{*})=RX^{*}$ (DFT 3).
Real spectrum gives a conjugate-symmetric signal
Apply duality to $X=X^{*}$: $NRx=\mathrm{DFT}(X)=\mathrm{DFT}(X^{*})$. DFT 3 on the signal $X$ gives $\mathrm{DFT}(X^{*})=R\,\mathrm{DFT}(X)^{*}=R\,(NRx)^{*}=N\,x^{*}$. So $NRx=Nx^{*}$ and $x=Rx^{*}$.
Real and symmetric in both domains
$x=x^{*}=Rx$ gives $X=RX^{*}$ and $X=RX$ (DFT 3, DFT 4). Hence $RX=RX^{*}$, so $X=X^{*}$: real. Also $X=RX$: symmetric.
Quick test, N = 4
$x=(3,1,0,1)$ is real and $Rx=(3,1,0,1)$. $X=(5,3,1,3)$: real, and $X[1]=X[3]$ ✓

SolutionReal and Symmetric: X
Solution

Real and even ⇒ real spectrum.

Symmetry check
$$\begin{aligned}x[1]=3&=x[7]\\x[2]=2&=x[6]\\x[3]=1&=x[5]\;\checkmark\end{aligned}$$
By the implications
$x$ is real ($x=x^{*}$) and $x=Rx$, so $X$ is real and $X=RX$.
Compute X explicitly
Pair $n$ with $8-n$: since $x[8-n]=x[n]$, $e^{-j\theta n}+e^{-j\theta(8-n)}=2\cos(\theta n)$ with $\theta=2\pi k/8$. Entry 0 is $-1$; entry 4 is $4\,e^{-j\pi k}=4(-1)^{k}$. $$\begin{aligned}X[k]=&-1+4(-1)^{k}\\&+2\Big[3\cos\tfrac{\pi k}{4}+2\cos\tfrac{\pi k}{2}+\cos\tfrac{3\pi k}{4}\Big]\end{aligned}$$

Solved Example A · modulation by (-1)^n

DFT of $(-1)^n x[n]$, $N=8$.

Let $x\leftrightarrow X$ be any length-8 DFT pair, and $y[n]=(-1)^{n}x[n]$. Find $Y$.

Key insight

Multiplying by $(-1)^n$ shifts the spectrum by $N/2$: low frequencies move to high frequencies.

Step 1 · identify the factor
$$(-1)^n=e^{j\pi n}=e^{j(2\pi/8)\,4\,n}=v^{(4)}[n]$$ the $k=4$ Fourier sinusoid for $N=8$. So $y=F^{4}x$.
Step 2 · apply DFT 6
$$Y=P^{4}X,\qquad Y[k]=X[(k-4)\bmod 8]$$
Step 3 · direct check
$$Y[k]=\sum_n x[n]e^{j2\pi 4n/8}e^{-j2\pi kn/8}$$ $$=\sum_n x[n]e^{-j2\pi(k-4)n/8}=X[k-4]$$

Solved Example B · all-ones vector

DFT of the all-ones vector $\mathbf{1}$, by duality.

$\mathbf{e}^{(0)}=(1,0,\dots,0)^\top$ is the unit impulse, $\mathbf{e}^{(0)}[n]=\delta[n]$.

Check directly
$$X[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}e^{-j2\pi kn/N}=\begin{cases}N,&k=0\\\dfrac{1-e^{-j2\pi k}}{1-e^{-j2\pi k/N}}=0,&k\neq0\end{cases}$$
Step 1 · DFT of the impulse
$$X[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\delta[n]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N}=e^{0}=1\quad\forall k$$ Only $n=0$ survives, so $\mathbf{e}^{(0)}\leftrightarrow\mathbf{1}$.
Step 2 · apply duality (DFT 7)
If $x\leftrightarrow X$ then $X\leftrightarrow NRx$. With $x=\mathbf{e}^{(0)}$, $X=\mathbf{1}$: $$\mathbf{1}\leftrightarrow N\,R\,\mathbf{e}^{(0)}$$
Step 3 · reverse the impulse
$R$ fixes entry 0, so $R\mathbf{e}^{(0)}=\mathbf{e}^{(0)}$: $$\text{DFT of }\mathbf{1}=(N,0,0,\ldots,0)^\top\;\checkmark$$

Common mistakesShift, modulation, duality
Common mistakes

Four ways to lose points.

  1. Wrong sign of the exponent. A delay by $m$ gives $F^{-m}$ (factor $e^{-j2\pi mk/N}$), not $F^{m}$. Test with $m=1$, $N=4$: bin 1 is multiplied by $-j$.
  2. Mixing DFT 5 and DFT 6. Shift in time modulates the spectrum; modulate in time shifts the spectrum. Magnitudes survive a time shift, not a modulation.
  3. Forgetting $N$ and $R$ in duality. The DFT of $X$ is $N\,Rx$: scaled by $N$ and reversed with index 0 fixed. Check bin 0: $\sum_k X[k]=N\,x[0]$.
  4. Calling a signal symmetric because it looks even. Circular symmetry means $x[N-n]=x[n]$ with $x[0]$ alone in its place.
