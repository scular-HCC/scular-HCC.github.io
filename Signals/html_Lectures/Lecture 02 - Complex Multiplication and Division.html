Geometry, Multiplication
& Division on $\mathbb{C}$
Lines and circles described by complex equations. Multiplying and dividing complex numbers — and why polar form makes both effortless.
Lecture 02Objectives
By the end of this lecture you will

Multiply, divide, and reason
geometrically about $z_1$ and $z_2$.

  1. Recognize $|z-z_0|=a$ as a circle and $|z-z_1|=|z-z_2|$ as a perpendicular bisector.
  2. Multiply complex numbers in Cartesian form using $j^2=-1$, and in polar form by combining moduli and angles.
  3. Divide by multiplying numerator and denominator by the conjugate.
  4. Use the identities $z\,z^{*}=|z|^{2}$ and $z^{-1}=z^{*}/|z|^{2}$.

ConceptModulus as Distance
Modulus as distance

$|z_1 - z_2|$ is the distance
between two points on $\mathbb{C}$.

Circle
$$|z - z_0| = a$$

All points at fixed distance $a$ from $z_0$.

Perpendicular bisector
$$|z - z_1| = |z - z_2|$$

All points equidistant from $z_1$ and $z_2$.

Why distance?

With $z_k=x_k+jy_k$: $\;z_1-z_2=(x_1-x_2)+j(y_1-y_2)$, so by Pythagoras $|z_1-z_2|=\sqrt{(x_1-x_2)^2+(y_1-y_2)^2}$. In particular $|z-z_0|=a$ reads $(x-x_0)^2+(y-y_0)^2=a^2$.

SolutionCurves A & B
Solution — algebra

One circle, one line.

Curve A · rewrite
$$z-3+4j \;=\; z-(3-4j)$$

Adding $4j$ is subtracting $-4j$.

Curve A · read off

$|z-z_0|=5$ with $z_0=3-4j$: a circle, centre $(3,-4)$, radius $5$. In $x,y$: $(x-3)^2+(y+4)^2=25$.

Curve A · check
$$|0-(3-4j)|=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5$$

So the circle passes through the origin.

Curve B · square both sides
$$(x-3)^2+y^2=x^2+(y+4)^2$$ $$x^2-6x+9+y^2=x^2+y^2+8y+16$$
Curve B · simplify
$$-6x-8y=7\;\Longrightarrow\;3x+4y=-3.5$$

A line, slope $-\tfrac34$. Its normal $(3,4)$ is parallel to the segment from $-4j$ to $3$, so it is the perpendicular bisector.

Curve B · check midpoint
$$\tfrac{3+(-4j)}{2}=1.5-2j:\;\;3(1.5)+4(-2)=-3.5\;\checkmark$$

ConceptMultiplication · Cartesian
Cartesian product

Distribute, then collapse $j^{2} = -1$.

$$z \,=\, x + jy, \qquad j^{2} = -1$$

Here $j=\sqrt{-1}$. Just like with real polynomials, FOIL through the product and collect real and imaginary parts.

Every $j^{2}$ becomes $-1$ — that's the only new rule.

General rule
$$(a+jb)(c+jd)=ac+jad+jbc+j^{2}bd=(ac-bd)+j\,(ad+bc)$$
Example: a = 5, b = −2, c = 3, d = −4
$$(5-2j)(3-4j)=\underbrace{15}_{5\cdot3}\underbrace{-20j}_{5\cdot(-4j)}\underbrace{-6j}_{(-2j)\cdot3}\underbrace{+8j^{2}}_{(-2j)(-4j)}=15-26j-8=7-26j$$

ConceptMultiplication · Polar
Polar product

Multiply moduli.
Add angles.

$$|z_1 z_2| \,=\, |z_1|\,|z_2|$$
$$\angle (z_1 z_2) \,=\, \angle z_1 + \angle z_2$$

Multiplying by a complex number of modulus $1$ is therefore a pure rotation of the plane.

Why: write z_k = r_k (cos θ_k + j sin θ_k)
$$z_1z_2=r_1r_2\big[(\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2-\sin\theta_1\sin\theta_2)+j(\cos\theta_1\sin\theta_2+\sin\theta_1\cos\theta_2)\big]\quad(j^{2}=-1)$$ $$=r_1r_2\big[\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)+j\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)\big]\quad\text{(angle-sum identities)}$$

Worked ExampleTwo ways to the same product
Worked example

Multiply $z_1 = 5 - 2j$ and $z_2 = 3 - 4j$.

Cartesian
$$z_1 z_2 = 15-20j-6j+8j^{2}$$ $$= 15-26j-8 = 7 - 26j$$
Polar inputs (real parts positive: no π added)
$$|z_1| = \sqrt{25+4}=\sqrt{29}$$ $$\angle z_1 = \arctan\tfrac{-2}{5}= \arctan(-0.4)= -0.3805$$ $$|z_2| = \sqrt{9+16}=5$$ $$\angle z_2 = \arctan\tfrac{-4}{3}= -0.9273$$
Polar product
$$|z_1 z_2| = 5\sqrt{29}\approx 26.926$$ $$\angle (z_1 z_2) = -0.3805-0.9273 = -1.3078$$
Back to Cartesian
$$\cos(-1.3078)=0.2600,\;\sin(-1.3078)=-0.9656$$ $$26.926\,(0.2600)=7.00$$ $$26.926\,(-0.9656)=-26.00$$

Both ways give $7-26j$.

ConceptConjugate & Modulus²
The conjugate

$z^{*}$ flips the imaginary part —
and the angle.

$$z\,z^{*} \,=\, |z|^{2}$$

If $z = x + jy$, then $z^{*} = x - jy$.

In polar form, $|z^{*}| = |z|$ and $\angle z^{*} = -\angle z$ — reflection across the real axis.

The product $z\,z^{*}$ is therefore real and non-negative.

Why $z\,z^{*}=|z|^{2}$, and a number check
$$(x+jy)(x-jy)=x^{2}-jxy+jxy-j^{2}y^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}=|z|^{2},\qquad (3-4j)(3+4j)=9+16=25=5^{2}$$

ConceptDivision
Division — Cartesian

Multiply top and bottom
by the denominator's conjugate.

$$\frac{z_1}{z_2} \,=\, \frac{z_1\,z_2^{*}}{|z_2|^{2}}$$

The denominator becomes real, so the rest is just real-division on the real and imaginary parts of the numerator.

Example
$$\frac{5 - 2j}{3 - 4j} \,=\, \frac{(5 - 2j)(3 + 4j)}{3^{2} + 4^{2}} \,=\, \frac{23+14j}{25} \,=\, 0.92+0.56j$$

Numerator: $15+20j-6j-8j^{2}=23+14j$. Check: $(0.92+0.56j)(3-4j)=2.76-3.68j+1.68j+2.24=5-2j$.

ConceptENES-222 · L02
ConceptInverse
Inverse in polar form

Invert the modulus.
Negate the angle.

$$z^{-1} \,=\, \frac{z^{*}}{|z|^{2}}$$
$$|z^{-1}| = |z|^{-1},\;\; \angle z^{-1} = -\angle z$$

For division in polar form:

$$\left|\frac{z_1}{z_2}\right| = \frac{|z_1|}{|z_2|}, \qquad \angle\!\frac{z_1}{z_2} = \angle z_1 - \angle z_2.$$

SolutionConjugate and Inverse
Solution

One point, two reflections.

Polar form (no π needed)
$$|z| = \sqrt{3^{2} + 4^{2}} = 5,\;\; \angle z = \arctan\tfrac{-4}{3}= -0.9273$$
Conjugate
$$z^{*} = 3 + 4j,\;\; |z^{*}| = 5,\;\; \angle z^{*} = +0.9273$$
Inverse
$$z^{-1} = \frac{z^{*}}{|z|^{2}} = \frac{3 + 4j}{25} = 0.12 + 0.16\,j$$ $$|z^{-1}| = \sqrt{0.12^2+0.16^2}=\sqrt{0.04}=\tfrac{1}{5}$$ $$\angle z^{-1} = \arctan\tfrac{0.16}{0.12}=+0.9273$$

Solved ExampleTwo Fully-Solved Examples
Solved Example A · both factors left of the imaginary axis

Multiply $z_1=-2+2j$ by $z_2=-1-j\sqrt{3}$.

Step 1 · Cartesian (FOIL)
$$(-2+2j)(-1-j\sqrt3)=2+2\sqrt3\,j-2j-2\sqrt3\,j^{2}$$ $$=(2+2\sqrt3)+j(2\sqrt3-2)\approx5.464+1.464j$$
Step 2 · Polar form of each factor
$$|z_1|=\sqrt{4+4}=2\sqrt2,\quad \angle z_1=\arctan(-1)+\pi=\tfrac{3\pi}{4}$$ $$|z_2|=\sqrt{1+3}=2,\quad \angle z_2=\arctan(\sqrt3)+\pi=\tfrac{4\pi}{3}$$

Both real parts are negative, so $\pi$ is added each time.

Step 3 · Multiply moduli, add angles
$$|z_1z_2|=2\sqrt2\cdot2=4\sqrt2\approx5.657$$ $$\tfrac{3\pi}{4}+\tfrac{4\pi}{3}=\tfrac{25\pi}{12}=2\pi+\tfrac{\pi}{12}\;\Rightarrow\;15^{\circ}$$
Step 4 · Check against Step 1 ✓
$$5.657(0.9659)=5.464,\qquad 5.657(0.2588)=1.464$$
FIG. · $135^{\circ}+240^{\circ}=375^{\circ}$: one full turn plus $15^{\circ}$.

Solved Example B · multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator

Divide $z_1=\sqrt{3}-j$ by $z_2=1+j$.

Step 1 · Numerator
$$(\sqrt{3}-j)(1-j)=\sqrt{3}-j\sqrt{3}-j+j^2$$ $$=(\sqrt{3}-1)-j(\sqrt{3}+1)$$
Step 2 · Denominator and quotient
$$(1-j)(1+j)=1-j^2=2$$ $$\frac{z_1}{z_2}=\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2}-j\,\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2}\approx0.366-1.366j$$
Step 3 · Polar check
$$|z_1|=2,\;\angle z_1=\arctan\tfrac{-1}{\sqrt3}=-\tfrac{\pi}{6}$$ $$|z_2|=\sqrt2,\;\angle z_2=\arctan 1=\tfrac{\pi}{4}$$ $$|z_1/z_2|=\tfrac{2}{\sqrt2}=\sqrt2,\;\angle=-\tfrac{\pi}{6}-\tfrac{\pi}{4}=-\tfrac{5\pi}{12}$$

Both have $x>0$, so no $\pi$ is added. Division rule: divide moduli, subtract angles.

Step 4 · Back to Cartesian
$$\sqrt2\cos75^{\circ}=0.366$$ $$-\sqrt2\sin75^{\circ}=-1.366\;\checkmark$$

Lecture 02Summary
Take with you

Four rules for $z_1$ and $z_2$.

01 · GEOMETRY
$|z-z_0|=a$ is a circle.
$|z-z_1|=|z-z_2|$ is the perpendicular bisector.
02 · MULTIPLY
Cartesian: FOIL, use $j^{2} = -1$.
Polar: multiply moduli, add angles.
03 · CONJUGATE
$z\,z^{*} = |z|^{2}$ — always real.
$z^{*}$ flips the imaginary part and the angle.
04 · DIVIDE / INVERT
$z^{-1} = z^{*}/|z|^{2}$
In polar: divide moduli, subtract angles.
