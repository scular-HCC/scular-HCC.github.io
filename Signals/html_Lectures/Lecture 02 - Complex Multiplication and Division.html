Geometry, Multiplication
& Division on $\mathbb{C}$
Lines and circles described by complex equations. Multiplying and dividing complex numbers — and why polar form makes both effortless.
Reading · Textbook §1.3
Lecture 02Objectives
By the end of this lecture you will

Multiply, divide, and reason
geometrically about $z_1$ and $z_2$.

  1. Recognize $|z-z_0|=a$ as a circle and $|z-z_1|=|z-z_2|$ as a perpendicular bisector.
  2. Multiply complex numbers in Cartesian form using $j^2=-1$, and in polar form by combining moduli and angles.
  3. Divide by multiplying numerator and denominator by the conjugate.
  4. Use the identities $z\,z^{*}=|z|^{2}$ and $z^{-1}=z^{*}/|z|^{2}$.

ConceptModulus as Distance
Modulus as distance

$|z_1 - z_2|$ is the distance
between two points on $\mathbb{C}$.

Circle
$$|z - z_0| = a$$

All points at fixed distance $a$ from $z_0$.

Perpendicular bisector
$$|z - z_1| = |z - z_2|$$

All points equidistant from $z_1$ and $z_2$.

ConceptMultiplication · Cartesian
Cartesian product

Distribute, then collapse $j^{2} = -1$.

$$z \,=\, x + jy, \qquad j^{2} = -1$$

Just like with real polynomials, FOIL through the product and collect real and imaginary parts.

Every $j^{2}$ becomes $-1$ — that's the only new rule.

Example
$$(5 - 2j)(3 - 4j) \;=\; 15 \;-\; 20j \;-\; 6j \;+\; 8j^{2} \;=\; 7 \;-\; 26j$$
ConceptENES-222 · L02
ConceptMultiplication · Polar
Polar product

Multiply moduli.
Add angles.

$$|z_1 z_2| \,=\, |z_1|\,|z_2|$$
$$\angle (z_1 z_2) \,=\, \angle z_1 + \angle z_2$$

The angle-sum identities for $\cos$ and $\sin$ collapse the product of two polar forms into a single one.

Multiplying by a complex number of modulus $1$ is therefore a pure rotation of the plane.

ConceptENES-222 · L02
Worked ExampleTwo ways to the same product
Worked example

Multiply $z_1 = 5 - 2j$ and $z_2 = 3 - 4j$.

Cartesian
$$z_1 z_2 \,=\, 7 - 26j$$
Polar inputs
$$|z_1| = \sqrt{29},\;\; \angle z_1 = -0.3805$$ $$|z_2| = 5,\;\;\;\;\; \angle z_2 = -0.9273$$
Polar product
$$|z_1 z_2| = 5\sqrt{29},\;\; \angle (z_1 z_2) = -1.3078$$
Back to Cartesian
$$5\sqrt{29}\,(\cos(-1.3078) + j\sin(-1.3078)) = 7 - 26j$$
Takeaway

Cartesian and polar agree — pick whichever is less work. For sums use Cartesian; for products and powers use polar.

Powers, the polar way
$$\big|z_1^{5}\,z_2^{2}\big| \,=\, 29^{5/2}\cdot 5^{2} \;\approx\; 1.13\times 10^{5}$$ $$\angle\!\big(z_1^{5}\,z_2^{2}\big) \,=\, 5(-0.3805) + 2(-0.9273) \,=\, -3.7571$$

ConceptConjugate & Modulus²
The conjugate

$z^{*}$ flips the imaginary part —
and the angle.

$$z\,z^{*} \,=\, |z|^{2}$$

If $z = x + jy$, then $z^{*} = x - jy$.

In polar form, $|z^{*}| = |z|$ and $\angle z^{*} = -\angle z$ — reflection across the real axis.

The product $z\,z^{*}$ is therefore real and non-negative.

ConceptENES-222 · L02
ConceptDivision
Division — Cartesian

Multiply top and bottom
by the denominator's conjugate.

$$\frac{z_1}{z_2} \,=\, \frac{z_1\,z_2^{*}}{|z_2|^{2}}$$

The denominator becomes real, so the rest is just real-division on the real and imaginary parts of the numerator.

Example
$$\frac{5 - 2j}{3 - 4j} \,=\, \frac{(5 - 2j)(3 + 4j)}{3^{2} + 4^{2}} \,=\, \frac{23}{25} \,+\, j\,\frac{14}{25}$$
ConceptENES-222 · L02
ConceptInverse $z^{-1}$
Inverse in polar form

Invert the modulus.
Negate the angle.

$$z^{-1} \,=\, \frac{z^{*}}{|z|^{2}}$$
$$|z^{-1}| = |z|^{-1},\;\; \angle z^{-1} = -\angle z$$

For division in polar form:

$$\left|\frac{z_1}{z_2}\right| = \frac{|z_1|}{|z_2|}, \qquad \angle\!\frac{z_1}{z_2} = \angle z_1 - \angle z_2.$$

Solved Example A · §1.3.3

Multiply $z_1=5-2j$
by $z_2=3-4j$.

Compute in Cartesian form, then verify via polar.

Step 1 · Cartesian
$(5-2j)(3-4j)=15-20j-6j+8j^2$ $$=15-26j-8=7-26j$$
Step 2 · Polar forms
$|z_1|=\sqrt{29},\;\angle z_1\approx -0.381\text{ rad}$ $|z_2|=5,\;\angle z_2\approx -0.927\text{ rad}$
Step 3 · Polar product
$$|z_1z_2|=5\sqrt{29}\approx26.93,\quad\angle z_1z_2\approx-1.308\text{ rad}$$ $z_1z_2=5\sqrt{29}(\cos(-1.308)+j\sin(-1.308))=7-26j\;\checkmark$
Key insight

Polar: multiply magnitudes, add angles. Cartesian: distribute and use $j^2=-1$.

Solved Example B · §1.3.5

Divide $z_1=\sqrt{3}-j$
by $z_2=1+j$.

Multiply numerator and denominator by $z_2^*$.

Step 1 · Multiply by $z_2^*=1-j$
$$\frac{\sqrt{3}-j}{1+j}=\frac{(\sqrt{3}-j)(1-j)}{(1-j)(1+j)}$$ $$=\frac{\sqrt{3}-j\sqrt{3}-j+j^2}{2}=\frac{(\sqrt{3}-1)}{2}-j\frac{(\sqrt{3}+1)}{2}$$
Step 2 · Polar check
$z_1=2e^{-j\pi/6},\;z_2=\sqrt{2}e^{j\pi/4}$ $$z_1/z_2=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}e^{j(-\pi/6-\pi/4)}=\sqrt{2}\,e^{-j5\pi/12}$$
Division rule

$|z_1/z_2|=|z_1|/|z_2|$ and $\angle(z_1/z_2)=\angle z_1-\angle z_2$.

Take with you

Four rules for $z_1$ and $z_2$.

01 · GEOMETRY
$|z-z_0|=a$ is a circle.
$|z-z_1|=|z-z_2|$ is the perpendicular bisector.
02 · MULTIPLY
Cartesian: FOIL, use $j^{2} = -1$.
Polar: multiply moduli, add angles.
03 · CONJUGATE
$z\,z^{*} = |z|^{2}$ — always real.
$z^{*}$ flips the imaginary part and the angle.
04 · DIVIDE / INVERT
$z^{-1} = z^{*}/|z|^{2}$
In polar: divide moduli, subtract angles.
