ConceptThe Z-Transform
Definition

A power series in $z^{-1}$.

$$X(z) \,\stackrel{\text{def}}{=}\, \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n]\, z^{-n}$$

For finite-duration $x$, always well-defined.

For infinite-duration $x$, convergence depends on $|z|$ — that's the region of convergence.

$X(e^{j\Omega})$ is the $z$-transform restricted to the unit circle — the DTFT.

ConceptENES-222 · L24
Worked ExampleReading a Z-Transform
Worked example

The coefficients are the signal.

$x=(2,-1,0,3)$ at $n=0,1,2,3$. Each sample $x[n]$ multiplies $z^{-n}$:

$$X(z)=2-z^{-1}+0\cdot z^{-2}+3z^{-3}$$
Evaluate at chosen z
$$\begin{aligned}z=1:&\ 2-1+0+3=4=\textstyle\sum x\\z=-1:&\ 2+1+0-3=0\\z=j:&\ 2-(-j)+3(j)=2+4j\end{aligned}$$

$z^{-1}=-j$ and $z^{-3}=(-j)^3=j$ at $z=j$.

Delay = multiply by z⁻¹

$x[n-2]$ has coefficients $2,-1,0,3$ moved two places later:

$$Z\{x[n-2]\}=2z^{-2}-z^{-3}+3z^{-5}=z^{-2}X(z)$$
Filters are z-transforms

For $y[n]=\sum_k b_k x[n-k]$ the impulse response is $h=b$, so $H(z)=\sum_k b_k z^{-k}$, and each delayed term contributes $b_k z^{-k}X(z)$. Hence $Y(z)=H(z)X(z)$.

Worked ExampleENES-222 · L24
ConceptConvolution → Multiplication
Convolution theorem

$y = h * x \;\Leftrightarrow\; Y(z) = H(z)\, X(z)$

$$Y(z) \,=\, H(z)\, X(z)$$

Cascade interpretation: pass $\delta$ through $H_1$ then $H_2$. Output is $h^{(1)} * h^{(2)}$, with $z$-transform $H_1(z)\, H_2(z)$.

For FIR filters: multiply two polynomials in $z^{-1}$ — that's all.

ConceptENES-222 · L24
Worked ExampleConvolution by Polynomial Multiplication
Worked example

$z$-transform shortcut.

$b=(2,-1,1,-2)^\top$, $s=(1,2,3,-1)^\top$ (L23 example): $B(z)=2-z^{-1}+z^{-2}-2z^{-3}$, $S(z)=1+2z^{-1}+3z^{-2}-z^{-3}$. Each pair $b_k z^{-k}\cdot s_{n-k}z^{-(n-k)}$ contributes to $z^{-n}$, so its coefficient is $c_n=\sum_k b_k s_{n-k}$:

$$\begin{aligned} z^{-0}\!:\; c_0 &= 2\cdot1 = 2\\ z^{-1}\!:\; c_1 &= 2\cdot2 + (-1)\cdot1 = 4 - 1 = 3\\ z^{-2}\!:\; c_2 &= 2\cdot3 + (-1)\cdot2 + 1\cdot1 = 6 - 2 + 1 = 5\\ z^{-3}\!:\; c_3 &= 2\cdot(-1) + (-1)\cdot3 + 1\cdot2 + (-2)\cdot1 = -2 - 3 + 2 - 2 = -5\\ z^{-4}\!:\; c_4 &= (-1)\cdot(-1) + 1\cdot3 + (-2)\cdot2 = 1 + 3 - 4 = 0\\ z^{-5}\!:\; c_5 &= 1\cdot(-1) + (-2)\cdot3 = -1 - 6 = -7\\ z^{-6}\!:\; c_6 &= (-2)\cdot(-1) = 2 \end{aligned}$$

So $b*s=(c_0,\dots,c_6)=(2,3,5,-5,0,-7,2)^\top$, matching L23. $\checkmark$

SolutionMultiply Two Filters
Solution

Distribute, then collect.

Distribute (u = z⁻¹)
$$\begin{aligned}1\cdot(1-u+3u^2)&=1-u+3u^2\\2u\cdot(1-u+3u^2)&=\ \ \ \ \,2u-2u^2+6u^3\end{aligned}$$
Collect powers of u

$u^0$: $1$. $\;u^1$: $-1+2=1$. $\;u^2$: $3-2=1$. $\;u^3$: $6$.

$$H(z)=1+z^{-1}+z^{-2}+6z^{-3}$$
Input/output equation
$$y[n]=x[n]+x[n-1]+x[n-2]+6x[n-3]$$
Checks

$z=1$: $H_1H_2=(1+2)(1-1+3)=9$ and $1+1+1+6=9$ ✓

$z=-1$: $(1-2)(1+1+3)=-5$ and $1-1+1-6=-5$ ✓

Convolution of $(1,2)$ and $(1,-1,3)$: $h=(1,\,-1+2,\,3-2,\,6)=(1,1,1,6)$ ✓

ConceptIdeal Filters
Ideal lowpass filter

Piecewise constant
— and unbuildable.

$H(e^{j\Omega}) = 1$ for $|\Omega| \leq \Omega_c$, $0$ otherwise.

A finite sum of cosines is smooth — no flat sections, no discontinuities. So no FIR filter (and in fact, no realisable filter) can achieve this exactly.

FIG. · Ideal lowpass — the brick wall.
ConceptENES-222 · L24
DerivationIdeal Lowpass: Its Impulse Response
Why no FIR filter is ideal

The brick wall needs an endless $h[n]$.

1 · Impulse response from the frequency response

$H(e^{j\Omega})=\sum_n h[n]e^{-j\Omega n}$ is $2\pi$-periodic in $\Omega$, so $h[n]$ is its Fourier-series coefficient: $\;h[n]=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}H(e^{j\Omega})\,e^{j\Omega n}\,d\Omega$.

2 · Insert the brick wall
$$h[n]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\Omega_c}^{\Omega_c}e^{j\Omega n}d\Omega=\frac{e^{j\Omega_c n}-e^{-j\Omega_c n}}{2\pi jn}=\frac{\sin(\Omega_c n)}{\pi n},\quad h[0]=\frac{\Omega_c}{\pi}$$
3 · Numbers and conclusion

$h[0]=0.3$, $\;h[1]=\tfrac{\sin 0.3\pi}{\pi}=0.2575$, $\;h[2]=\tfrac{\sin 0.6\pi}{2\pi}=0.1514$, $\;h[3]=\tfrac{\sin 0.9\pi}{3\pi}=0.0328$, and $h[-n]=h[n]$. It never becomes zero and extends to negative $n$: it is neither finite nor causal. A realisable FIR filter keeps $|n|\le M/2$ and delays the result by $M/2$.

DerivationENES-222 · L24
Worked ExampleTruncate and Delay
Worked example

Build an $M=6$ lowpass.

Cutoff $\Omega_c=\pi/2$, so $h[n]=\sin(\pi n/2)/(\pi n)$.

Step 1 · Samples for |n| ≤ 3
$$\begin{aligned}h[0]&=\tfrac12=0.5\\h[\pm1]&=\tfrac{\sin(\pi/2)}{\pi}=0.3183\\h[\pm2]&=\tfrac{\sin\pi}{2\pi}=0\\h[\pm3]&=\tfrac{\sin(3\pi/2)}{3\pi}=-0.1061\end{aligned}$$
Step 2 · Delay by M/2 = 3

$b_n=h[n-3]$ for $n=0,\dots,6$:

$$b=(-0.1061,\,0,\,0.3183,\,0.5,\,0.3183,\,0,\,-0.1061)$$

Symmetric: $b_n=b_{6-n}$.

Step 3 · Frequency response

Pair $b_n$ with $b_{6-n}$ and factor out $e^{-j3\Omega}$:

$$H(e^{j\Omega})=e^{-j3\Omega}F(\Omega)$$ $$F=0.5+0.6366\cos\Omega-0.2122\cos3\Omega$$
Step 4 · Evaluate F
$$\begin{aligned}F(0)&=0.5+0.6366-0.2122=0.9244\\F(\pi/4)&=0.5+0.4502+0.1500=1.1002\\F(\pi/2)&=0.5+0+0=0.5\\F(\pi)&=0.5-0.6366+0.2122=0.0756\end{aligned}$$

Not 1 and 0: ripple and leakage. The gain at the cutoff is $0.5$.

ConceptA Realistic Filter Spec
What real filters look like

Pass + transition + stop.

PASSBAND
$[0,\, \Omega_p]$
$|H| \in [A(1-\delta),\, A(1+\delta)]$. $\delta$ = ripple.
STOPBAND
$[\Omega_s,\, \pi]$
$|H| \leq A\,\epsilon$. $1/\epsilon$ = attenuation.
TRANSITION
$[\Omega_p,\, \Omega_s]$
$|H|$ drops from $A(1-\delta)$ to $A\,\epsilon$.
DELAY
$M/2$ samples
Linear phase across the passband.

PracticeReading a Filter Spec
Practice · ≈ 6 min

Turn a spec sheet into numbers.

Spec

Sampling rate $F_s=8000$ Hz. Lowpass FIR, order $M=50$, $A=1$. Passband edge $1200$ Hz, stopband edge $1600$ Hz, passband ripple $\delta=0.02$, stopband gain $|H|\le\epsilon=0.01$.

  • Convert both edges to $\Omega$ in rad/sample using $\Omega=2\pi f/F_s$ (i.e. $\omega/F_s$ with $\omega=2\pi f$ in rad/s). Give them as multiples of $\pi$.
  • State the passband limits $[A(1-\delta),A(1+\delta)]$ and the transition width in Hz.
  • Stopband attenuation in dB: $20\log_{10}(1/\epsilon)$.
  • Delay of the filter in samples and in milliseconds.
PracticeENES-222 · L24
SolutionReading a Filter Spec
Solution

Spec to numbers.

Edges in rad/sample
$$\begin{aligned}\Omega_p&=\frac{2\pi\cdot1200}{8000}=0.3\pi=0.942\\\Omega_s&=\frac{2\pi\cdot1600}{8000}=0.4\pi=1.257\end{aligned}$$
Passband and transition

$|H|\in[1-0.02,\,1+0.02]=[0.98,\,1.02]$ for $0\le\Omega\le0.3\pi$. Transition: $1600-1200=400$ Hz, i.e. $0.1\pi$ rad/sample.

Attenuation
$$20\log_{10}\frac{1}{0.01}=20\log_{10}100=20\cdot2=40\ \text{dB}$$
Delay

$M/2=25$ samples. Each sample lasts $1/8000=0.125$ ms, so the delay is $25\times0.125=3.125$ ms.

Reminder

Smaller $\delta$, smaller $\epsilon$ or a narrower transition all demand a larger $M$, hence a longer delay.

ConceptSymmetric Coefficients
Symmetric $b$

$b_n = b_{M-n}$
buys linear phase + cosine sum.

$$H(e^{j\Omega}) \,=\, e^{-j\Omega M/2}\,F(\Omega)$$

$M$ is the filter order: $b_0,\dots,b_M$ are the $M+1$ coefficients. $F(\Omega)$ is a real-valued sum of cosines: pairing $b_n e^{-j\Omega n}$ with $b_{M-n}e^{-j\Omega(M-n)}$ and factoring out $e^{-j\Omega M/2}$ leaves $2b_n\cos\!\big(\Omega(\tfrac M2-n)\big)$.

Phase response in the passband is exactly $-M\Omega/2$ (where $F>0$): uniform delay of $M/2$ samples.

Bigger $M$ ⇒ better approximation, longer delay. The trade-off.

ConceptLowpass ⇄ Highpass
Modulation trick

Multiply $b_n$ by $(-1)^{n}$
to flip lowpass into highpass.

$$\tilde{b}_n = (-1)^{n}\, b_n$$
$$\tilde{H}(e^{j\Omega}) \,=\, H(e^{j(\Omega - \pi)})$$

Multiplication by $(-1)^{n}$ is modulation by the highest discrete-time frequency $\pi$ — shifts the spectrum by $\pi$ radians per sample.

Why: $(-1)^n=e^{j\pi n}$, so $\tilde H(e^{j\Omega})=\sum_n b_n e^{j\pi n}e^{-j\Omega n}=\sum_n b_n e^{-j(\Omega-\pi)n}=H(e^{j(\Omega-\pi)})$.

A lowpass response centered at $0$ becomes a highpass response centered at $\pi$.

Practice

Filter

PracticeENES-222 · L24
Solution$|H|^{2} = 5 + 4\cos\Omega$
Product (real coefficients)

$H(e^{j\Omega})=1+2e^{-j\Omega}$. Replacing $\Omega$ by $-\Omega$ gives its complex conjugate $H(e^{-j\Omega})=1+2e^{j\Omega}$.

$$|H|^2=(1+2e^{-j\Omega})(1+2e^{j\Omega})$$
Expand term by term
$$\begin{aligned} &=1\cdot1+1\cdot2e^{j\Omega}+2e^{-j\Omega}\cdot1+2e^{-j\Omega}\cdot2e^{j\Omega}\\ &=1+2e^{j\Omega}+2e^{-j\Omega}+4\quad(e^{-j\Omega}e^{j\Omega}=1)\\ &=5+2\,(e^{j\Omega}+e^{-j\Omega})\\ &=5+4\cos\Omega\quad(\text{Euler: }e^{j\Omega}+e^{-j\Omega}=2\cos\Omega) \end{aligned}$$
Sanity checks

$\Omega = 0$: $|H|^{2} = 5 + 4 = 9 = 3^{2}$, and $H(1) = 1 + 2 = 3$. $\checkmark$

$\Omega = \pi$: $|H|^{2} = 5 - 4 = 1 = 1^{2}$, and $H(-1) = 1 - 2 = -1$. $\checkmark$

$\Omega=\pi/2$: $e^{-j\pi/2}=-j$, so $|1-2j|^2=1+4=5$, and $5+4\cos(\pi/2)=5$. $\checkmark$

$|H|^{2}$ is a finite cosine sum: always real, and never negative here since $5+4\cos\Omega\ge1$.

Solved ExampleTwo Fully-Solved Examples
Solved Example A

3-point moving average:
find $|H(e^{j\Omega})|$ and zeros.

$y[n]=\frac{1}{3}(x[n]+x[n-1]+x[n-2])$. Here $H(z)=\sum_n h[n]z^{-n}$ and $H(e^{j\Omega})$ is $H(z)$ at $z=e^{j\Omega}$.

Checks

$\Omega=0$: $H=\tfrac13(1+1+1)=1$, and the formula gives $|1+2|/3=1$. $\checkmark$

$\Omega=\pi$: $H=\tfrac13(1-1+1)=\tfrac13$, and $|1-2|/3=\tfrac13$. $\checkmark$

Step 1 · System function

The impulse response is $h=(\tfrac13,\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$, so

$$H(z)=\tfrac{1}{3}(1+z^{-1}+z^{-2})$$
Step 2 · Frequency response
$$\begin{aligned}H(e^{j\Omega})&=\tfrac13(1+e^{-j\Omega}+e^{-2j\Omega})\\ &=\tfrac{e^{-j\Omega}}{3}(e^{j\Omega}+1+e^{-j\Omega})\\ &=\tfrac{e^{-j\Omega}}{3}(1+2\cos\Omega)\end{aligned}$$

Factoring $e^{-j\Omega}$ out, and Euler's $e^{j\Omega}+e^{-j\Omega}=2\cos\Omega$. Since $|e^{-j\Omega}|=1$: $|H|=\tfrac13|1+2\cos\Omega|$.

Step 3 · Zeros

$1+2\cos\Omega=0\Rightarrow\cos\Omega=-\tfrac12\Rightarrow\Omega=\tfrac{2\pi}{3},\tfrac{4\pi}{3}$ (in $[0,2\pi)$; equivalently $\pm\tfrac{2\pi}{3}$).

Solved Example B

Lowpass to highpass.

$b=(1,2,1)$ is a lowpass filter (gain $4$ at $\Omega=0$). Form $\tilde b_n=(-1)^n b_n$ and find its frequency response.

Step 1 · Flip the signs
$$\tilde b=(1,\,-2,\,1)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\tilde H(z)=1-2z^{-1}+z^{-2}$$
Step 2 · Factor
$$\begin{aligned}\tilde H(e^{j\Omega})&=1-2e^{-j\Omega}+e^{-j2\Omega}\\&=e^{-j\Omega}\big(e^{j\Omega}-2+e^{-j\Omega}\big)\\&=e^{-j\Omega}(2\cos\Omega-2)\end{aligned}$$
Step 3 · Compare with H(Ω − π)

$H(e^{j\Omega})=e^{-j\Omega}(2+2\cos\Omega)$, so $H(e^{j(\Omega-\pi)})=e^{-j(\Omega-\pi)}\big(2+2\cos(\Omega-\pi)\big)=-e^{-j\Omega}(2-2\cos\Omega)$, which equals $\tilde H$ ✓

Checks

$\Omega=0$: $\tilde H=1-2+1=0$ (DC rejected). $\Omega=\pi$: $1+2+1=4$, the old DC gain, now at the highest frequency.

ReviewCommon Mistakes
Check before the exam

Six slips to avoid.

1 · Adding polynomials

A cascade multiplies: $z^{-a}z^{-b}=z^{-(a+b)}$. The product of a degree-3 and a degree-2 polynomial has degree 5, hence 6 coefficients.

2 · Mixing ω and Ω

$\Omega$ is rad/sample, $\omega$ is rad/s: $\Omega=\omega/F_s=2\pi f/F_s$. The DT filter only knows $\Omega$.

3 · Delay of M/2

$M$ is the order (number of taps minus 1). A 51-tap filter has $M=50$ and delay $25$ samples, not $25.5$.

4 · Squaring H instead of |H|²

$|H|^2=H(e^{j\Omega})\,H(e^{-j\Omega})$ for real $b$, not $H^2$. For $b=(1,2)$: $5+4\cos\Omega$.

5 · Highpass trick

$(-1)^n b_n$ shifts the response by $\pi$: DC gain becomes the gain at $\pi$ and vice versa. It does not invert the response.

6 · Expecting a brick wall

Finite $M$ always leaves a transition band, passband ripple and stopband leakage. State the spec, then pick $M$.
