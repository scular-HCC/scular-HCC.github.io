ConceptHow every codec works
Three-step recipe

Transform, threshold, code.

  1. Transform into a basis where energy compacts.
  2. Keep the $M$ largest-magnitude coefficients.
  3. Entropy-code what remains.
Real-world hook

JPEG (DCT) gets ~20× compression. JPEG 2000 (wavelet) gets ~50× at similar quality — because real images have edges where wavelets shine. Step 2 is this lab.

ConceptThe simplest wavelet
Average and difference

Build the Haar basis scale by scale.

  • Column 0: constant — captures the mean.
  • Column 1: one up–down step — captures asymmetry.
  • Columns 2–3: half-length steps — local trends.
  • Scale $k$ adds $2^{k-1}$ columns; total $N$.
Why Haar wins on edges

Smooth regions need few coefficients; only the kinks need many.

Each finer scale refines the staircase near the kink — exactly where energy lives.

SyntaxTransform → keep top M → reconstruct
The whole pipeline in five lines

COMPRESS1 in MATLAB and Python.

MATLAB
N = 512; V = nhaar(N); % provided helper c = V.' * s(:); % project % Keep M largest by magnitude [~, J] = sort(abs(c), 'descend'); ck = zeros(size(c)); ck(J(1:M)) = c(J(1:M)); s_rec = V * ck; % reconstruct rrmse = norm(s - s_rec) / norm(s);
PYTHON
N = 512 V = nhaar(N) # provided helper c = V.T @ s # Keep M largest by magnitude J = np.argsort(np.abs(c))[::-1][:M] ck = np.zeros_like(c) ck[J] = c[J] s_rec = V @ ck rrmse = np.linalg.norm(s - s_rec) / np.linalg.norm(s)

ConceptPicking the right basis
Match the basis to the signal class

Stationary tones ↔ DFT. Edges ↔ Haar.

DFT wins for

Pure sinusoids, sustained audio tones, periodic textures.

Haar wins for

Piecewise-smooth signals with edges or transitions: images, ECG, step responses, geological logs.

At the same M, the wrong basis can mean an order of magnitude more error. Modern codecs (HEVC, AV1) choose adaptively per block.

Worked ExampleEnergy-compaction curve
How few coefficients do we really need?

Sort. Sum. Plot.

MATLAB
c = V.' * s(:); [c_sq, ~] = sort(c.^2, 'descend'); energy = cumsum(c_sq) / sum(c_sq); semilogx(1:N, energy); yline(0.99); yline(0.999); xlabel('top K coefficients'); ylabel('fraction of energy');
PYTHON
c = V.T @ s c_sq = np.sort(c**2)[::-1] energy = np.cumsum(c_sq) / c_sq.sum() plt.semilogx(np.arange(1, N+1), energy) plt.axhline(0.99); plt.axhline(0.999) plt.xlabel('top K coefficients') plt.ylabel('fraction of energy')

For a typical piecewise signal: 99% of energy lives in < 5% of coefficients. That's the compression ratio.

PitfallsApproximation traps
Common mistakes

Five errors to avoid.

  1. endpoint=False vs endpoint=True. $N$ samples over $[0, 4)$ wants endpoint=False; otherwise you get $N+1$.
  2. Counting basis vectors per scale. Scale 0 = 1 vector; scale $k$ adds $2^{k-1}$.
  3. Output of COMPRESS1 has length $N$. Only $M$ entries are nonzero; the rest are zero.
  4. Comparing bases at $M = N$. Both reconstruct exactly. Interesting regime is low $M$.
  5. Forgetting Parseval. norm(c) == norm(s) for an orthonormal $V$. Useful sanity check.
