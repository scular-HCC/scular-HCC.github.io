ConceptThe central radio trick
Modulation theorem

Multiply by a sinusoid ⇒ shift the spectrum.

$$\mathcal F\{m(t)\cos(2\pi f_c t)\} = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[M(f-f_c) + M(f+f_c)\bigr]$$
In plain words

Take the baseband, make two copies, slide one to $+f_c$ and the other to $-f_c$. That is AM modulation.

Multiplication by $\cos(2\pi f_c t)$ slides the spectrum to $\pm f_c$.
ConceptENES-222 · Lab 10
ConceptFrequency-division multiplexing
Sinusoids are orthogonal

Different carriers don't interfere.

Two stations on carriers $f_{c1}$ and $f_{c2}$ live in disjoint frequency bands. A lowpass filter after the mixer at $f_{c1}$ rejects everything from station 2. This is FDM — the principle behind broadcast bands.

Receiver chain
  1. Multiply received signal by cos(2π·fc·t) — mixer.
  2. Lowpass filter — keep baseband copy, reject the 2·fc image and other stations.
  3. Audio out.

SyntaxModulate a baseband message
Build the AM signal

Voice tone at $f_c = 1$ kHz.

MATLAB
fs = 8000; T = 1.0; N = fs*T; t = (0:N-1)/fs; m = cos(2*pi*200*t); % baseband fc = 1000; s_am = (1 + 0.7*m) .* cos(2*pi*fc*t); S = fftshift(abs(fft(s_am))); f = ((0:N-1) - N/2) * fs/N; plot(f, S);
PYTHON
from numpy.fft import fft, fftshift, fftfreq fs, T = 8000, 1.0 N = int(fs*T) t = np.arange(N)/fs m = np.cos(2*np.pi*200*t) # baseband fc = 1000 s_am = (1 + 0.7*m) * np.cos(2*np.pi*fc*t) S = fftshift(np.abs(fft(s_am))) f = fftshift(fftfreq(N, 1/fs)) plt.plot(f, S)

SyntaxReceiver: mix + lowpass
Bring the carrier back to baseband

Multiply, then filter.

MATLAB
% Mix down mixed = received .* cos(2*pi*fc*t); % Lowpass FIR (Hamming-windowed sinc) b = fir1(100, 400/(fs/2)); baseband = filter(b, 1, mixed); soundsc(baseband, fs);

from scipy.signal import firwin, lfilter # Mix down mixed = received * np.cos(2*np.pi*fc*t) # Lowpass FIR (cutoff 400 Hz) b = firwin(101, 400, fs=fs) baseband = lfilter(b, 1, mixed) from IPython.display import Audio Audio(baseband, rate=fs)

Worked ExampleFind carriers automatically
Software-defined radio in 10 lines

DFT peak picking → carrier list.

MATLAB
S = abs(fft(received)); half = S(1:N/2); f_one = (0:N/2-1) * fs/N; [~, peaks] = findpeaks(half, ... 'MinPeakHeight', 0.05*max(half), ... 'MinPeakDistance', 200); carriers = f_one(peaks); disp(carriers);
PYTHON
from scipy.signal import find_peaks S = np.abs(fft(received)) half = S[:N//2] f_one = np.arange(N//2) * fs/N peaks, _ = find_peaks(half, height=0.05*half.max(), distance=200) carriers = f_one[peaks] print(carriers)

PitfallsModulation traps
Common mistakes

Five errors to avoid.

  1. Carrier above Nyquist. If $f_c > f_s/2$ it aliases. Pick $f_s$ at least $2(f_c + B_m)$.
  2. Forgetting the lowpass. After the mixer you get baseband and a copy at $2 f_c$. The lowpass removes the image.
  3. Phase mismatch. Local oscillator phase $\neq$ transmitter phase → amplitude scales by $\cos\Delta\phi$. Real receivers use a PLL.
  4. Missing fftshift. Negative frequencies appear at high indices without it.
  5. Confusing DSB-SC and DSB-LC. Plain $m(t)\cos$ is suppressed-carrier; $(1+m)\cos$ has the carrier and decodes with a diode.
